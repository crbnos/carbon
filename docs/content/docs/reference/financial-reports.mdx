---
title: Financial statement reports
description: "The Balance Sheet, Income Statement, and Trial Balance built from posted journals by account class."
---

Three reports read the posted [general ledger](/docs/reference/accounting). The **Balance Sheet** shows cumulative assets, liabilities, and equity at each selected period end. The **Income Statement** shows revenue and expense activity by period. The **Trial Balance** shows beginning and ending balances plus net debit or credit movement for one date range. All three use the same balance engine.

The reports are under Accounting and require `accounting` view permission. They are available after the company [sets up accounting](/docs/reference/accounting#setting-up-accounting). Before that, every posting writes a *Provisional* journal, and the reports count only Posted and Reversed journals.

## The three statements

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Balance Sheet">Shows one column per selected month, quarter, or fiscal year. Each value is cumulative through that period's end. Carbon appends a computed **"Net Income"** equity line so the sheet ties.</Field>
  <Field name="Income Statement">Shows Revenue and Expense activity within each selected period.</Field>
  <Field name="Trial Balance">Shows one selected date range. **Beginning** is the cumulative balance before the range, **Ending** is cumulative through the end date, and **Debits** or **Credits** show net movement on the normal side when positive and the opposite side when negative.</Field>
</FieldTable>

<Screenshot label="Balance Sheet report with monthly columns for the selected date range" caption="Each column shows cumulative account balances through that period's end." ratio="wide" />

The Balance Sheet and Income Statement divide the selected range into monthly, quarterly, or yearly columns. The Balance Sheet reads each column's cumulative ending balance; the Income Statement reads activity within each column. The Trial Balance keeps the selected range as one period and derives its beginning balance, net debit or credit movement, and ending balance.

Each account's `incomeBalance` value selects **Balance Sheet** or **Income Statement**. Asset, Liability, and Equity accounts belong to the Balance Sheet; Revenue and Expense accounts belong to the Income Statement.

<Callout type="note">
Balances always exclude **Draft** journals. Only posted (and reversed) entries move a report, so an unposted entry sitting in the Journal Entries list has no effect on any statement until it posts. This is why the account ledger, the reports, and the [period-close](/docs/reference/period-close) snapshots all agree.
</Callout>

## How a balance is built

Every report starts from a single balance function that reads the posted ledger and returns three numbers per account:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="balance">All-time cumulative balance, no date bound. The running total of every posted line on the account.</Field>
  <Field name="balanceAtDate">Cumulative balance from inception up to the report's **end date**. This is the *as-of* figure the Balance Sheet shows.</Field>
  <Field name="netChange">Activity *within* the start-to-end window only. The Income Statement shows this movement by period, and the Trial Balance places its net value in the debit or credit column.</Field>
</FieldTable>

Group (heading) accounts don't hold postings; their totals roll up from their children. Because a debit and a credit are stored with opposite signs, the roll-up is **sign-aware by account class**. Asset and Revenue accounts add to their parent; Liability, Equity, and Expense subtract.

That rule makes the accounting identities fall out cleanly. A Balance Sheet root nets Assets minus Liabilities minus Equity to roughly zero. The Income Statement root is Revenue minus Expense, which is net income.

<Callout type="info" title="Net Income is computed, never posted">
Undistributed profit lives in the Revenue and Expense accounts until a period close moves it. A Balance Sheet would otherwise be out of balance because that profit belongs in equity.

Carbon closes the gap by summing every Income Statement leaf account and showing it as a single **"Net Income"** line inside the Equity group. It is a calculated row, not an account you can post to. Don't create a "Net Income" account in the chart of accounts.
</Callout>

## Period and date selection

All three reports use a start and end date and default to the trailing six months:

- The **Balance Sheet** and **Income Statement** can group the range into monthly, quarterly, or yearly columns. A Balance Sheet value remains an as-of figure because each column is cumulative through that column's end date.
- The **Trial Balance** keeps the range as one period and shows its beginning balance, net debit or credit activity, and ending balance. Fiscal presets follow the company's fiscal start month from [fiscal year settings](/docs/reference/period-close).

These pickers slice by **posting date**, not by the [accounting period](/docs/reference/period-close) a journal landed in, so a report window can span open and closed periods freely. Closing a period doesn't change what a report shows; it just snapshots balances so the reads stay fast.

Alongside the date picker, a company selector lets you view a single company or roll several together (multi-company consolidation, with currency translation when a **"Show translated"** toggle is on), and an account search filters the tree by name or number.

## What the reports do not slice

The core statements do not support dimension filtering.

<Callout type="warn" title="Core statements are not sliced by reporting dimension">
Journal lines can carry <Term id="reporting-dimension">reporting dimensions</Term> (location, department, cost center, and custom axes), but the Balance Sheet, Income Statement, and Trial Balance **do not** offer a dimension filter. They aggregate every posting on an account regardless of its dimension tags. To analyze the ledger by dimension, use the [dimensions](/docs/reference/dimensions) tooling, not these three statements.
</Callout>

The account ledger drawer (the drill-down you get by clicking an account on any of these reports) lists that account's individual posted lines for the same window, so a report figure always traces back to the transactions that make it up.

## Troubleshooting

The financial reports (Balance Sheet, Income Statement, Trial Balance) rarely throw string errors; the common problems are wrong-looking numbers with a precondition cause.

### "Why are all my numbers zero"
The company has not set up accounting, so every journal is Provisional and the reports count none of them. Set up accounting (Settings → Accounting → **Set up accounting**): journals dated on or after the cutover date become Posted, and the opening journal carries the balances at the cutover. A company with accounting set up but no posted documents also reads zero until the first shipment, invoice, receipt, or journal posts.

### "Why do my numbers look too low / an entry is missing"
Balances count only **Posted** and **Reversed** journals. An entry that is still Draft in the Journal Entries list does not move any report until it is posted. Post it (or check its status) and the figure updates. Reversed journals *do* count, as their offsetting entry. *Provisional* journals (before the cutover) and *Before cutover* journals (replaced by the opening journal) never count.

### "Why is my Balance Sheet out of balance / Assets ≠ Liabilities + Equity"
The Balance Sheet appends a computed **"Net Income"** equity line that carries undistributed Revenue-minus-Expense into equity. If a figure still looks off, it is almost always a date-selection issue (a range that ends before some postings) or an account created with the wrong class or `incomeBalance` marker (for example a Revenue account flagged Balance Sheet), which misroutes it in the sign-aware roll-up. Check the account's class and Balance Sheet vs Income Statement setting in the chart of accounts.

### "Why doesn't my dimension filter change the totals"
There isn't one. The core statements aggregate every posting on an account regardless of dimension tags. Dimension analysis is a separate tool, not a filter on these three reports.

### "Why does the Balance Sheet show multiple columns"
Each column is an as-of snapshot that is cumulative through that period's end. The report uses the selected start and end dates to choose which monthly, quarterly, or yearly period ends to display.

### Preconditions to check first
- Accounting must be set up (the company has a cutover date), or every balance is zero.
- The report window is by **posting date**, independent of accounting-period close status; closing a period does not hide its postings from reports.
- The signed-in user needs the `accounting` view permission to open any of the three reports.
</AgentContext>
