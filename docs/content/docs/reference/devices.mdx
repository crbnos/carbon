title: Your devices
description: Every live session on your account across ERP and MES, what Carbon records about each sign-in, and how to sign out a device you don't recognize.
---

Sooner or later every shared system raises the same question — where am I signed in right now? The
**"Your devices"** card on **Account → Security** answers it. The card's own description says what it's
for: *"Where you're signed in. Sign out of any device you don't recognize."* Each row is one live
<Term>session</Term> on your account, across both the ERP and the shop-floor MES, and any of them except
the one you're using can be ended from here (`apps/erp/app/routes/x+/account+/security.tsx`).

It sits alongside your passkeys and [two-factor authentication](/docs/reference/two-factor) because it
completes the same story: those control how someone gets in, this shows who currently *is* in.

## Reading the list

Your own session is always first, marked with a green **"This device"** badge. Every other row gets a
sign-out button instead. What a row shows:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Device">The browser and operating system, read from the sign-in's user agent — "Chrome on macOS", "Safari on iOS". Phones and tablets get a phone icon. A session Carbon can't classify shows "Unknown device".</Field>
  <Field name="Location and IP">The approximate city and country of the sign-in, with the IP address beside it. Sign-ins from a private or loopback address read "Local network"; when no location is available the row says "Unknown location".</Field>
  <Field name="Last active">When that device last renewed its sign-in. Renewal happens automatically while the app is in use, so this trails real activity by up to an hour — it tells you the device is alive, not what it last clicked.</Field>
  <Field name="App">An ERP or MES badge. MES has no account screen of its own, so shop-floor sessions are listed and managed here.</Field>
</FieldTable>

<Callout type="info" title="Location is an estimate, not a street address">
The city comes from IP geolocation at the network edge, which resolves to the area of the network's
population center — good enough to notice a sign-in from the wrong country, not good enough to identify a
building. Self-hosted installs have no geolocation at the edge, so their rows read "Unknown location";
the IP is still shown.
</Callout>

<Callout type="success" title="Two browsers on one computer look identical">
Two browser profiles on the same machine share a user agent and an IP, so their rows read the same.
Tell them apart by **"Last active"** and by which one carries the **"This device"** badge — the badge always
belongs to the browser you're viewing the page from, so it moves if you check from the other profile.
</Callout>

## Signing a device out

Press the sign-out icon on any row that isn't yours. Carbon asks you to confirm, naming the device and
location, and states the contract exactly: the session *"can no longer stay signed in and is returned to
the login page within a minute of its next page load."* Ending a session revokes its credentials at the
server, so there is nothing the other device can do to keep itself alive — it must sign in again from
scratch.

<Callout type="warn" title="Within a minute, not the same instant">
Signing a device out doesn't blank its screen mid-click. The revoked session fails its next security
check, which runs on page navigation and is cached for sixty seconds — so the device is back at the login
page within a minute of its next page load. Its ability to renew credentials dies immediately.
</Callout>

Two related controls round it out:

- **"Sign out other devices"**, at the top of the card, ends every session except the one you're using —
  the right move when you've signed in somewhere you shouldn't have, or just want a clean slate. The
  button is disabled when this device holds the only session.
- **Your own row has no sign-out button.** Ending the session you're sitting in is what **Log out** is
  for, from the avatar menu. Logging out ends the session at the server too, so a device you signed out
  of properly disappears from this list rather than lingering as a ghost.

Sessions don't need manual housekeeping to stay safe: a device that sits idle for seven days loses its
sign-in on its own. Its row can outlive that access for a while, though — if a stale entry bothers you,
**"Sign out other devices"** sweeps everything but the session you're on.

<Callout type="note" title="A sign-in stuck at the two-factor prompt still holds a session">
When two-factor is on, a sign-in that passes its first step but never enters the code has still opened a
session, and it appears here until it expires. If you see one you don't recognize, sign it out — that is
precisely the situation the card exists for.
</Callout>

## The record behind the card

The card is powered by a sign-in history Carbon keeps for your account. Every completed sign-in is
recorded at the moment it succeeds — magic link, Google, Microsoft, passkey, or the email verification
code at signup — along with the app, the IP, the approximate location, and the device's user agent
(`packages/auth/src/services/login-history.server.ts`). With two-factor on, the record is written when
the first step succeeds, which is why the stuck-at-the-prompt session above is visible at all.

Three properties of that record matter:

- **Only you can read it.** The history is scoped to your user; no other user, in any company, can query
  your sign-ins.
- **No one can rewrite it — including you.** Rows are written by the system at sign-in and can't be
  inserted, edited, or deleted from a signed-in session, so the record can't be forged or scrubbed by
  someone who got into the account.
- **It keeps ninety days.** Older entries are pruned automatically, which also bounds how long the IP
  addresses (personal data) are retained.

Your devices is the visibility half of account security; the prevention half is adding a passkey and an
authenticator app on the same screen. For what else lives there, see [your account](/docs/reference/account),
and for the company-wide enforcement an admin controls, see
[two-factor authentication](/docs/reference/two-factor).

## Troubleshooting

### A row says "Unknown device" with no location
That session's sign-in predates the device details Carbon now records, or its user agent wasn't
classifiable. The session itself is real. If you don't recognize it, sign it out — "Sign out other
devices" clears every session but the current one in a single step.

### The rows read "Local network" or "Unknown location"
"Local network" means the sign-in came from a private or loopback address — normal for local development
and on-premise networks. "Unknown location" means no geolocation was available at the network edge, which
is the expected state for self-hosted installs; location data is populated on Carbon Cloud.

### The device I signed out is still on screen
Revocation takes effect on the device's next page load, within a minute — the sixty-second window is the
security-check cache. Its credentials are already dead; it cannot renew, and any navigation lands on the
login page.

### "Sign out other devices" is disabled
The button is enabled only when at least one session other than the current one is live. Disabled means
this device holds the only session.

### Two identical rows
Two browsers or browser profiles on one machine share a user agent and IP. Distinguish them by "Last
active" and by the "This device" badge, which always marks the browser currently viewing the page.
</AgentContext>
