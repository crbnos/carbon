The **"Your devices"** card on **Account → Security** lists every device currently signed in to your
account, across both the ERP and the shop-floor MES. Sign out any you don't recognize.

## Reading the list

Your own device is first, badged **"This device"**. Each row shows:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Device">Browser and operating system, read from the sign-in. Phones and tablets get a phone icon. Unclassifiable sign-ins read "Unknown device".</Field>
  <Field name="Location and IP">Approximate city and country, with the IP beside it. Private addresses read "Local network"; with no location available, "Unknown location".</Field>
  <Field name="Last active">When that device last renewed its sign-in. Renewal is automatic while the app is open, so this can trail real activity by up to an hour.</Field>
  <Field name="App">An ERP or MES badge. MES has no account screen, so shop-floor sessions are managed here.</Field>
</FieldTable>

<Callout type="info" title="Two browsers on one computer look alike">
Two browsers on the same machine share an operating system and an IP, so their rows read almost
identically. Tell them apart by **"Last active"** and by the **"This device"** badge, which always marks
the browser you're reading from.
</Callout>

## Signing out a device

Press the sign-out icon on a row. Carbon confirms, naming the device, and states what happens: the
session *"can no longer stay signed in and is returned to the login page within a minute of its next
page load."*

**"Sign out other devices"** does the same to every device at once, except the one you're on.

Three things to know:

- **It isn't instant.** The signed-out device lands on the login page within a minute of its next page
  load. Its ability to stay signed in dies immediately, so it cannot renew itself in the meantime.
- **Your own row has no sign-out button.** Use **Log out** from the avatar menu for that.
- **Idle devices drop off by themselves** after seven days without use.

## Why some devices say "Newer device"

You can sign out a device only if the browser you're using has been on the account **longer** than that
device's session. When it hasn't, the row shows **"Newer device"** instead of a sign-out button.

This keeps a stolen session from locking you out. An attacker signs in on a browser Carbon has never
seen, so they can't touch anything older than their break-in. Your everyday laptop, known to the account
for months, can still sign them out.

<Callout type="info" title="It measures the browser, not the sign-in">
Signing out and back in this morning doesn't weaken your laptop. What counts is how long Carbon has
recognized the **browser**, which is the day you first signed in on it.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warn" title="Clearing cookies resets a browser">
Carbon recognizes a browser by a cookie set the first time you sign in there. Clearing cookies, private
browsing, or switching browsers makes it a device Carbon is meeting for the first time, so older
sessions may show **"Newer device"**. Use a browser you've had longer, or wait for the new one to age
past the sessions you want to end.
</Callout>

**"Sign out other devices"** is all or nothing: it's disabled while any row reads **"Newer device"**, and
also when yours is the only device.

## The new-device email

The first time your account is signed into from a browser Carbon hasn't seen, you get an email:
**"A new device signed in to your Carbon account"**. It names the device and the time, and links back to
this card. If it was you, do nothing.

- **One email per device, not per sign-in.** A laptop you use daily is announced once.
- **Both apps trigger it**, including MES.
- **It ignores notification settings.** This is a security receipt, not a notification, so nothing can
  switch it off. It's the one message that reaches you when someone else is in your account.
- **It carries no IP or location.** Those are approximate at best and unavailable on self-hosted
  installs, so a security alert shouldn't lean on them. Open this card for those details.

With two-factor on, the email waits until the code is entered. A sign-in abandoned at the prompt never
completed, so announcing it would only teach you to ignore the alert.

## What Carbon records

Every completed sign-in is recorded: the app, the IP, the approximate location, and the device. That
history is what the card reads from.

- **Only you can read it.** No other user, in any company, can see your sign-ins.
- **Nobody can rewrite it, including you.** Rows are written by the system and can't be edited or
  deleted from a signed-in session, so the record can't be forged or scrubbed by someone who got in.
- **It keeps ninety days**, which also bounds how long your IP addresses are stored. One entry per
  device outlives that: the first sign-in from each browser, which is the date the **"Newer device"**
  rule measures against.

## Troubleshooting

### A row shows "Newer device" instead of a sign-out button
The current browser's first recorded sign-in is later than that session's start, so the device-age gate
refuses. Posting the revoke directly returns 403 with "Sign out from a device you've used for longer".
The gate is `deviceFirstSeenAt < session.startedAt`, evaluated in both the loader and the action through
the same `getDevices` helper (`apps/erp/app/routes/x+/account+/security.tsx`). Causes: cookies cleared,
private browsing, a different browser, or a genuinely new machine.

### "Sign out other devices" is disabled
Either no other session is live, or at least one row reads "Newer device". The bulk revoke is
all-or-nothing and refuses when any single session is out of reach.

### The device cookie
Recognition is a signed, httpOnly cookie named `carbon-device`, set at every login mint point, valid 365
days so it outlives the 7-day session cookie (`packages/auth/src/services/device.server.ts`). Its id is
stored on the sign-in row as `deviceId`. Per-browser, not per-machine: two browsers on one laptop are two
devices.

### No new-device email arrived
It sends only on the first completed sign-in per device, so a browser already in the history stays quiet.
With two-factor enabled it's deferred until the code is entered, and a sign-in abandoned at the prompt
never sends. Accounts with no company membership are skipped, because the send is company-scoped. Subject:
"A new device signed in to your Carbon account".

### A row says "Unknown device" with no location
The sign-in predates the device details Carbon now records, or its user agent wasn't classifiable. The
session is real. Sign it out if you don't recognize it.

### Rows read "Local network" or "Unknown location"
"Local network" means a private or loopback address, normal for local development and on-premise
networks. "Unknown location" means no geolocation was available at the network edge, expected for
self-hosted installs. Location data is populated on Carbon Cloud.

### The device I signed out is still on screen
Revocation takes effect on its next page load, within a minute. The sixty-second window is the
security-check cache. Its credentials are already dead; it cannot renew, and any navigation lands on the
login page.

### A sign-in stuck at the two-factor prompt
It still holds a session and appears in the list until it expires. It does not earn the browser any
standing on the account, so failing at the prompt cannot make a device count as recognized.
</AgentContext>
