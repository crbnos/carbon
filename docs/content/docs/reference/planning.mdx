---
title: Planning (MRP)
description: How demand turns into suggested jobs and purchase orders — and where that ends and scheduling begins.
---

Planning determines what to make or buy and in what quantity. Carbon's <Term>MRP</Term> nets demand against supply across the complete <Term id="bom">bill of materials</Term>. It converts shortages into suggested orders and offsets component demand by <Term>lead time</Term>.

## Two stages

Planning has two distinct stages:

1. **MRP, explode and net.** The engine explodes demand through bills of material, nets it against on-hand plus open jobs and purchase orders, and writes period-bucketed demand forecasts (plus the demand and supply *actuals* it read). It does **not** apply <Term id="reorder-point">reorder points</Term> and does **not** create orders.
2. **Planning views, suggest a quantity.** The production and purchasing planning pages read those forecasts, project running stock week by week, and apply each item's **<Term id="reordering-policy">reorder policy</Term>** to propose a quantity to order.

<Callout type="note">
MRP **suggests, it never auto-orders.** The engine only writes forecasts; turning a suggestion into a real job or PO is an explicit action you take on the planning page.
</Callout>

## What feeds it

| Input | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Customer demand | Open sales-order lines, by promised date. |
| Production demand | Job material still to issue, offset by lead time. |
| Forecast demand | Manually entered demand projections. |
| Open supply | Open jobs and open purchase-order lines. |
| On hand | The item ledger balance. |

## Reorder policy

Each item, per location, carries a **reordering policy** that decides how a suggestion is sized: *Manual Reorder* (suggests nothing), *Demand-Based Reorder* (the default), *Fixed Reorder Quantity*, or *Maximum Quantity*. Each is tuned by reorder point, order quantity, accumulation period, and min/max/multiple rounding. See [Reordering](/docs/reference/reordering) for the parameters in full.

## From suggestion to order

An item's **<Term>replenishment system</Term>** determines where its shortage appears. *Make* items produce suggested <Term id="job">jobs</Term> on the production planning page; *Buy* items produce suggested POs on the purchasing page. Choosing **Order** creates a saved job or purchase order with status **"Planned"**, pending release into its normal lifecycle.

<Callout type="info">
**"Planned"** is a real, saved job or PO that came from a planning suggestion and is waiting to be released — distinct from a hand-started **"Draft"**.
</Callout>

## Planning vs scheduling

Planning determines quantities; **scheduling** determines timing and work-center allocation. After a job exists, scheduling places its <Term id="operation">operations</Term> on <Term id="work-center">work centers</Term> and calculates dates without changing quantities.

## Troubleshooting

### "Manufacturing is blocked for item …"
Ordering a suggested job was stopped because that item's planning settings have manufacturing blocked. Clear the flag on the item, then order again from planning.

### "Manufacturing requires configuration for item …"
The item is configurable, and planning can't supply a configuration. Create the job from a context that sets the configuration (for example the sales order line) instead of from the planning page.

### "Failed to create job method for item …"
The job was created but copying the item's method into it failed — usually the item has no **Active** make method. Activate a method on the item, then delete and re-order the job (or run Get Method on it).

### "Failed to load any locations"
Planning needs at least one location. Create one in settings and set a default.
</AgentContext>
