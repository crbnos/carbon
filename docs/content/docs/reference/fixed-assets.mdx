---
title: Fixed assets
description: "Capital equipment on the books: acquired, depreciated, and retired."
---

A **fixed asset** represents capital equipment that is capitalized and depreciated instead of expensed, such as a press, CNC, or forklift. It is separate from the <Term>work center</Term> used to schedule the same equipment. Every asset belongs to an **<Term>asset class</Term>** that defines its general-ledger accounts.

## Lifecycle

<StatusFlow entity="fixedAsset">
  <Status name="Draft">Created, not yet on the books.</Status>
  <Status name="Active" accent>Capitalized and depreciating.</Status>
  <Status name="Fully Depreciated">Written down to its residual value; no longer accruing.</Status>
  <Status name="Disposed" terminal>Retired or sold off the books.</Status>
</StatusFlow>

## Acquiring

Assets start in **Draft** and enter the books in either of two ways:

- **Register** one you already own: supply its acquisition cost and depreciation start date, and it moves to **Active**. No money posts; you're recording something you have.
- **Buy** one through purchasing: a <Term id="purchase-order">purchase-order</Term> line of type *Fixed Asset* that, when the receipt posts, debits the asset account, **adds** the cost to the asset, and flips it to **Active**.

<Callout type="note">
The purchase-receipt path activates the asset and posts its acquisition entry **only when accounting is enabled** for the company. Cost is added cumulatively, so an asset can accrue value across several receipts; with accounting off, the line is received but the asset is left in Draft.
</Callout>

## Depreciating

<Term id="depreciation">Depreciation</Term> runs as a manually triggered monthly batch; it is not posted in the background. Create a run for a period, review Carbon's calculated charges, then post them. Posting debits depreciation expense and credits accumulated depreciation. When enabled, Carbon maintains a separate **tax** book, including <Term>MACRS</Term>. When <Term id="net-book-value">net book value</Term> reaches residual value, the asset becomes **Fully Depreciated**.

Book depreciation follows the asset's method: <Term id="straight-line">straight line</Term>, <Term id="declining-balance">declining balance</Term>, or units of production.

## Selling vs. disposing

An Active or Fully Depreciated asset leaves the books two different ways:

| | Sell | Dispose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | A sales action | An accounting action |
| What happens | Drafts a **sales order** for the asset at its net book value | Posts a **write-off**: clears accumulated depreciation, removes the asset at cost |
| Asset status | Unchanged (stays Active) | **Disposed** |
| Money | Collected through the invoice, like any sale | Remaining book value booked as a loss |

<Callout type="warn">
Selling and disposing are **not** two names for one thing. Selling hands the asset to the normal quote-to-cash flow at book value and leaves it on the books; disposing retires it directly as a write-off. <Term id="disposal">Disposal</Term> today is scrapping at a loss. There's no proceeds-based gain calculation wired up.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
A fixed asset is **not** the same record as a work center. The machine you schedule production on and the machine you depreciate are tracked independently. Carbon keeps no link between them.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

### "Fixed Asset invoice line … has no asset selected"
A purchase invoice line of type *Fixed Asset* has no asset record linked. Open the line and select (or create) the fixed asset before posting the invoice.

### "Asset is no longer in Draft status"
The register action found the asset already activated — usually someone else registered it between page load and save. Reload the asset; if it's Active, the registration already happened.

### Depreciation or receipt posting fails with a period error
Asset postings land in an accounting period like everything else. "Accounting period is closed/locked" means the run's period needs reopening — see [Accounting](/docs/reference/accounting).
</AgentContext>
