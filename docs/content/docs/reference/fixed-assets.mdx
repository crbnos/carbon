title: Fixed assets
description: "Capital equipment on the books: bought, built, or capitalized from stock, then depreciated and retired."
---

A **fixed asset** is a piece of capital equipment you capitalize and depreciate rather than expense: a press, a CNC, a forklift, or a unit you built to rent out. It's an accounting record of value over time, distinct from the <Term>work center</Term> that represents the same machine on the floor. Every asset belongs to an **<Term>asset class</Term>**, which supplies the general-ledger accounts it posts to.

## Lifecycle

<StatusFlow entity="fixedAsset">
  <Status name="Draft">Created, not yet on the books.</Status>
  <Status name="Active" accent>Capitalized and depreciating.</Status>
  <Status name="Fully Depreciated">Written down to its residual value; no longer accruing.</Status>
  <Status name="Disposed" terminal>Retired, sold, or returned to stock. Off the books.</Status>
</StatusFlow>

## Acquiring

An asset comes onto the books four ways. Registering and buying start from a **Draft** asset you created; building and capitalizing create the asset for you.

- **Register** one you already own: supply its acquisition cost and depreciation start date, and it moves to **Active**. No cash moves; the entry offsets equity, since you're recording something you already have.
- **Buy** one through purchasing: a <Term id="purchase-order">purchase-order</Term> line of type *Fixed Asset* that, when the receipt posts, debits the asset account, **adds** the cost to the asset, and flips it to **Active**. A purchase invoice with no receipt does the same. The asset page's **"Purchase"** action drafts the purchase order for you, and it is offered while the asset is Draft.
- **Build** one on a job: set the job's **"Complete To"** to **"Fixed Asset Class"** and choose the class. When the job completes, each finished unit becomes its own asset priced at the <Term>WIP</Term> cost it absorbed, and the unit never enters stock. This is <Term id="make-to-asset">Make to Asset</Term>.
- **Capitalize** a unit already in stock: on the item's inventory page, the storage-unit row action **"Capitalize as Fixed Asset"** moves one <Term id="serial">serialized</Term> unit out of stock at its carrying cost and creates the asset, or fills a Draft one, as **Active**. The form shows that cost before you confirm. The serial number travels with it. A unit inventory carries at no cost, such as one a job completed with no production or material recorded, or one issued into stock at no cost, takes the **Acquisition Cost** you enter instead. You also choose the **Offset Account** the value was spent from (Retained Earnings by default). Entering a cost takes permission to update accounting. To carry the unit at that cost in inventory first, use **Recost** on its storage-unit row.

<Callout type="note" title="A job that builds assets has two rules">
A job that completes to a fixed asset class needs a serialized item, so every finished unit maps to one numbered asset that the fleet can rent, return and capitalize. And it cannot be linked to a sales order line, because that job is a sale. Carbon refuses the job when you save it, and again at release.
</Callout>

<Callout type="note">
The purchase-receipt path activates the asset and posts its acquisition entry. Cost is added cumulatively, so an asset can accrue value across several receipts. Before the company has [set up accounting](/docs/reference/accounting#setting-up-accounting), the acquisition journal is Provisional, but the asset still takes its cost and its status.
</Callout>

## Fleet register

**Accounting → Fixed Assets → Fleet** lists every asset tied to an item, whether built on a job or capitalized from stock, with a status Carbon derives from the asset rather than stores:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **"Available"** | On the books, in service, and free to rent |
| **"Reserved"** | Named on a draft or undelivered line of a [rental agreement](/docs/reference/rental-agreements) |
| **"On Rent"** | Delivered to a customer on an active rental agreement |
| **"In Maintenance"** | Taken out of service |
| **"Sold"** | Disposed by sale or scrapping, including a rental unit [treated as a sale](/docs/reference/rental-agreements#sales-type-leases) |
| **"Returned to Stock"** | Returned to inventory |

Two actions manage whether a unit is <Term id="out-of-service">out of service</Term> without touching its accounting. **"Take Out of Service"** records a reason and date, keeps the asset depreciating, and flips the register to In Maintenance; **"Return to Service"** clears it. Both live on the asset page and in the register's row menu. A unit on rent is taken out of service from its rental agreement instead: **"Return"** it with **"Take out of service"** ticked.

An Available unit's row menu also offers **"Rent"**, which starts a new rental agreement with that unit on it.

A unit can also go back to stock. **"Return to Inventory"** on an Active or Fully Depreciated asset with an item puts the same serial back on the shelf at <Term id="net-book-value">net book value</Term>, and marks the asset Disposed.

The unit keeps that value. Under FIFO or LIFO costing, selling, issuing, or adjusting out that serial later relieves it at its net book value, not at the cost of the oldest stock. Return it to service first, and return it from its rental agreement if it is reserved or on rent: Carbon refuses either case.

The seeded **<Term id="rental-fleet">Rental Fleet</Term>** class is the default target for these units, and **"Build for Fleet"** on the register opens a new job with that class already set as its Complete To target.

## Adjusting cost

An Active or Fully Depreciated asset that came onto the books for less than it cost, such as a unit a job built with no labor or material recorded, takes the rest through **"Adjust Cost"** in the asset's menu. Enter the increase and the **Offset Account** it was spent from: Retained Earnings (the default) for an earlier year, or this year's labor or material expense account. Carbon debits the class's asset account, credits the offset account, raises the acquisition cost and records a **Cost Adjustment** transfer on the asset. A Fully Depreciated asset with value above its residual again returns to **Active**. Cost can only be raised here, and adjusting it takes permission to update accounting.

If depreciation already ran at the old cost, the next run catches up a straight-line asset: its first month carries the depreciation the earlier months missed, so the asset still finishes on its original schedule. Declining balance and MACRS correct themselves, since both depreciate what is left over the life that is left; units of production does not catch up.

## Depreciating

<Term id="depreciation">Depreciation</Term> runs as a **monthly batch you trigger**. There's no background poster. You create a run for a period, Carbon pulls every Active asset and computes each charge, you review it as a draft, then post: debit depreciation expense, credit accumulated depreciation. An asset under construction is not Active, so it is skipped until it is capitalized; a fleet unit out of service is still Active, so it keeps depreciating. Carbon keeps a separate **tax** book too, including <Term>MACRS</Term>, when tax depreciation is enabled. When an asset's net book value reaches its residual, posting flips it to **Fully Depreciated**.

Book depreciation follows the asset's method: <Term id="straight-line">straight line</Term>, <Term id="declining-balance">declining balance</Term>, or units of production.

## Selling vs. disposing

An Active or Fully Depreciated asset leaves the books two different ways:

| | Sell | Dispose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | A sales action | An accounting action |
| What happens | Drafts a **sales order** for the asset at its net book value | Posts a **write-off**: clears accumulated depreciation, removes the asset at cost |
| Asset status | Unchanged (Active or Fully Depreciated) until its shipment posts, then **Disposed** | **Disposed** |
| Money | Collected through the invoice, like any sale | Remaining book value booked as a loss |

<Callout type="warn">
Selling and disposing are **not** two names for one thing. Selling hands the asset to the normal quote-to-cash flow at book value; the asset leaves the books when its shipment posts, and the invoice then books the gain or loss against net book value. Disposing retires it directly: <Term id="disposal">disposal</Term> from the asset page is scrapping at a loss. A fleet unit has a third exit, Return to Inventory, described above.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info">
A fixed asset is **not** the same record as a work center. The machine you schedule production on and the machine you depreciate are tracked separately, but they can point at each other: an asset's **"Work Center"** field records which work center it serves, and that work center's page shows a read-only **Capital Cost** panel with its assets, their net book value, and the monthly straight-line depreciation they carry. Machine rates are still typed by hand.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting

### "Fixed Asset invoice line … has no asset selected"
A purchase invoice line of type *Fixed Asset* has no asset record linked. Open the line and select (or create) the fixed asset before posting the invoice.

### "Asset is no longer in Draft status"
The register action (or a capitalization into a Draft asset) found the asset already activated — usually someone else registered it between page load and save. Reload the asset; if it's Active, the registration already happened.

### "A job that completes to a fixed asset class needs a serialized item"
Raised when the job is saved, when it goes Ready (`$jobId.status.tsx`) and again by `complete_job_to_inventory`: the job's Complete To is a fixed asset class and its item is not serial-tracked, whatever the quantity. Use a serial-tracked item, or set Complete To back to Inventory.

### "Make to Asset needs a serialized item or a quantity of one"
Raised at the same three points for a job attached to an asset under construction: its item is not serial-tracked and its quantity is not exactly 1. Set the quantity to 1, use a serial-tracked item, or set Complete To back to Inventory.

### "A job linked to a sales order line cannot complete to a fixed asset"
Same gate: the job has a `salesOrderLineId`. A make-to-order job is a sale; create a separate job for the asset.

### "Job … targets fixed asset … which is not in a Construction in Progress class"
`complete_job_to_inventory`: the job's `fixedAssetId` points at an asset whose class is not flagged `isConstructionInProgress`. Only an asset under construction accumulates job cost; an Active asset's basis is never restated.

### "A job can only be attached to an asset under construction"
Attach Job needs a CIP-class asset at Draft or Under Construction. The server also refuses "Job … is Completed/Cancelled/Closed and can no longer be attached to an asset", "Job … is linked to a sales order line and cannot build an asset", and "Job … already completes to a fixed asset".

### "Only an asset under construction can be capitalized"
The Capitalize action needs a CIP-class asset at status Under Construction. Attach a job or post a purchase line first so it has cost; the server also refuses a CIP class as the target ("… is a Construction in Progress class; choose the in-service class") and an asset with no location ("Set a location on asset … before capitalizing it").

### "Only an asset capitalized from inventory can be returned to inventory" / "Only Active or Fully Depreciated assets can be returned to inventory" / "Return the asset to service before returning it to inventory"
Return to Inventory needs `fixedAsset.itemId`, status Active or Fully Depreciated, and no `outOfServiceSince`. A job-built asset has an item and can be returned too.

### "Asset … is on rent on rental agreement …; return it from the agreement first" / "Asset … is reserved on rental agreement …; return it from the agreement first"
Return to Inventory refuses a unit on a live rental agreement line (Pending or On Rent). Return the unit on the agreement, or cancel the agreement if it was never delivered.

### "… has no cost in inventory, so the asset would be worth nothing"
The unit is carried at zero and no cost was entered. Enter its **Acquisition Cost** on the Capitalize form, or give the item a unit cost first.

### "… is carried in inventory at a cost, so it is capitalized at that cost"
A cost was entered for a unit that inventory already values. The transfer moves the value inventory holds; capitalize it without a cost, then use **Adjust Cost** on the asset for anything more.

### "Choose the account the cost was booked to when it was spent" / "The offset account must be an active posting account"
An entered cost or a cost adjustment needs an active, non-group account to credit.

### "… only an Active or Fully Depreciated asset's cost can be adjusted"
Adjust Cost is offered for Active and Fully Depreciated assets. Register a Draft asset instead; a disposed asset is off the books.

### "Only an Available unit can be capitalized" / "Choose a serialized unit from the item's inventory to capitalize"
Capitalize as Fixed Asset needs an Available serialized unit with on-hand at the chosen location; the capitalize route is reached from the item's storage-unit row with `itemId`, `trackedEntityId`, `locationId` and `storageUnitId` in the URL.

### "Only a Draft or Under Construction asset can be purchased"
The asset page's Purchase action (`$fixedAssetId.purchase.tsx`, `PURCHASABLE_ASSET_STATUSES`) drafts a purchase order with one Fixed Asset line at price 0; it needs `create: purchasing`. An Active, Fully Depreciated, or Disposed asset cannot be bought again: register a new asset instead.

### "Asset was capitalized; reverse the capitalization first"
Voiding a receipt or purchase invoice whose Fixed Asset line fed a CIP asset that has since been capitalized (status no longer Under Construction). The CIP cost rows cannot be backed out of an Active asset.

### "The asset is already out of service" / "A disposed asset cannot be taken out of service"
Out-of-service gates on the asset route. Return to Service is a POST to the same route with `?intent=return`.

### "The asset is on rent on RA…; return it from the agreement and take it out of service there"
The unit is on a rental agreement. Return it from the agreement with **"Take out of service"** ticked; the register hides Take Out of Service for units on rent.

### Depreciation or receipt posting fails with a period error
Asset postings land in an accounting period like everything else. "Accounting period is closed/locked" means the run's period needs reopening — see [Accounting](/docs/reference/accounting).

## Internals
Construction in progress (a CIP class, `Under Construction`, Attach Job, Capitalize, Complete To → Asset Under Construction) is built but HIDDEN from the app until it returns with projects: `CONSTRUCTION_IN_PROGRESS_ENABLED = false` (`accounting.models.ts`) keeps CIP classes out of every class list and picker and hides the actions, the CIP cost card and the status filters. The engines, schema and error messages below are live, so treat a CIP asset as unreachable from the UI rather than impossible.
Tables: `fixedAssetTransfer` (sequence `FAT`; `type` Capitalization / Return to Inventory / Cost Adjustment; `sourceType` Inventory / Job / Construction in Progress / Manual), `fixedAssetCipCost` (append-only; `sourceType` Purchase Invoice / Receipt / Job / Manual), view `fleetAssets` (derived `fleetStatus`; since the rental migration it joins the live `rentalAgreementLine` for Reserved / On Rent and exposes `rentalAgreementId`, `customerId`, `customerLocationId`). Serial costing: a layer `bookAdjustment` writes for a serial unit carries `costLedger.trackedEntityId`, and `calculateCOGS` relieves the leaving unit's own layer first (`shared/cost-layer-order.ts`), so Return to Inventory's NBV layer is what that serial later costs under FIFO / LIFO; Standard and Average use the item's current cost. Columns: `fixedAsset.itemId`, `trackedEntityId`, `workCenterId`, `outOfServiceSince`, `outOfServiceReason`; `fixedAssetClass.isConstructionInProgress`; `job.fixedAssetClassId` / `job.fixedAssetId`. Server function `post-asset-transfer` (`capitalize`, `return`, `attachJob`, `capitalizeCip`, `adjustCost`); journal source type `Asset Transfer`; the job branch lives in `complete_job_to_inventory`. Routes: `/x/accounting/fleet`, `/x/fixed-asset/capitalize`, `/x/fixed-asset/:id/capitalize`, `/x/fixed-asset/:id/return-to-inventory`, `/x/fixed-asset/:id/adjust-cost`, `/x/fixed-asset/:id/attach-job`, `/x/fixed-asset/:id/out-of-service`. Seeded accounts 1370 Rental Fleet, 1380 Accumulated Depreciation – Rental Fleet, 1390 Construction in Progress; classes Rental Fleet and Construction in Progress.
