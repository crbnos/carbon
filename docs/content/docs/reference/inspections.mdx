Carbon's <Term id="inbound-inspection">inbound inspection</Term> evaluates received goods against a sampling plan. When a receipt posts for an item that [requires inspection](/docs/reference/quality), Carbon holds the received units, calculates the sample size, and requires inspection results before releasing stock.

This page describes the inspection lifecycle, sampling calculations, template step types, and ballooned-drawing FAI workflow. See [quality](/docs/reference/quality) for the module overview.

## The inbound inspection lifecycle

Inspection is **lot-based**: one inspection per received line (the `receiptLineId` is unique, so a line can't spawn two lots). The lot carries a snapshot of the resolved plan — sample size, acceptance number, rejection number, the standard, the code letter — frozen at receipt so later plan edits never rewrite history.

<StatusFlow entity="inspection">
  <Status name="Pending">The lot exists but no samples are recorded yet. Its tracked entities were posted **On Hold** at receipt, not Available.</Status>
  <Status name="In Progress">At least one sample has been inspected. The inspector is working through the sample size, recording each unit as Passed or Failed.</Status>
  <Status name="Passed" accent>The lot was **accepted** on disposition. Un-sampled entities are released to Available; sampled passes stay Available, sampled failures stay Rejected.</Status>
  <Status name="Failed" terminal>The lot was **rejected**. Every entity in the lot flips to Rejected, and an issue can be opened automatically.</Status>
  <Status name="Partial" terminal>Part of the lot passed and part failed. The passed part can be put away. An untracked lot holds the rest until its NCR is Closed, so it cannot be marked Partial while linked to a Closed NCR; a serial lot's failed units are Rejected.</Status>
  <Status name="Cancelled" terminal>The receipt was voided while the lot was still Pending or In Progress. A cancelled lot holds nothing.</Status>
</StatusFlow>

**Passed**, **Failed**, and **Partial** are disposition outcomes. Recording all samples does not close a lot; the inspector must select **Accept Lot**, **Reject Lot**, or **Partial**. **Cancelled** comes only from voiding the receipt.

<Callout type="warn" title="Received stock is held until the lot is decided">
A part that needs inspection posts into its location's inspection storage unit, a <Term id="non-nettable">non-nettable</Term> unit set in **Settings → Quality**. Serial and batch units are **On Hold**. For an untracked part, the open lot holds its lot size at that unit. Nothing held can be picked, shipped, issued or transferred until the lot is decided.

See [parts that need inspection](/docs/reference/receipts#parts-that-need-inspection) for setup, the receipt's inspection status and Put Away.
</Callout>

### What triggers a lot

A part needs inspection when it has an inspection plan assigned to **Receipt** (the part's **Quality** tab, **Inspection Plan Assignments**). Posting a purchase-order receipt opens one lot for each received line of such a part, and resolves the lot's sampling plan. Outside processing receipts open no lot. A sales return receipt goes to the inspection storage unit too, but opens no lot.

### Recording samples

Each recorded result is an `inboundInspectionSample` row. For a **Serial** item, the inspector scans or selects a discrete <Term>tracked entity</Term> per sample; for **Batch / Inventory / Non-Inventory** items there's no discrete unit to scan, so the sample records just a Pass/Fail with `trackedEntityId` left null (`inboundInspectionSampleValidator`, `quality.models.ts:754`). Same modal either way — the serial path just adds a Scan/Select step.

### Disposition

The inspection page has three disposition buttons:

- **Accept Lot** is available once the inspected count reaches the sample size and failures are at or below the **acceptance number**.
- **Reject Lot** is available on any open lot. Without a statistical failure, the reject dialog asks for a reason.
- **Partial** is available on an open lot of an untracked part, and on an open lot of a serial part that has a failed unit, a passed sample and complete sampling. A batch or non-inventory lot is accepted or rejected as a whole.

Disposition writes the terminal status, stamps `dispositionedBy` / `dispositionedAt`, and appends a row to `inspectionHistory` (a log kept for future plan auto-switching). Rejecting a **non-tracked Inventory** lot also posts a compensating `itemLedger` negative adjustment under the `Inbound Inspection` document type, booked from the storage unit that holds the lot.

A reject can also open an <Term id="nonconformance">issue</Term> automatically: the reject dialog has an "Open an NCR" checkbox, on by default. An untracked receipt lot belongs to one NCR, and a Partial lot must be the only lot on its NCR. See [Quality](/docs/reference/quality) for how those issues run to closure.

### Partial lots

**Partial** opens the **Partial Lot** dialog. What it does depends on the part's tracking:

| Tracking | What Partial does |
|---|---|
| Serial | Units that failed a sample stay **Rejected**. Every other unit, including units never sampled, becomes **Available** and is ready for putaway. |
| Untracked | You enter the **Accepted quantity**, more than 0 and less than the lot size. The accepted quantity is ready for putaway. The rest stays held until its NCR is **Closed**, so the dialog always opens an NCR and needs an **Issue Type**. |

### Where rejected stock goes

When the location has a rejected storage unit (**Settings → Quality**, **Goods-in storage**), Reject and Partial move the rejected stock there. For an untracked part, the hold moves with the stock. A full Reject of an untracked lot is written off instead, from the inspection storage unit. With no rejected storage unit set, rejected stock stays in the inspection storage unit.

## Sampling plans and AQL

Every lot needs a sample size and an accept/reject rule. That comes from the item's **sampling plan** (`itemSamplingPlan`, 1:1 with the item, created lazily) resolved against the lot size at receipt. There are four plan types (`samplingPlanTypes`, `samplingStandards.ts:17`):

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Inspect All" type="All">Sample size equals the whole lot. Accept on 0 defects, reject on 1.</Field>
  <Field name="Inspect First N" type="First">A fixed count off the top of the lot (`sampleSize`). Accept 0 / reject 1.</Field>
  <Field name="Percentage" type="Percentage">A share of the lot (`percentage`), rounded up, capped at the lot size. Accept 0 / reject 1.</Field>
  <Field name="AQL" type="AQL">Statistical acceptance sampling per ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 or ISO 2859-1 — the sample size and accept/reject numbers come from the standard's tables.</Field>
</FieldTable>

The first three plans use a zero-defect rule. **AQL** uses standard acceptance tables.

### How an AQL plan resolves

<Term id="sampling-plan-aql">AQL</Term> — Acceptable Quality Limit — is the defect rate you're willing to treat as "good enough". The resolver (`resolveSamplingPlan` in `samplingStandards.ts`, mirrored for `post-receipt` in `sampling-engine.ts`) takes the plan, the lot size, and the company's standard, then walks the standard's tables in two steps:

1. **Lot size → code letter.** A single lot-size-range table maps the lot to a code letter (A–R) for the chosen **inspection level**. Level II is the general default; I and III trade sample size for confidence, and S1–S4 are special small-sample levels (`inspectionLevels`, `samplingStandards.ts:20`).
2. **Code letter + AQL + severity → sample plan.** The code letter and AQL pick a cell giving the sample size `n`, the **acceptance number** (`Ac`, max defects to still accept), and the **rejection number** (`Re`). Severity switches which table is used: **Normal**, **Tightened** (stricter — used when a supplier's quality slips), or **Reduced** (`inspectionSeverities`, `samplingStandards.ts:31`).

The standard AQL values Carbon exposes are the twelve canonical steps `0.065, 0.1, 0.15, 0.25, 0.4, 0.65, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5, 4.0, 6.5, 10.0` (`standardAqlValues`, `samplingStandards.ts:34`). The whole Z1.4 / ISO 2859-1 constant tables live in `samplingStandards.ts` (and the posting copy at `sampling-engine.ts`) — ISO 2859-1 cells match Z1.4 for the plans Carbon exposes, so both standards share the same tables today.

<Callout type="note" title="The plan is snapshotted, not referenced">
When the receipt posts, the resolved numbers are copied onto the `inboundInspection` row (`sampleSize`, `acceptanceNumber`, `rejectionNumber`, `aql`, `inspectionLevel`, `severity`, `codeLetter`). Editing the item's sampling plan afterward changes future lots, never the lot already on the dock. If no plan is configured, `post-receipt` defaults to `Inspect All`, level II, Normal.
</Callout>

The **standard** itself (`ANSI_Z1_4` or `ISO_2859_1`) is a company-wide setting, not per-item — it lives on `companySettings.samplingStandard`, defaulting to ANSI Z1.4. A company can also require **four-eyes** sign-off on inspections via `companySettings.enforceInspectionFourEyes`, so the person who records a sample can't be the one who dispositions the lot.

## Inspection templates and step types

Sampling decides *how many* to inspect. An **inspection template** decides *what to check on each one*. Templates are built from ordered steps, and every step has a **type** that determines what the inspector is asked to do (`procedureStepType`, `shared.models.ts:207`):

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Task">A do-this instruction with no captured value — a checklist line.</Field>
  <Field name="Measurement">A dimensional reading. Requires a **unit of measure**, and takes an optional min/max range that bounds an acceptable value.</Field>
  <Field name="Value">A free numeric or text value with no unit requirement.</Field>
  <Field name="Checkbox">A pass/acknowledge tick.</Field>
  <Field name="Timestamp">Captures the moment the step was performed.</Field>
  <Field name="Person">Captures who performed the step.</Field>
  <Field name="List">A pick from a defined set of options (the `listValues`).</Field>
  <Field name="File">An attachment — a photo, a scan, a cert.</Field>
  <Field name="Inspection">A nested inspection reference.</Field>
</FieldTable>

The step validator (`qualityDocumentStepValidator`, `quality.models.ts:591`) enforces the type-specific rules: a **Measurement** step must have a unit of measure, a **List** step must have at least one non-empty option, and when both `minValue` and `maxValue` are set the max must be greater than or equal to the min.

## FAI, inspection documents, and balloons

For first-article inspection (FAI) and dimensional sign-off, Carbon works from the drawing itself. An **inspection document** is a part's PDF drawing overlaid with numbered **balloons**, each balloon marking a characteristic to inspect (`inspectionDocumentValidator` / `balloonFeatureValidator`, `quality.models.ts:106`).

Each balloon feature carries:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="balloonNumber" required>The number stamped on the drawing next to the characteristic — the reference an inspector reads off.</Field>
  <Field name="description" required>What the characteristic is (a bore diameter, a hole position, a surface finish).</Field>
  <Field name="nominalValue">The target value.</Field>
  <Field name="tolerancePlus / toleranceMinus">The allowed deviation above and below nominal.</Field>
  <Field name="unitOfMeasureCode">The unit the nominal and tolerances are expressed in.</Field>
</FieldTable>

Balloons anchor to a region of a specific PDF page (normalized 0–1 coordinates, so they stay put at any zoom) and each balloon can carry a step **type** from the same `procedureStepType` set, so a ballooned characteristic can be a Measurement, a Checkbox, and so on. Carbon can also auto-detect balloons from the drawing and turn detected regions into features. Because a balloon, its geometry, and its inspection feature must be created together or not at all, saves go through a single atomic RPC (`saveInspectionDocumentAtomic`) rather than separate writes.

Inspection plans live under **Quality → Inspection → Inspection Plans**. The editor has no Save button: each change saves itself a moment after you stop editing, and the toolbar shows **Saving…**, **Saved**, or **Could not save**.

### Plans from a model's PMI

A STEP AP242 model can carry its own dimensions and geometric tolerances (PMI, the model-based definition). When you upload such a model to a part, Carbon reads the PMI and makes an inspection plan for that part on its own. The plan holds the 3D model instead of a PDF:

- Each dimension and each geometric tolerance becomes one characteristic, numbered in the model's order. Datums and notes are left out, because they have nothing to measure.
- A ± dimension keeps its nominal and tolerances. A limit dimension, or one whose tolerances are both above or both below the nominal, is centred on its band, so a reading passes exactly between the drawing's limits.
- A geometric tolerance is measured as a deviation: nominal 0, with the tolerance zone as the upper limit.
- A dimension with no numeric tolerance (a fit class only, a min or max) is recorded Pass or Fail.
- Lengths are in the model's own unit (mm or in); angles are in degrees.

The plan is made once for each part and model. Uploading the same model again does not change it, so your edits are kept; a new model on the part makes a new plan. Assign it to receipts or an inspection operation like any other plan. While you record measurements, the model takes the drawing's place: picking a characteristic highlights its annotation on the model and turns the view to it, and picking an annotation on the model picks its characteristic.

### Gauges

A characteristic can name the [gauge](/docs/reference/calibration) type it must be measured with, in the plan editor's **Gauge Type** column. Leave it empty to allow any gauge.

When an inspection runs, each characteristic has a **Gauge** cell. The list offers only Active gauges, of the required type when one is set, with the gauges recently used at the same work center first; all receipts share one list. The gauge is recorded once per lot and characteristic, with the time it was recorded. An out-of-calibration gauge is flagged but can still be recorded, and a gauge retired later still shows on the lots it measured.

## Troubleshooting

Exact errors and the sampling/disposition preconditions from the inbound-inspection validators, the sampling engine, and the reject-to-NCR flow.

### "Status is required" / "Decision is required"
Recording a sample needs a result (**Passed**/**Failed**); dispositioning the lot needs a decision (**Accept**, **Reject**, or **Partial**). The corresponding field wasn't selected.

### "Inspection id mismatch"
The inspection id in the URL doesn't match the `inspectionId` on the submitted form — a stale page or a crossed request. Reload the inspection and try again.

### "Inspection not found"
The sample or disposition references an inspection that doesn't exist in this company. It was likely deleted or belongs to another company — reload the list.

### "Failed to save sample" / "Failed to accept lot" / "Failed to reject lot" / "Failed to mark partial"
The underlying transaction failed (inspection missing, an entity conflict, or a permission denial). Retry; if it persists the lot may already be in a terminal state — see below.

### "Enter the accepted quantity: more than 0 and less than the lot size"
Partial on an untracked receipt lot needs an accepted quantity above 0 and below the lot size. To accept the whole lot, use **Accept Lot**. An accepted quantity of 0 is refused with "That is the whole lot rejected. Use Reject Lot instead." The rest of the lot stays held, in the rejected storage unit when the location has one, until the lot's NCR is Closed.

### The inspection page shows a Cancelled lot
The lot's receipt was voided while the lot was Pending or In Progress. A Cancelled lot holds no stock and takes no samples or decisions.

### A terminal lot won't take more samples
Once a lot is **Passed**, **Failed**, or **Partial** it's terminal and locks — no further samples can be recorded and the status won't recompute. Only Pending/In Progress lots accept samples. To re-inspect you'd need a fresh inspection.

### The lot passed or failed on its own after one bad sample
Accept/reject is derived by the sampling engine, not chosen by hand. For All / First N / Percentage plans the acceptance number is 0 and the rejection number is 1, so a single defect fails the lot; AQL plans look up the accept/reject numbers from the ANSI Z1.4 / ISO 2859-1 code-letter tables by lot size, inspection level, and severity. Lot passes when sampled defects are within the acceptance number; it fails once defects exceed the rejection number.

### "Lot rejected. Configure at least one Issue Type and a default user location to auto-create an NCR."
The reject succeeded but the automatic NCR couldn't be created: the company has no issue types, or your user has no default location. Set up an issue type and a default location, or create the NCR manually. The lot is still rejected either way.

### "Lot rejected, but failed to create NCR" / "… failed to create NCR tasks" / "… failed to load it for NCR"
The lot was rejected but a later step of the auto-NCR pipeline failed (inserting the issue, seeding its workflow tasks, or reloading the lot for the NCR title). The rejection stands; the NCR may need to be created or completed manually.

### Permission-gated: recording samples or dispositioning a lot
All inbound-inspection actions require `quality` **update** and employee role, enforced again by row-level security. A blocked action means your role lacks the quality update permission.
</AgentContext>
