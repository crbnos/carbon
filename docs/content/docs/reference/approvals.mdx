---
title: Approvals
description: Route purchase orders, quality documents, suppliers and shop-floor scrap for sign-off before they take effect, with amount-tiered approval rules.
---

An **approval** keeps a document pending until an authorized user approves or rejects it. Per-company **approval rules** determine whether approval is required and who can decide. <Term id="purchase-order">Purchase-order</Term> rules can use amount tiers.

For example, with a **$25,000** purchase-order rule, a $4,000 order can finalize directly to **To Receive**, while a $40,000 order enters **Needs Approval**.

## What can be approved

These document types support approval. Each document waits in its pending status; scrap waits for an approver's PIN instead:

| Document | Pending status | Approved → | Rejected → | Amount-tiered? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase order | "Needs Approval" | recomputed (e.g. "To Receive") | "Rejected" | Yes, the order total |
| Quality document | "Draft" | "Active" | stays "Draft" | No |
| Supplier | "Pending" | "Active" | "Rejected" | No |
| Scrap | none: signed at the tablet | posts at once | not recorded | Yes, the estimated material value |

<Callout type="info">
Only **purchase orders** and **scrap** use the amount threshold. Quality documents and suppliers match a single
rule (its minimum is 0), so for them approval is simply on or off.
</Callout>

## Approval rules

Rules live under **Settings → Approval Rules**, grouped by document type. Each rule:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Minimum amount">The tier floor: approval applies at or above this, up to the next rule's minimum. 0 means every document of the type. (Purchase orders and scrap.)</Field>
  <Field name="Who can approve">Groups **and/or** individual users eligible to decide.</Field>
  <Field name="Default approver">A named fallback approver for the tier.</Field>
  <Field name="Enabled">Whether the rule is live.</Field>
</FieldTable>

For purchase orders, rules form **amount tiers**. Each tier runs from its minimum to the next tier's minimum. An order matches the highest threshold its total meets.

<Callout type="note">
There's **no global "require approval" switch.** A document type needs approval only while an **enabled**
rule exists for it. Disable or delete the rule and those documents finalize directly.
</Callout>

## Who can approve

A user can decide a request when they are the tier's **default approver**, are listed under **Who can approve**, or belong to an authorized group. They also need the relevant module permission: purchasing for purchase orders and suppliers, quality for quality documents, or settings for editing rules.

<Callout type="warn">
Approval authority flows **upward only.** Someone assigned to the $25k tier can approve a $4k order, but a
$4k-tier approver cannot approve a $250k one. Higher tiers cover everything beneath them; lower tiers don't
reach up.
</Callout>

## The decision

The pending document shows **Approve** and **Reject** on its header, each opening a confirm dialog.
Supplier and quality decisions can carry an optional note; the purchase-order dialog has no note field.
Approving runs the document's transition in one step; rejecting moves it to its rejected state (a quality
document simply stays in **"Draft"**). The person who raised the request can withdraw it while it's still
pending: on a purchase order that surfaces as **Delete** (only the requester can delete a pending order).

Carbon notifies the resolved approvers when a request is raised, and the requester when it's decided, over
**email, Slack, and in-app**. When a purchase order is decided, approvers of lower tiers are notified too, so
they can see spend that cleared above their level.

<Callout type="warn">
Approval is **single-step**. Carbon selects one rule, and one authorized decision finalizes the document. It does not support sequential stages, parallel sign-offs, quorums, or delegation. Amount tiers select a rule; they are not approval stages.
</Callout>

## Scrap at the tablet

Scrap posts the moment it is logged, so a scrap rule is met on the shop floor rather than in a queue. When an
operator logs scrap whose estimated value meets a rule, the scrap dialog shows the value and the people who may
approve it. One of them picks their name and enters their console PIN on the same tablet; the scrap then posts
as usual and the approval is recorded with it.

- **Value**: the material in the scrapped units: the materials the operation consumes (kitted sub-assemblies
  included) and those of the operations before it, times the quantity scrapped. For a scrapped component it is
  that component's cost. A standard-cost item counts at its standard cost, any other at its unit cost (its
  standard cost when the unit cost is 0). Labor and overhead are not counted. The scrap journal can write off
  less: it stops at the job's planned material, and a batch completion posts no scrap journal.
- **What the approver signs**: the value on the screen. Changing the quantity after the approver enters their
  PIN clears the PIN, and the new value is shown again.
- **Who may approve**: the approvers of the matched tier and every higher tier who have a console PIN. The person
  logging the scrap cannot approve it. Set a PIN from **Users → Employees → Set Console PIN**.
- **Paths**: scrapping a unit on an operation, scrapping a component, and completing a batch with scrap. Scrap
  typed in on a job's **Quantities** in the ERP can be recorded only by an approver at that tier.
- **Records**: every approved scrap is listed under **Production → Scrap Approvals** with its value, who logged
  it and who approved it. A row marked **Not confirmed** was approved but its posting never answered; check the
  job's scrap.

A wrong PIN counts against the same lockout as console pin-in. Approval rules for scrap need the Business plan,
like every approval rule.
