title: The MES app
description: The shop-floor surface where operators run operations, report quantities, issue material, and log their time.
---

Carbon is two apps over one data model: the ERP is the office, the <Term>MES</Term> is the floor. Where a planner releases and schedules work in the ERP, an operator picks up that work here, on a station tablet or a shared kiosk, and reports it back in real time. This page is the scannable map of that surface. For the narrative walkthrough, read the [run the floor tour](/guides/floor); for the entities behind it, see [jobs](/docs/reference/jobs), [picking lists](/docs/reference/picking), and [quality](/docs/reference/quality).

Every mutation in the **browser** hits the network. The MES keeps an IndexedDB read-cache for the item and people pickers so a scan stays snappy, but in a browser there is **no offline mode**: without a connection you cannot start, report, or issue anything. The [native app](#the-native-app) does queue work offline.

## Getting onto the floor

An operator signs in on the login screen with whichever methods the company has enabled: **"Sign in with Google"**, **"Sign in with Outlook"**, **"Sign in with Passkey"**, or **"Sign in with Email"** (a magic link, which lands on a **"Check your email"** confirmation). The available buttons follow the company's `AUTH_PROVIDERS`, so a floor that only allows passkeys sees only that one.

Once in, the operator sets their context. If the company has more than one location, a **"Location"** submenu appears in the sidebar to pick which floor they're standing on. With a single location the choice is skipped entirely. This selection scopes the board, the assigned lists, and everything they report.

### Console (shared-station) mode

A single tablet bolted to a work center can't ask every operator to log in and out all shift. **Console mode** turns that station into a shared kiosk. It's gated by the `consoleEnabled` company setting (Business plan), and when on, the sidebar exposes a **"Console Mode"** toggle. Switching a station INTO console mode takes `settings_update` on the account the station is signed in with; switching it back out is open to anyone, so a station is never stuck.

In console mode an operator identifies themselves with a **4-digit PIN**, not a full login. PINs are generated by Carbon and handed out by an admin — shown once when an operator is created or a PIN is reset, and stored only as a hash (see [shop-floor PIN accounts](/docs/reference/permissions#shop-floor-pin-accounts)). The station prompts to enter a PIN for the named operator, checks it, and attributes the work to that person. Three things stop it: **"Incorrect PIN"**, **"Too many incorrect PINs. Please wait a few minutes and try again."** after repeated wrong guesses, or **"No PIN set. Ask an admin to set a console PIN for you."** if the person has none yet.

<Callout type="note" title="One station, many operators">
A shared station is meant to hand off. **"Switch Operator"** re-prompts for a PIN so the next person takes over, and **"Pin Out"** clears the current operator. Ending the shift also pins out automatically. The PIN identifies *who did the work*; it is not a substitute for the person's real account.
</Callout>

## The operations board

The released work lands on the **"Schedule"** board: a Kanban with one column per <Term>work center</Term>, each column holding that center's queued <Term id="job-operation">operations</Term> sorted by **dispatch priority** so the top card is always the next thing to run.

The board is **display and filter only**. A Display popover lets each station tune its own view, toggling **"Empty work centers"** on or off and showing or hiding **"Customer"**, **"Description"**, **"Due Date"**, **"Duration"**, **"Progress"**, **"Status"**, **"Sales Order"**, and **"Thumbnail"** on each card. Filters narrow the queue by **"Work Center"**, **"Process"**, **"Tag"**, or **"Assignee"**.

<Callout type="warn" title="The floor shows the plan; it doesn't reshuffle it.">
Operators can filter and focus, but they cannot drag a card to reschedule it. Rebalancing the plan, moving an operation to a different work center or shifting a job's dates, happens on the [ERP scheduling boards](/docs/reference/scheduling), where a planner owns the sequence. What reaches the floor is the result.
</Callout>

### Focused lists

Alongside the board, three sidebar lists cut the queue down to what one operator cares about right now:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Assigned to Me">Operations assigned to the signed-in operator, grouped by work center. Empty state: **"No assigned operations"**.</Field>
  <Field name="Active">Operations the operator currently has running (a production event is open). Empty state: **"No active operations"**.</Field>
  <Field name="Recent">Operations the operator recently touched, for quick return. Empty state: **"No recent operations"**.</Field>
</FieldTable>

Each card carries a status badge, its progress, and a deadline chip (**"ASAP"**, **"No Deadline"**, or a date that turns red when overdue). The badge tracks the operation's own status, not the job's.

### Open Jobs

The **"Open Jobs"** list is the job-level view: columns for **Job**, **Item**, **Quantity**, **Tracking**, **Assignee**, **Due Date**, **Deadline**, and **Status**. One relabel to know here, because it trips people up:

<Callout type="note" title="A job at &quot;Ready&quot; shows as &quot;Released&quot; on the floor.">
The ERP job status <Term id="job">jobs</Term> reach when they're cleared to run is `Ready`, but the MES Open Jobs list renders that same value as **"Released"** — the floor's word for "you can start this now." It's a display alias, not a different status. `Closed` jobs also surface in this list.
</Callout>

Opening a job shows its operations as a directed graph (the routing DAG), so an operator sees what feeds what. The toolbar lays it out **"Left to Right"** or **"Top to Bottom"** and offers **"Fit"**; nodes flag a **"Rework"** loop and a scrapped-quantity count when they apply.

## Running an operation

Open an operation and the station panel is where the shift actually happens. A big control starts and stops the clock: **"Start"** opens a production event, **"Pause"** closes it. A toggle picks which kind of time is being logged, matching the three <Term id="production-event-type">production event types</Term>: **"Setup"**, **"Labor"**, or **"Machine"**. The panel is tabbed into **details**, **model** (the 3D part view), **procedure** (work instructions, with **attributes** and **parameters** sub-views), and **chat** (a per-operation note thread).

### The guided assembly view

An operation whose type is **Assembly** opens a guided 3D view instead of a plain part model: the assembly plays back one step at a time, and each step lights up the components it installs.

Deep into a build this creates a problem. The parts already fitted sit between the camera and the ones the current step is naming, so the operator is looking at a finished-looking lump and guessing where the next piece goes. A view control in the player toolbar answers that directly:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Build">The assembly as it stands on the bench right now: everything fitted so far is solid, and anything from a later step is not drawn. This is the default.</Field>
  <Field name="Focus">The already-fitted parts fade to see-through, so the current step's components are visible in place without losing the shape around them.</Field>
  <Field name="Full">Every component solid, the finished product.</Field>
</FieldTable>

The view only changes what the operator sees. It never changes the step order, the components a step installs, or anything reported back.

The author can also hide parts that get in the way on a particular step, and group steps into a sub-assembly that is built on its own before it goes in as one piece. Both play here exactly as authored; see [assembly instructions](/docs/reference/assembly-instructions).

<Callout type="note" title="The camera follows what is drawn.">
Picking a view that hides parts also stops the camera treating them as obstacles. On **Build** and **Focus**, parts from later steps are not drawn, so the view angle never angles around them.
</Callout>

The same control appears in the ERP assembly editor, where an author checks that a step reads clearly before publishing. An author and an operator ask the model the same question, so the buttons mean the same thing on both screens.

### Reporting quantities

Good, bad, and reworked units are reported separately, so yield stays honest:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Log Completed">Records good units off this operation. **"Complete All"** fills the remaining quantity; **"Total Quantity"** sets it explicitly.</Field>
  <Field name="Log Scrap">Records scrapped units against a required **"Scrap Reason"**, so the loss is attributable. The unit flips to **"Scrapped"**, a replacement is spawned, and the routing reopens — see [scrap and unscrap](/docs/reference/scrap).</Field>
  <Field name="Log Rework">Records units reworked in place at this operation, without sending them anywhere.</Field>
</FieldTable>

Reworking *in place* is different from sending a unit back upstream. **"Create Rework"** does the latter: pick an operation under **"Go back to operation"**, give a **"Reason for rework"**, and Carbon re-runs every operation from that point forward to the current one.

Closing the operation is a distinct action. **"Finish"** ends any open events and flips the operation to `Done`. If quantities or steps aren't satisfied Carbon warns (**"Insufficient quantity"**, **"Steps are missing"**) and offers **"Finish Anyways"**. A guard also fires when tracked material was never issued; the operator must acknowledge **"I understand and want to complete without issuing"** to proceed.

<Callout type="warn" title="&quot;End Operations&quot; is not the same as finishing.">
**"End Operations"** ends *all* the operator's active production events at once (a clean end-of-shift stop) and, when time cards are on, clocks them out. It does **not** complete or finish any operation. The work stays exactly where it was, ready to resume next shift.
</Callout>

### Issuing material

Material is consumed against an operation from the same panel. The **"Issue"** action has an Adjustment Type: **"Add to Inventory"** (a `Positive Adjmt.`) or **"Pull from Inventory"** (a `Negative Adjmt.`). For serial- or batch-<Term id="tracked-entity">tracked</Term> material, tabs let the operator **"Scan"** a barcode, **"Select"** from stock, or **"Unconsume"** to return material already issued.

Two edge cases surface right in the flow rather than failing silently. Issuing expired stock is governed by a policy (`Warn`, `Block`, or `BlockWithOverride`); an overridable block asks for a **"Reason for issuing expired stock"**. And when a pick splits a batch, the station reports a **"Batch Split Occurred"** and offers to **"Convert to New Size/Revision"** or scrap the remainder.

### Clocking in and out

When the `timeCardEnabled` company setting is on, the sidebar carries a time card: **"Clock In"** and **"Clock Out"**, a **"My Hours"** view (a table of **Date**, **Clock In**, **Clock Out**, **Duration**), and a **"Clocked in since"** badge with the start time while a shift is open. Leave a shift open too long and Carbon nudges: after twelve hours it asks **"Forgot to Clock Out?"** with **"I'm Still Working"** or **"Set Clock Out"**. Time cards are labor time for payroll and presence; they are separate from the per-operation production events that drive job costing.

## Picking, quality, inspections, and maintenance

The floor does more than run operations. Four adjacent surfaces live in the same app.

### On-floor picking

The **"Picking"** view lists the picking lists assigned to the operator. **"Start"** moves a list to `In Progress`; **"Finish"** takes it to `Completed`. Each line is worked with **"Pick"**, **"Unpick"**, **"Short"**, or **"Scan"** (for tracked material), and a **"No stock"** badge flags an empty source. A short pick asks **"How many were actually picked?"** and records the shortfall with **"Mark Short"**.

<Callout type="note" title="The floor can't reopen a closed picking list.">
Once a list is `Completed` or `Cancelled` it locks. MES shows **"This picking list is closed. Reopen it from the ERP to continue."** — reopening requires an inventory permission the floor doesn't hold. See [picking lists](/docs/reference/picking) for the full lifecycle.
</Callout>

### Quality issues and inspections

An operator who spots a problem raises it without leaving the station. **"Create Quality Issue"** captures an **"Issue Type"**, a **"Priority"** (Low, Medium, High, or Critical), and a **"Description"**, and files a non-conformance for quality to triage. See [quality](/docs/reference/quality) for how that record is worked.

Inspections and work instructions run as typed steps inside the procedure tab. A step is one of nine kinds:

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Task">A do-this instruction the operator marks complete.</Field>
  <Field name="Checkbox">A yes/no confirmation.</Field>
  <Field name="Value">A recorded text or free value.</Field>
  <Field name="Measurement">A numeric reading with bounds — out of range, Carbon warns that the value must fall between the configured min and max for the unit of measure.</Field>
  <Field name="Timestamp">Captures the moment the step was done.</Field>
  <Field name="List">A choice from a predefined list.</Field>
  <Field name="Person">Records who performed the step.</Field>
  <Field name="File">An uploaded attachment (a photo, a document).</Field>
  <Field name="Inspection">A pass/fail gate with a **"Passed Inspection"** switch.</Field>
</FieldTable>

### Maintenance dispatch

When a machine goes down, an operator raises a maintenance dispatch from the floor. Every dispatch created here is `Reactive` by source (scheduled and non-conformance dispatches originate elsewhere). It carries a severity, a priority, and an OEE impact; choosing an OEE impact of **"Down"** ends the work center's active production events, because the center can't produce while it's under repair. The maintenance worker executes it from a detail view with Play, Pause, and Complete controls, a **"Time Worked"** total, and a **"Spare Parts"** section (**"Add & Issue"**), landing on **"Maintenance Completed"** when done.

## The native app

Carbon MES also runs as a native app on iOS and Android, built on the same data
through a JSON API (`/api/v1`) that executes the same server code the browser's
own routes execute. That is the design's central constraint rather than a note
about it: a write from a tablet runs the same material backflush, the same cost
posting, the same genealogy and the same floor gates as a write from Chrome, so
the two cannot report a job differently.

<Callout type="warn" title="Not in the app stores yet">
The app is not published to the App Store or Google Play. It runs today through
Expo Go or an internal build made from this repository, which is what the pilot
devices use. Nothing below describes a download you can install from a store.
</Callout>

### Linking a device

A device is pointed at one Carbon by scanning the QR code on the **"Connect a
device"** page in the ERP or MES, or by typing the server's address. One device
can hold several linked instances — a production server and a staging one, or
two customers' servers — and every piece of state the app keeps is scoped to the
one it is on: the session, the chosen company and location, the read cache and
the offline queue.

Sign-in is the same 6-digit emailed code the browser uses. A password is
accepted only for the store-review accounts listed in `APP_REVIEW_EMAILS`,
because an App Store reviewer cannot receive a code sent to someone else's
mailbox.

### What an operator can do

<FieldTable>
  <Field name="Operations">The operations at the chosen location, filtered by work center. Start and pause Setup, Labor or Machine time; report good, scrap and rework; finish the operation.</Field>
  <Field name="Materials">The operation's bill of material with issued-against-required. Scanning a part anywhere on the tab issues it. Tracked parts carry their lot or serial.</Field>
  <Field name="Instructions">The work instructions with their step records, per unit. A File step takes a photo with the camera or from the library, stored in the job's own files.</Field>
  <Field name="Notes">The operation's notes — what the last shift left for this one.</Field>
  <Field name="Picking">The lists assigned to this kitter, with quantity and tracked-lot picking and the company's expiry policy enforced.</Field>
  <Field name="Timecard">The week's entries with a live elapsed timer, and End Shift.</Field>
  <Field name="Scan">The camera, and a hardware scanner paired in keyboard mode. A Carbon QR code opens the operation or picking list it names.</Field>
</FieldTable>

Quality issues and label printing are available from the operation's **"More"**
sheet. Nothing is printed by the device — the print is queued on the server,
which already knows the printers.

### What stays in the browser

The app is deliberately narrower than the MES in a browser, and it says so in
place rather than hiding the control:

- **Assembly and inspection operations** open in the browser; the app sends the
  operator there by name.
- **Batch-mode operations** are reported in the browser.
- **Timestamp, Person and Inspection** work-instruction steps are recorded in
  the browser. The instruction text is still readable in the app.
- **Maintenance requests** are raised in the browser.
- **Editing or deleting a time-card entry** happens in the browser.

### Shared terminals

A tablet bolted to a work center is turned into a shared terminal from the
app's **"More"** screen, the same idea as the browser's console mode and gated
by the same company setting and plan. Each operator then picks their name and
enters their console PIN, and a header above the tab bar shows whose name the
next write will carry — amber while nobody is pinned in, because on a shared
terminal that is not a neutral state. Both the terminal and operator claims are
held in memory only, so a stolen tablet carries no operator session and a cold
start asks for a PIN.

### Offline work

Unlike the browser, the app queues what it cannot send. Commands are ordered
per operation, so two reports on one operation apply in the order the operator
made them, while a jammed operation never blocks another. A retry reuses the
original request key, so a replay cannot post a second production event; a
failure the server already recorded stops that operation's queue and waits for
the operator rather than retrying by itself.

### Air-gapped and controlled environments

When `/me` reports an air-gapped deployment or a controlled environment, the app
makes no outbound request to any host but the linked Carbon — analytics are not
merely disabled but never initialised. In a controlled environment an idle
device signs itself out; on a shared terminal an idle device pins the operator
out instead, so the terminal stays ready for the next person.

### Languages

Thirteen languages, sharing the browser MES's own translation catalog — so a
string already translated for the web is already translated in the app.

## Troubleshooting

The exact blocks operators hit on the floor, and what unblocks each.

### "Incorrect PIN"
In console mode the 4-digit PIN entered for the named operator didn't match. Re-enter it, or use **"Switch Operator"** to pick the right person. The PIN identifies who did the work; it is not the person's login password.

### "Too many incorrect PINs. Please wait a few minutes and try again."
Five wrong PINs for one person within 15 minutes lock that person's pin-in, from every station, for a minute at first (doubling on repeat offenses, up to an hour). A station is also capped at 30 wrong PINs in 15 minutes across all operators — and stations signed in with the same account share that cap. Wait, then enter the right PIN.

### "No PIN set. Ask an admin to set a console PIN for you."
The named person has no console PIN, so console mode can't attribute work to them. PINs aren't chosen on the floor: an admin generates one in the ERP — **Reset PIN** under **Settings → Users → Operators** for an operator, or **Set Console PIN** on the **Accounts** list for anyone else (that one also needs `settings_update`). Then the station can pin them in.

### "This picking list is closed. Reopen it from the ERP to continue."
The picking list is already `Completed` or `Cancelled`, which locks it — the floor can't pick, unpick, short, or scan against a closed list. Reopening it requires an inventory permission the MES doesn't hold; a user reopens it from the ERP.

### Finish is blocked or warns ("Insufficient quantity" / "Steps are missing")
**"Finish"** flips the operation to `Done`, but guards fire first. If reported quantity is short you'll see **"Insufficient quantity"**; if procedure steps aren't satisfied, **"Steps are missing"**. You can override with **"Finish Anyways"**. Separately, if tracked material was never issued, the operator must tick **"I understand and want to complete without issuing"** to proceed.

### "Forgot to Clock Out?"
A time card was left open for twelve hours or more. Choose **"I'm Still Working"** to keep the shift open, or **"Set Clock Out"** to close it. Time cards are labor/presence time for payroll — separate from the per-operation production events that drive job costing.

### The Console Mode toggle or the time card isn't there
Both are company-setting gated. **"Console Mode"** only appears when `consoleEnabled` is on and the plan includes it, and then only to an account with `settings_update` (or on a station already in console mode, so it can leave); the **"Clock In"/"Clock Out"** time card (and the 12-hour prompt) only appear when `timeCardEnabled` is on. If a floor needs either, an admin enables it in company settings.

### I can't drag a card to reschedule on the floor
The MES **"Schedule"** board is display and filter only — it never reschedules. Rebalancing the plan (moving an operation to another work center, or shifting a job's dates) happens on the [ERP scheduling boards](/docs/reference/scheduling), where a planner owns the sequence.
</AgentContext>
