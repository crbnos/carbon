Carbon tracks capitalized equipment, such as presses, CNC machines, and forklifts, as **<Term id="fixed-asset">fixed assets</Term>**. Their value enters the books at acquisition and decreases through <Term id="depreciation">depreciation</Term> until sale or disposal.

A fixed asset is an accounting record, not a production resource. Its **<Term>asset class</Term>** defines the general-ledger accounts for acquisition cost, accumulated depreciation, depreciation expense, and write-offs.

<Callout tone="amber" badge="ASSET ≠ WORK CENTER" title="A fixed asset is not the same record as a work center.">
The machine you schedule production on is a <Term>work center</Term>; the machine you depreciate is a fixed asset. They stay separate records, so the same physical press is a work center on the floor and a fixed asset in the books. If you want the books to know which press that is, set the asset's **"Work Center"** field: the work center then shows a **"Capital Cost"** panel listing its assets, and scheduling is unaffected.
</Callout>

## Onto the books

A machine you buy for the shop comes onto the books one of two ways, and both start at **"Draft"**. The [fixed assets reference](/docs/reference/fixed-assets) covers the other two: building one on a job and capitalizing a unit from stock.

**Register one you already have.** Create the asset, then register it, supplying its acquisition cost, acquisition date, and the date depreciation should start. A machine that is already partway through its life also takes its **"Accumulated Depreciation"** to date. Registering moves it to **"Active"**. No cash moves, but it posts a journal: the asset account takes the acquisition cost, the opening accumulated depreciation goes to its contra account, and the net book value offsets retained earnings, since you're recording something you already own.

**Buy one through purchasing.** Put a **"Fixed Asset"** line on a purchase order pointed at the asset, then receive it. The asset page's **"Purchase"** action drafts that purchase order for you.

When the receipt posts (or a purchase invoice for the line posts with no receipt), Carbon debits the asset account, credits goods-received-not-invoiced, **adds** the cost to the asset, stamps the dates, and flips it from **"Draft"** to **"Active"**. Because the cost is added, an asset can accumulate value across several receipts.

<Callout tone="blue" badge="BEFORE THE CUTOVER" title="The asset moves before accounting is set up; the ledger waits.">
Before the company sets up accounting, receiving still adds the cost and activates the asset, but its journal is Provisional and counts in no balance. The setup's **Fixed assets** step opens each asset at its cost and accumulated depreciation on the cutover date. See [setting up accounting](/docs/reference/accounting#setting-up-accounting).
</Callout>

## Depreciate it

Depreciation applies to **"Active"** assets. It runs as a user-initiated monthly batch, not as a scheduled background job. Create a run for a period, review the calculated charges in **"Draft"**, and then post it.

Carbon calculates the book charge using the asset's method: **<Term>straight line</Term>**, **<Term>declining balance</Term>**, or **units of production**. Posting debits depreciation expense and credits accumulated depreciation for the period.

<Figure illustration="depreciation-curve" caption="Net book value falls from acquisition cost toward residual: straight line in equal steps, declining balance faster early." />

<Callout tone="neutral" badge="DRAFT THEN POST" title="Depreciation is computed at run time, reviewed, then posted.">
Carbon calculates amounts when it creates the run and holds them in draft for review. When posting reduces an asset's <Term>net book value</Term> to its <Term>residual value</Term>, Carbon marks it **"Fully Depreciated"** and stops further depreciation.
</Callout>

## Two books at once

Each asset has independent **book** and **tax** depreciation schedules. They can use different methods; the tax schedule supports **<Term>MACRS</Term>** and IRS property-class tables.

When tax depreciation is enabled, posting a run can also recognize the **deferred tax** created by the difference between the two schedules.
