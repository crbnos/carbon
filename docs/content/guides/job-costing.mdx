As production proceeds, Carbon accumulates consumed material and labor, machine, and overhead costs in <Term id="wip">work-in-process</Term>. Job completion transfers that cost to finished-goods inventory. This guide describes the ledger entries, timing, and accounts for that flow.

## Accounting is a switch

The company-level **accounting enabled** setting is **off by default**. When disabled, Carbon still updates item-ledger quantities, job and operation statuses, and material issues, but it does not create general-ledger entries or cost layers. When enabled, the same operations also post financial entries.

<Callout tone="amber" badge="OFF BY DEFAULT" title="Quantities always move; money only moves when accounting is on.">
Receiving, issuing, and completion always update physical inventory and status. The GL postings in this guide occur only when accounting is enabled.
</Callout>

## Cost lands in WIP

Two cost streams enter a job's work-in-process balance.

<Figure illustration="wip-inflow" caption="Two streams feed a job's WIP: material out of inventory, plus labor, machine, and overhead time at the work center's rates." />

**Material.** When a job consumes a part through <Term id="material-issue">issue</Term> or completion <Term id="backflush">backflush</Term>, Carbon moves its cost from inventory to WIP. The *Job Consumption* entry debits work-in-process and credits inventory.

**Labor, machine, and overhead.** Each production event has a duration that Carbon prices at the **work center's** rates and posts in real time. The *Production Event* entry debits WIP and credits the applicable absorption account. Labor and machine time post at their respective hourly rates. If the work center has an overhead rate, Carbon also debits WIP and credits overhead absorption. All rates come from the <Term>work center</Term>, not the operation estimate.

<Callout tone="blue" badge="WHAT LANDS IN WIP" title="WIP collects real material plus labor, machine, and overhead time.">
WIP consists of consumed material and production events priced at work-center labor, machine, and overhead rates. Overhead posts only when the overhead-absorption account is configured. These are actual costs, not standard estimates.
</Callout>

<Screenshot src="/screens/job-production-events.png" label="Job — production events" caption="Production events on J000001 — setup, labor, and machine time logged against each work center — priced into WIP at that work center's rates." ratio="wide" />

## Issued or backflushed

Material reaches WIP through **issue** or **backflush**. Issuing consumes a part during the operation, including after a picking list stages it at the line. At job completion, backflush consumes required material that has not already been issued.

Both methods credit inventory and debit WIP. Use issue for explicit consumption control and backflush for routine material.

<Callout tone="neutral" badge="SCRAP LEAVES WIP" title="Ruined cost is relieved to a scrap account, not carried to close.">
<Term>Scrap</Term> and <Term>rework</Term> are separate production quantities. Scrapping a tracked part debits the scrap account and credits WIP, or inventory if the part remains in stock. The entry includes the reason, work center, and operator. Scrap removes the cost from the job and reopens the routing for a replacement. See [scrap and unscrap](/docs/reference/scrap).
</Callout>

## WIP isn't a table

Work-in-process is not stored as a job field or separate table. It is the **[general-ledger](/docs/reference/accounting) balance** of entries posted to the work-in-process account with the job ID.

The **cost ledger** stores each inbound cost layer and its remaining quantity. The item's <Term>costing method</Term> determines consumption value: **FIFO** and **LIFO** select open layers, **Average** uses the running unit cost, and **Standard** uses the standard cost. The method changes the amount but not the flow from inventory to WIP and then finished goods.

<Callout tone="neutral" badge="COST LAYERS" title="A layer is a cost-ledger row with quantity left on it.">
An open cost layer is a cost-ledger row with remaining quantity; there is no separate layer table. FIFO and LIFO traverse these rows by date. The costing method selects the consumption cost but does not affect WIP tracking.
</Callout>
