Building the satellite is most of the work, but it isn't the whole job. You still have to prove it was built right, and close the loop with the customer and your books.

## Raise an issue

Say an operator flags a short in the EPS wiring harness. You open an Issue, Carbon's name for a <Term id="nonconformance">non-conformance</Term>. It moves through a clear lifecycle, from Registered to In Progress to Closed, and it links to the part and the job operation it was found on, so the bus now carries that open issue everywhere it appears in the system.

## Workflows & actions

An issue doesn't just sit there — it runs a workflow you define. A classic <Term>8D</Term> is one such workflow; a quick containment-only response is another. Whichever you pick, it spins up the required actions as tasks, each one started and completed on its own:

<Figure illustration="issue-workflow" caption="An issue moves Registered → In Progress → Closed, spinning up its required-action tasks along the way." />

- <Term id="containment-action">Containment</Term>: stop the bad parts from spreading. Set it on the affected process and it surfaces on the shop floor, so the next operator can't keep building the defect.
- <Term id="corrective-action">Corrective</Term>: find and fix the root cause.
- <Term id="preventive-action">Preventive</Term>: change something so it can't recur.
- Verification: prove the fix actually worked.
- Communication: keep the customer in the loop.

<Callout tone="amber" badge="DISPOSITION" title="Held material doesn't move until someone signs off.">
A review step gates what happens to the non-conforming assemblies: use as is, <Term>rework</Term>, <Term>scrap</Term>, or return to the supplier. Nothing is dispositioned on a hunch; the decision is recorded against the issue alongside its actions.
</Callout>

## Versioned procedures

Those work instructions the operator followed didn't appear from nowhere. They're generated from versioned procedures, the same Draft / Active / Archived discipline you saw on <Term id="method">methods</Term>. When a corrective action changes how the structural frame is assembled, you publish a new revision; every future job picks it up, and every job already running keeps the revision it started on.

<Callout tone="green" badge="REVISIONS" title="Change the procedure once; the floor follows everywhere.">
New jobs pick up the latest published version; in-flight jobs never shift mid-build. And every satellite's record pins the exact procedure revision it was actually built to.
</Callout>

## Traceability

Because everything was captured at build time, the <Term>genealogy</Term> of a finished satellite is complete. Carbon stores it as a graph of <Term id="tracked-entity">tracked entities</Term> and the activities that link them, so from one serial you can walk backward to every ancestor (which frame, which battery lot, which plate of aluminium) or forward to every place a lot was used.

<Figure illustration="traceability-graph" caption="The genealogy of one finished robot, from serial number down to material lots." />

One click, or one scan of the satellite's QR label, answers the question every auditor and every recall asks: what exactly went into this unit, and where else did that lot go?

<Screenshot src="/screens/ship-traceability-graph.png" label="Traceability graph" caption="The full as-built genealogy for SAT-1000 serial SAT1000-SN-0001, down to the aluminium plate and reaction wheels it consumed." ratio="wide" />

## Ship & invoice

The first finished units are ready. You create a [shipment](/guides/order-to-cash) against the <Term id="sales-order">sales order</Term>. It drafts its lines from what's outstanding, and you ship only what's actually built: on SHP000001 the line reads 2 shipped against 3 ordered, so 1 stays outstanding. Post it, then <Term id="invoice">invoice</Term> that same 2. Because the order still has a unit to ship, it stays at "To Ship and Invoice" and simply carries the remainder forward; the line's shipped count rolls up as each batch goes.

Carbon ties the invoice straight into your accounting system, so the build that started at the [sales desk](/guides/quote-to-cash) closes on the books — partial quantity and all.

<Screenshot src="/screens/ship-partial-shipment.png" label="Partial shipment" caption="Shipping part of the order — SHP000001 sends 2 of the 3 ordered, leaving 1 outstanding, so the order stays open." ratio="wide" />
