Creating a <Term>job</Term> generates demand for its materials and capacity. <Term id="sales-order">Sales orders</Term> provide confirmed demand, while forecasts represent expected demand that is not yet ordered.

Carbon plans against both sources and applies each part's replenishment policy.

## Demand forecasting

Under **Production**, open **Projections** and enter expected weekly quantities. In this example, demand for SAT-1000 increases from two units per week to four by week six, with corresponding demand for ADCS-001 and EPS-001. <Term id="demand-forecast">Forecasting</Term> adds these quantities to the plan without creating sales orders.

## Reordering policy

Each part has a <Term>reordering policy</Term> that controls when and how much Carbon proposes to replenish:

- **Manual Reorder**: Carbon creates no proposal; a planner decides when and how much to order.
- **Demand-Based Reorder**: replenish net demand, grouped by the configured demand-accumulation period.
- **Fixed Reorder Quantity**: when on-hand reaches the <Term>reorder point</Term>, propose the configured reorder quantity.
- **Maximum Quantity**: when on-hand reaches the reorder point, propose enough to reach the maximum inventory quantity.

<Figure illustration="reorder-policy" caption="Reorder point in action: on-hand falls to the trigger, and an order tops it back up." />

<Callout tone="blue" badge="BUY OR MAKE" title="The policy decides how much; the replenishment system decides what kind of order.">
The policy determines quantity. The replenishment system determines the document: **Buy** creates a <Term id="purchase-order">purchase order</Term>, while **Make** creates a job. Reorder, minimum, and maximum quantities adjust the proposed quantity to an orderable amount.
</Callout>

<Screenshot src="/screens/plan-part-planning.png" label="Part: Planning tab" caption="A part's Reordering Policy, Reorder Point, and Reorder Quantity that drive every suggestion." ratio="wide" />

## Into jobs & POs

Carbon nets order and forecast demand against on-hand inventory, open jobs, and open purchase orders. [Production planning](/docs/reference/planning) proposes jobs for manufactured parts; purchasing planning proposes purchase orders for purchased parts.

<Figure illustration="planning-engine" caption="Demand in, plan out: production planning suggests jobs, purchasing planning suggests POs." />

Purchasing planning applies <Term id="conversion-factor">conversion factors</Term> to translate inventory units into purchasing units. A proposal becomes a job or purchase order only after confirmation.

<Callout tone="neutral" badge="BY LOCATION" title="Demand and planning can differ by location.">
Run plans by location. The same part can use different replenishment settings at different sites.
</Callout>

## Receiving

When a purchase order arrives, create a <Term>receipt</Term>. Carbon initializes the Draft receipt from the order and validates its quantities. <Term id="posting">Posting</Term> the receipt adds the goods to on-hand inventory.

For a <Term id="batch">batch-tracked</Term> item, record the batch number on the receipt line. The conversion factor converts the purchased quantity to the inventory quantity. Carbon retains the batch identity for later genealogy queries.
