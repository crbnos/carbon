The satellite bus's antenna pointing bracket needs a thicker wall and one fewer fastener. Create a
<Term id="change-order">change notice</Term>, edit a hidden draft of the part's method, review the diff, and release
the change. Until release, jobs, planning, and costing continue to use the current bracket.

> A change notice records the change and contains its working draft. Release makes that draft active.

## Start with the parts, not the change

Start from **Items → Change Notices → New**, the part header, the part's table row, or **New Change Notice** in
the method-version menu. Item-based entry points attach the bracket as the first affected item. Creating a
notice from a quality issue links the non-conformance to the resulting change.

The header form contains **Name**, **Category**, **Reason for Change**, **Description of Change**, **Owner**,
**Priority**, and dates. Categories come from the configured change-notice types, initially seeded with values
such as *Design Improvement*, *Cost Reduction*, and *Obsolescence / End-of-Life*. A quality-issue entry point
also links its non-conformance. After creation, **Add Affected Item** accepts Parts only; materials,
consumables, and services are excluded.

A part on an open change notice displays *"This part is on 1 open change notice"* with a link to the notice.
This warning is informational. Carbon permits parallel notices for the same part and assigns independent
draft versions to avoid version collisions.

<Screenshot src="/screens/co-new-notice.png" label="New change notice form" caption="Name, category, reason, owner, and priority. The header before any affected item is added." ratio="wide" />

<Callout tone="blue" badge="TOP-DOWN, NOT BOTTOM-UP" title="You pick the parts first, then edit them in place.">
Select the affected part, then edit its bill of materials and bill of process inside the change notice. The
completed draft defines the change.
</Callout>

## Draft the change in place

Adding the bracket copies its active <Term id="bom">method</Term> into a **Draft** version associated with the
change notice. The draft is excluded from version selectors, copy-target lists, MRP, jobs, and costing. Edit it
with the standard **Bill of Material** and **Bill of Process** editors.

Before editing, select a **change type** for the affected item. The type determines which fields are editable
and what release creates. Changing the type later discards the draft and creates a new one from the live
method, removing all draft edits.

<Screenshot src="/screens/co-affected-item.png" label="Affected item with change-type selector" caption="An affected item's draft method open for editing on CN-000001, with the 'Change type' select on the item card." ratio="wide" />

- <Term id="change-order-change-type">Version</Term> changes the BoM or BoP for the same part. Release activates
  a new method version and archives the previous version. It creates no item or supersession.
- **Revision** creates the next revision under the same Part ID. Its draft supports attributes, documents, and
  method edits. Release activates the revision and supersedes the previous one.
- **Replacement Part** derives a new part number from the affected part. Release activates it and supersedes
  the affected part in a one-to-one replacement.
- **New Part** creates a part without a predecessor. Release activates it without creating a supersession.

<Callout tone="amber" badge="A VERSION MOVES THE METHOD, NOT THE PART" title="Only Revision and Replacement Part supersede.">
A **Version** changes the method on the existing item, preserving its stock and history without supersession.
A **Revision** or **Replacement Part** derives a new item and writes a supersession at release. A **New Part**
has no predecessor and creates none. Because a purchased part has no method to version, a **Buy** item defaults
to **Revision**.
</Callout>

This bracket requires a new drawing revision and a stock phase-over, so select **Revision**. Update the wall
attribute, remove the fastener from the BoM, and attach the revised drawing to the draft.

<Callout tone="amber" badge="PURCHASED PARTS NEED A SUPPLIER" title="A bought revision releases un-buyable unless you set its supplier up first.">
A purchased revision includes a **Supplier Parts** grid. Supplier parts are not copied from the old revision.
Before releasing a **Buy** revision, add its supplier, supplier part number, price, and ordering quantities.
Without a supplier part, the released item cannot be added to a purchase order.
</Callout>

## Review the diff

Advance the notice one stage at a time: **Draft**, **Start**, **Engineering Complete**, then **Implementation**.
Entering **Start**, **Implementation**, or **Done** notifies the notice assignee and action-task assignees.
The stages are the approval and release workflow; there is no separate approval configuration. **Cancel** is
available at every open stage. It locks the notice but preserves its drafts. **Reopen** returns a Cancelled
notice to Draft with its edits intact.

The overview's **Changes** card displays each affected item's current diff throughout authoring, including
material, property, and supplier-part changes.

At **Implementation**, **Release** first displays a read-only diff for every affected item. It compares added
and removed materials, operation and step changes, and modified attributes with the snapshotted method.

<Screenshot src="/screens/co-release-diff.png" label="Release change notice — diff review" caption="The dropped fastener line and the thicker-wall attribute, shown as a diff before you confirm." ratio="tall" />

<Callout tone="neutral" badge="EXACT BY CONSTRUCTION" title="The diff is real because the draft is real.">
The diff compares the actual draft method with its live source method. The reviewed draft is the artifact that
release activates.
</Callout>

## Release, and let supersession carry it

On confirmation, Carbon processes each affected item. For the bracket revision, it activates the draft method,
archives the previous version, activates the new revision, and writes a <Term>supersession</Term> from the old
revision to the new one. After all items are released, the notice becomes read-only in **Done**.

Configure phase-over through the affected item's cutover fields: a <Term id="supersession-mode">supersession
mode</Term>, which defaults to **Consume First**, a discontinuation date, and a successor effectivity date.
Release creates the supersession automatically.

<Figure illustration="conversion-factor" caption="Once effective, the old bracket's demand redirects to the new revision, times its conversion factor." />

**Consume First** uses on-hand stock of the old bracket before redirecting demand. **Prefer New** redirects new
demand immediately. After the successor becomes effective, MRP redirects planned demand to the new revision,
and **Get Method** substitutes it on new jobs while retaining the source item for traceability.

<Callout tone="green" badge="NOTHING IN FLIGHT SHIFTS" title="Release steers the future, not the past.">
Existing stock remains on the old bracket. Existing jobs retain their materials and method versions. Open
purchase orders and quote lines also retain the old revision without warning or rewrite. Supersession affects
only forward planning: jobs created after the effectivity date use the new revision; earlier jobs do not.
</Callout>

<Divider />

The new revision contains a *"Created by CN-…"* link to its change notice, and the bracket page retains the
complete change-notice history.
