## TLDR

A **project** is an engineering workspace. Its content is a tree of typed **elements**: Systems, Requirements, Functions, Tasks, Test Cases, Parameters and Folders. Trace links connect the elements across the tree. The tree is the project's work breakdown structure (WBS), and each chargeable element is a *charge number*. The workspace follows Flow Engineering for the engineering model and Plane (an open-source Linear/Jira) for the look and feel.

A team can design, build and bill a whole project from the workspace alone:
1. Define the systems and the requirements.
2. Add tasks and test cases under them.
3. Log time and issue parts straight to any element, from the element itself.

No job, service order or other document is necessary. Jobs, PO lines and service orders can still carry a charge number when the work needs them:
- a **job** carries an element,
- a **PO line** carries an element,
- stock can be **issued straight to an element**,
- a **timesheet** logs hours against elements, in the ERP and the MES.

Like a BOM, each element can carry planned **materials**, **labor** and **other costs**. Their sum is the element's plan cost, and the materials become real demand.

Each project has one of four **cost treatments**:
- *Statistical* (tag only, today's behaviour),
- *Expense* (R&D, ASC 730),
- *Capitalize* (construction in progress),
- *Contract* (contract WIP, relieved by billing or revenue).

The treatment decides the posting account **at the moment the cost happens**, so there is no period-end settlement run. Every charge also lands in an append-only **project ledger**. The ledger keeps the charge's nature (material, labor, machine, outside processing, purchased service, other), the source document and its billing state. That ledger is the single source for the project cost report, commitments, and T&M and cost-plus billing. Later it is also the source for earned value, indirect burden and R&D tax reporting.

A project can hold **project stock**: inventory striped with its project, valuated through the existing FIFO `costLedger`. Moving stock between projects takes an explicit, approved **Transfer**, **Borrow** or **Payback**. A Borrow carries a payback date, and MRP treats it as firm demand on the borrower and firm supply on the lender.

Projects can be pooled into a **planning group**. That is SAP PMMO's Grouping, Pegging and Distribution, done the way SAP cannot:
- MRP plans each project's or group's demand **in its own netting segment**.
- MRP **pegs group supply to member-project demand during the run**, writing a versioned pegging ledger.
- A grouped receipt or job output is **split into member-project stock at the physical event**, following the pegs. Cost moves with the stock, so there is no batch distribution run and no cost shifting after receipt.
- MRP proposes the cross-project Transfers and Borrows it needs as planning actions.

**Billing** extends Contracts with the *Time & Materials* and *Cost Plus* billing methods, tied to a billing element. A billable-item engine draws on the project ledger: hours × labor-category bill rate, materials at cost plus handling, cost plus fee. Items are reviewed, capped by a funded amount, and invoiced through the shared recurring-invoicing layer.

Delivery is in five phases. Phases 1–3 are specified fully here, Phase 4 at design level, and Phase 5 as an outline.

## Problem Statement

Carbon cannot run a project.

- **"Project" is only a tag.** `project` is a flat list (`name`, `description`, `active`), coded on three spend-line tables (`purchaseInvoiceLine`, `chargeLine`, `reimbursementLine`) and pushed to Ramp. It has no structure, status, budget or treatment. Jobs, PO lines, inventory and time cannot reference it. `post-purchase-invoice` writes the Project dimension only for G/L Account lines.
- **R&D time has nowhere to go.** `productionEvent.jobOperationId` is NOT NULL, `timeCardEntry` is attendance only, and the only non-job costed time is maintenance. An engineer who spends Tuesday on a prototype cannot log it.
- **R&D parts can't be charged.** The only way to consume stock is to issue it to a job. An R&D job's labor and material sit in job WIP until completion, so a prototype spanning a month-end leaves R&D cost on the balance sheet. ASC 730 says it is expensed as incurred.
- **Customer projects can't be billed on actuals.** The contracts spec explicitly leaves T&M, usage and milestone billing out of v1. No cost-plus, no billable items, no funding limits.
- **A&D primes can't share supply across contracts.** There is no stock ownership: `itemLedger` has no owner column and `trackedEntity.status = Reserved` means "in-progress output". MRP nets one pool per item × location × period. A defense manufacturer must buy and build in common across contracts for economies of scale. It must also keep cost per contract for CAS and DCAA, and move material between contracts only by documented transfer or loan (DFARS 252.242-7004). A&D primes cite this capability for choosing SAP, and SAP itself only half-delivers it:
  - MRP plans one grouping segment.
  - Pegging and distribution are separate batch runs (Lockheed: 37–720-minute pegging, distribution up to 30 hours).
  - Every reschedule shifts cost between contracts.
  - PP/DS ignores grouping.
  - PMMO has no borrow/loan/payback.

Worked example used throughout:

> *Orbital Dynamics* has three government contracts for star trackers (projects **P-101**, **P-102**, **P-103**, all *Contract*, CPFF / T&M / FFP). It also has an internal R&D project **P-900** (*Expense*) for a next-gen sensor.
>
> P-101/102/103 are in planning group **Star Tracker**. Each project has the elements Engineering, Production (with Optics and Final Assembly under it) and Test. All three need the lens `ST-050` (P-101: 20, P-102: 30, P-103: 10).
>
> P-900 has the identifier `SNS`. Its tree holds the System `SNS-1 Sensor head` with the Requirement `SNS-4 Mass ≤ 120 g`, the Parameter `SNS-7 Sensor head mass`, the Task `SNS-12 Optical bench prototype` and the Test Case `SNS-15 Mass inspection`.
>
> An engineer logs 6 h to `SNS-12` from the task's own page, and 2 h to a P-101 engineering element on Tuesday. A technician issues 4 lenses to `SNS-12` from the same page. A buyer places one PO for 60 × ST-050 for the group. A prototype job for P-900 consumes stock and labor straight into R&D expense.

## Proposed Solution

### Concepts

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Project** | The header: **identifier** (a short key such as `SNS`, unique per company), name, cost treatment, status, customer (optional), manager, location, dates, project-stock flag, planning group, currency. Grown from the existing `project` row. |
| **Element** | A node of the project's tree. It has a **type**, a readable id (`SNS-12`: identifier plus a per-project sequence), a name, a rich-text description, a parent, a state, an owner, a priority, labels, dates, a milestone, an hours estimate, a budget, and the flags **chargeable** and **billing**. An element can also have a **part link**: an item, plus a serial when the item is serialized. Elements are the project's work items, in the Plane sense. |
| **Element type** | A fixed set: **Folder**, **System**, **Requirement**, **Function**, **Task**, **Test Case**, **Parameter**. Each type adds its own fields (below). Custom fields come from Carbon's `customField` mechanism. |
| **State** | A project-defined workflow state (for example *Todo*, *In Design*, *In Review*, *Done*). Each state belongs to one fixed **state group**: Backlog, Unstarted, Started, Completed, Cancelled. The group decides whether the element accepts charges. |
| **Trace link** | A typed, directed link between two elements that is not the parent link: *satisfies*, *verifies*, *derives*, *implements*, *blocks*, *precedes*, *relates to*. Trace links never roll up cost. |
| **Parameter** | An engineering value with a unit (mass, power, range). It is manual, a roll-up of its child parameters, a **formula** over other Parameters, or a value read from the linked part. It can be an allocation of a parent parameter (30 % of 2,000 kg) and can carry a contingency %. A Requirement compares its target with a Parameter. |
| **Constraint** | Not a separate type. A constraint is a Requirement whose target compares with a Formula Parameter, for example `Power margin = supply − load ≥ 0 W`. |
| **Project type** | **Standard** (every project a user creates) or **Service** (the tree of one service order, created by the order; see *Service projects*). |
| **Billable item** | One `projectBillableItem` row: a ledger row with its billing quantity, price and status. Service projects create them at posting; Phase 4 adds contract billing. |
| **Milestone** | A dated window for the project (a design review such as PDR, a phase or a sprint). Each element can belong to one milestone. Plane calls this a cycle. |
| **Charge number** | What a picker shows and what a timesheet line names: the element's readable id and name, `SNS-12 Optical bench prototype`. The project's identifier alone (`SNS`) charges the project with no element. Only chargeable elements in an Unstarted or Started state of a Released project can be picked. |
| **Cost treatment** | Where the project's cost posts, decided at transaction time. **Statistical**: today's account, project as a dimension only. **Expense**: the project's expense account, e.g. R&D Expense. **Capitalize**: the CIP account. **Contract**: Project WIP, relieved by billing or revenue recognition. Set on the project. A WBS branch may override it from an effective date, which covers the IAS 38 research → development switch. |
| **Plan lines** | A element's **materials** (item, quantity, required date, method: Pull from Inventory / Make to Order / Purchase to Order), **labor** (labor category, hours, dates) and **other costs** (description, amount). Like `jobMaterial`, materials are real demand. |
| **Project ledger** | Append-only `projectLedger`, one row per cost charged to a element, carrying its nature (`projectCostType`), source document, quantity, amount, journal line link, and billable/billing state. The CJI3 equivalent. |
| **Labor category** | The cost class of an hour (e.g. *Engineer II*), with an effective-dated **cost rate**. Employees are assigned one; a timesheet line may override it. Billing labor categories and bill rates come in Phase 4. |
| **Timesheet entry** | One row per employee × date × charge number × labor category with hours, a note and a status (Draft → Submitted → Approved → Posted). A correction never edits a posted row: it posts a reversal plus a replacement with a reason. |
| **Project stock** | Inventory whose ledger rows carry `projectId`. Null means **common stock**. Project-striped stock can only be consumed by its own project's demand. |
| **Transfer / Borrow / Payback** | `projectStockTransfer` documents. **Transfer** moves stock and its cost from one owner (project or common) to another. **Borrow** moves the stock now, while the lender keeps the cost until a **Payback** on or before the payback date returns like-for-like stock. Both are approval-gated and need a reason. |
| **Planning group** | A named pool of projects (e.g. *Star Tracker*) that MRP nets as one segment. It has an excess rule (*Common stock* or *Exception project*) and a default pegging priority. |
| **Peg** | A `projectPeg` row assigning a quantity of a grouped supply (PO line, job, later planned order) to one member project's demand (element plus source). **Soft** until the supply is received or completed, **hard** after. Versioned: a change supersedes the old row and never deletes it. |
| **Distribution** | Splitting a grouped receipt or job output into member-project stock by the hard pegs at the moment of receipt. Cost moves with the stock through `costLedger`. |
| **Netting segment** | MRP's fourth key, `planningSegmentId`: `''` for common, a planning group id, or a lone project's id. |

### Lifecycle

`projectStatus` has the values **Draft → Released → Technically Complete → Closed**, plus **On Hold**. This is SAP's Created / Released / TECO / Closed. Elements do not have this status. An element has a workflow **state**, and its state group gates charging (see *Element states* below).

| Status | Allows | Blocks |
|---|---|---|
| **Draft** | Editing the structure, plan lines and budget. Delete (project or element, only if nothing was ever charged). | Every charge. Plan-line materials do not drive MRP. |
| **Released** | Charging: timesheets, issues, PO lines, jobs, transfers, subject to each element's state group. Plan-line materials drive MRP. | Delete. |
| **On Hold** (project) | Posting against existing commitments: receipts and invoices of open PO lines, completion of open jobs. | New charges (new timesheet lines, issues, PO lines, jobs). MRP still plans its existing demand. |
| **Technically Complete** | Late invoices and receipts against existing commitments. Reversals. Billing. Paybacks owed to or by the project. | New charges and new planning. Plan-line demand leaves MRP. |
| **Closed** | Reading, reporting, billing of already-billable items. | Every posting. Allowed only when the project has no open PO lines, no open jobs, no unpaid Borrow, and no project stock (transfer it to common or another project first). |

- **Project status gates everything.** No element of a Draft project accepts a charge, whatever its state.
- **Reopening** goes Technically Complete → Released and is audited. Closed is final in v1.

### Phase 1: the project workspace, charging and the project ledger

#### The project workspace

The workspace is where a team defines, designs, builds and tracks a project. It combines two models:
- **Flow Engineering** gives the engineering model: typed elements, a single-parent tree, trace links, parameters with units and targets, and verification.
- **Plane** gives the work model and the UI: work items with states, owners, priorities, labels, dates and milestones. Plane shows them as a list, a board, a table and a timeline.

Neither product tracks cost or time. Carbon adds charge numbers, time, materials, budgets and billing to the same elements.

**Element types.** Each type shares the common fields in *Concepts* and adds its own:

| Type | Purpose | Own fields | Chargeable by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Folder | Groups elements (for example "Vehicle Mass") | none | No |
| System | A part of the product: system, subsystem or component | none; the part link (below) names the Carbon part | Yes |
| Requirement | A need the design must meet | statement (the description), category (Functional, Performance, Interface, Environmental, Design Constraint), target (`≤`, `≥`, `=`, range; value; unit), verification method (Test, Analysis, Inspection, Demonstration), computed verification status | Yes |
| Function | What a system does | none | Yes |
| Task | A unit of work | estimate in hours | Yes |
| Test Case | A check that verifies one or more requirements | steps (rich text) or a linked quality procedure (`qualityDocument`), verdict (Not Run, Pass, Fail, Inconclusive), evidence attachments | Yes |
| Parameter | An engineering value | value, unit, source (Manual; Sum, Min or Max of child parameters; Formula; Item), formula, item field, allocation % of a parent parameter, contingency % | No |

Any type can be a child of any type, except that a Parameter's children must be Parameters. A user can change the chargeable flag on any element.

**Readable ids.** Each project has an `identifier` (2 to 6 capital letters, unique per company). Each element gets `identifier-sequence`, for example `SNS-12`. The sequence never changes when an element moves in the tree, so a charge number printed on a traveller or a timesheet stays valid. The outline number (`1.2.3`) is shown as a computed column only.

**Part links.** Any element except a Parameter can link a part:
- `itemId`: the Carbon item (a part, an assembly, a tool);
- `trackedEntityId`: one serial of that item, optional. A trigger refuses a serial of a different item.

A part link does 4 things:
1. The element page shows the part's thumbnail, readable id and revision, with a link to the part.
2. **Issue material** on the element suggests the part's bill of materials first (Pull from Inventory and Purchase to Order lines of its make method).
3. A Parameter with source **Item** reads its value from the nearest linked part at or above it (below).
4. The element history by part (`get_element_history_by_item`) lists past hours and materials on every element linked to the same item, across projects. The budget section shows it as a hint, for example "past average: 5.8 h over 12 elements".

**Build the tree from a part.** **+ New → Build from part** creates elements under a chosen parent, from one of 3 sources:
1. **A serial's build history.** When Carbon built the serial, its genealogy (`trackedActivity` with `trackedActivityInput` / `trackedActivityOutput`) names the serials and lots it consumed. Each consumed serialized component becomes a System element linked to its item and serial. The walk recurses into each component's own build history.
2. **An item's make method.** The item's active make method (`activeMakeMethods`, which falls back to the highest Draft version) gives the method materials. Each Make to Order child becomes a System element linked to its item, and the walk recurses into its method.
3. **A template project** (*Copy from*, below).

The user chooses the depth (default 2 levels) and whether bought components become elements (default off, so the tree holds assemblies, not fasteners). The build writes all elements in one transaction. A user can then edit, move or delete them. An element built from a serial keeps the serial link. An element built from a make method links the item only.

**Element states.** Each project has its own states (seeded: Backlog, Todo, In Progress, In Review, Done, Cancelled). Each state belongs to a fixed group. The group decides what the element accepts:

| State group | Accepts new charges | Accepts posting against commitments | MRP plans its plan-line materials |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backlog | No | No | No |
| Unstarted | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Started | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Completed | No | Yes (late receipts and invoices) | No |
| Cancelled | No | No | No |

The first charge on an Unstarted element moves it to the project's default Started state. Completed is SAP's Technically Complete for one element.

**Trace links.** `projectElementLink` holds typed, directed links. Each type has a reverse label:

| Link | From → to | Reverse label |
|---|---|---|
| satisfies | System or Function → Requirement | satisfied by |
| verifies | Test Case → Requirement | verified by |
| derives | Requirement → Requirement (across branches of the tree) | derived from |
| implements | Task → System, Function or Requirement | implemented by |
| blocks | any → any | blocked by |
| precedes | any → any (finish-to-start, for the timeline) | follows |
| relates to | any → any | relates to |

The parent link is the only link that rolls up cost, hours and parameters. A link is **suspect** when the element at its "to" end changes a key field (name, statement, target, value) after the link was last reviewed. The element page shows a suspect badge until a user clears it.

**Verification.** A Requirement's status is computed:
1. If its target compares with a Parameter (`actualParameterId`) and the comparison fails, the status is Fail. The comparison uses the Parameter's **checked value**, which includes its contingency (below).
2. If a verifying Test Case has verdict Fail, the status is Fail.
3. If every check passes and at least one check exists, the status is Pass.
4. If the Requirement has child Requirements, it passes only when all children pass.
5. Otherwise the status is Unverified.

**Parameters and allocations.** A Parameter gets its value from its source:

| Source | Value |
|---|---|
| Manual | typed by a user |
| Sum, Min, Max | computed from its child Parameters |
| Formula | computed from `parameterFormula` over other Parameters of the same project |
| Item | read from the nearest linked part at or above the Parameter: `parameterItemField` names the item's unit cost (`unitCost`) or a numeric custom field of the item |

- **Formulas.** A formula starts with `=` and refers to Parameters by readable id: `= SNS-7 - SNS-9`, `= max(SNS-3, SNS-4) * 1.2`. The grammar has numbers, readable ids, `+ - * /`, parentheses, `min`, `max` and `abs`, and nothing else. A pure TypeScript parser in `computeVerification` evaluates it, so no code runs. A formula that refers to itself through any chain shows an error and no value.
- **Automatic links.** Saving a formula writes one read-only *feeds* link from each Parameter it reads to the formula Parameter. The Tree layout shows them, so a user sees what a change affects. Values and verification recompute live, so a *feeds* link never turns suspect.
- **Contingency.** `parameterContingencyPercent` (0–1) is a growth allowance, as aerospace mass and power budgets use. The checked value moves toward failure. Against a `≤` target it is value × (1 + contingency). Against a `≥` target it is value × (1 − contingency). Against `=` and Range, both must pass. The element shows the value and the checked value.
- **Allocation.** A Parameter with an allocation % shows its budget as a share of the parent value: "≤ 600 kg (30.0 % of 2,000 kg)". It shows Pass or Fail against that budget.
- **Units** are free text in v1 and never convert. Roll-ups add only children with the same unit; a child with a different unit raises a warning. A formula that adds or subtracts Parameters with different units also raises a warning.

**Money and hours.** Every element has a budget amount and an hours estimate. Both roll up the parent tree with the actuals from `projectLedger` and the timesheets (see *Plan, budget, actuals*). Money budgets are separate from Parameters. A Parameter is an engineering value, and a budget is money.

**Milestones.** `projectMilestone` has a name, a start date and an end date (for example SRR, PDR, CDR, or a two-week sprint). Each element can belong to one milestone. A milestone shows a progress ring (completed elements ÷ all elements), its hours and cost actuals against plan, and a burn chart. Phase 4 milestone billing releases billing plan lines from a milestone.

**Views.** One element list with six layouts, all from Plane except Tree and Reader:
1. **List** (the default): rows indent by tree level and have a quick-add row per group. A user can group the rows by state, type, owner, priority, label or milestone.
2. **Board**: columns by state, cards per element.
3. **Table**: a spreadsheet with editable cells and the money columns: budget, committed, actual, remaining, hours estimated, hours actual, parameter value.
4. **Tree**: a graph of elements and trace links (Flow's tree view), drawn with `@xyflow/react` and dagre.
5. **Timeline**: a Gantt chart of element dates with *precedes* links as arrows.
6. **Reader**: a document of the elements in tree order, for example a requirements specification, with PDF export.

A type filter (All, Systems, Requirements, Tasks, Test Cases, Parameters) works with every layout. Each user's layout, grouping, ordering, shown properties and filters persist per project. A user can save a filter set as a named view.

**Element page.** It opens as a side panel at 50 % width (Plane's peek), as a modal, or as a full page. From top to bottom:
1. The parent pill and the readable id.
2. The name, then the description (`@carbon/tiptap`). A Requirement's statement is its description.
3. The type block: target and verification status, parameter value and allocation, test steps and verdict, or the linked part.
4. The action row: Add child, Add link, **Log time**, **Issue material**, Attach.
5. Collapsible sections:
   - Children (with a progress ring),
   - Trace links (grouped by type),
   - **Time & materials** (this element and its descendants),
   - **Budget** (plan, budget, committed, actual, estimate at completion),
   - Attachments.
6. Activity: the audit history and comments.

A properties sidebar beside the content holds these properties:
- type, state, owner, priority, labels,
- start date, target date, milestone,
- charge number (with copy), chargeable,
- budget, hours estimate and the cost treatment override.

**Templates.** Tick *Template* on any project. Then **New project → Copy from** copies its elements, trace links, parameters, plan lines and budgets. The copy gets a new identifier and Draft status. This replaces SAP's standard WBS.

**Billing flag.** Phase 4 uses it. At most one billing element can exist in each branch of the tree. An element and its ancestors cannot both be billing elements.

#### Baselines and change requests (Phase 1c)

Flow Engineering branches the whole project graph. Carbon branches only the **plan layer**: elements, trace links, parameters, budgets and plan lines. Time entries, issues, receipts and journals are facts. They never branch.

- **Baseline.** A read-only snapshot of the plan layer, taken by a user or at a milestone. A user can compare any two baselines, or a baseline with the current plan. The compare view reuses the change notice diff engine (`diffRows`, `items.service.ts`) and its UI (`ChangeNoticeDiffViewer`, `diff-ui.tsx`).
- **Change request.** A draft set of plan-layer edits with a title and a reason. Reviewers see the diff per element, comment on a changed field, and mark each element as viewed. The approvals engine (`packages/ee/src/approvals`, new `approvalDocumentType` value `projectChangeRequest`) decides who must approve. On approval, the change request applies its edits and writes a baseline.
- **Lock.** A project can require change requests after a chosen milestone. After that milestone, users cannot edit the plan layer directly.
- **Variants** (SUV, 2026, 2027 in Flow) are product configuration. They belong to items, revisions and make methods, not to projects.

#### Charging: the five entry points

Every entry point uses one picker component, **Charge number** (`ChargeNumberSelect`). It lists chargeable elements in an Unstarted or Started state of Released projects as `SNS-12 Optical bench prototype`. A user can search by project, readable id or name. The picker remembers the user's recent picks. A charge with no element charges the project itself, unless the company setting `requireProjectElement` is on.

The element page puts the two most common entry points on the element itself:
- **Log time** opens a small form: date (default today), hours, labor category (default the employee's), and a note. It writes a timesheet entry for the current user.
- **Issue material** opens a small form: item, quantity, storage unit, and lot or serial. It posts an Issue to Project for the element.

With these two actions, a team can build and bill a whole project with no job, service order or PO.

1. **Timesheet** (ERP *My Timesheet* and MES *Timesheet*).
   - A weekly grid: rows are charge number × labor category, columns are days. Each cell is hours plus an optional note.
   - The **Submit** step locks the week. If the company setting `timesheetApprovalRequired` is on, a manager **Approves** from *Projects → Timesheet Approvals*, filtered to their projects. If the setting is off (the default), submit posts the entries at once. Posting does the following for each entry:
     - cost = hours × the labor category's cost rate effective on the entry date,
     - GL: Dr the treatment's account / Cr `laborAbsorptionAccount` (the maintenance labor journal pattern, `post-maintenance-event`),
     - dimensions: Project, Project Element, Employee,
     - one `projectLedger` row of type Labor.
   - The MES shows the employee's week next to their timecard. Total timesheet hours versus timecard hours is a visible reconciliation in v1, not a block (see Phase 5).
2. **Job.**
   - The job form gains **Charge number** and a **Consume to project** toggle. The toggle defaults on when two conditions are true: the project's treatment is Expense, Capitalize or Contract, and the project has no project stock. Phase 2 adds *Receive to project stock*.
   - **Capitalize exception.** A job charged to a Capitalize element does **not** post at source. It sets `job.fixedAssetId` to the element's CIP asset (see *Capital projects*). Then the rentals branch's job-to-CIP path takes over:
     - The job posts to WIP as any job does.
     - The posting sweeps the WIP to CIP when the job is attached.
     - `complete_job_to_inventory` sweeps the remainder at completion.

     There is one CIP mechanism, not two. Each job posting still writes the project ledger. So the project sees the cost as it is incurred, even while the CIP asset only sees it at the sweep.
   - For Expense and Contract, a **consume-to-project** job keeps the job as the execution and costing document (`documentId = jobId`, `costLedger`, estimates versus actuals). But it posts **at source** to the project's account rather than job WIP. These postings change:
     - `issue`: Dr project account / Cr inventory,
     - `post-production-event`: labor, machine and overhead; Dr project account / Cr absorption,
     - outside processing in `post-receipt` and `post-purchase-invoice`.
   - Each of those also writes a `projectLedger` row (Material / Labor / Machine / Overhead / Outside Processing).
   - **Completion** of a consume-to-project job records quantity complete with **no inventory receipt and no GL**, the way a service job completes. **Close** has nothing to sweep: no variance account.
   - A job **not** consuming to project (output to common stock) still carries the charge number for reporting and commitments only. Its costs are inventory, and the project is charged when that stock is issued to it.
   - Jobs created from a plan-line material with method *Make to Order* get the charge number and toggle automatically.
3. **PO line.**
   - `purchaseOrderLine` gains **Charge number**, which defaults onto the receipt and invoice lines.
   - **Non-inventory lines** (G/L Account, Service, Consumable, Tool, Fixture): the receipt or invoice debits the project's account instead of the line's own account. Statistical keeps the line's account and adds dimensions. Writes a `projectLedger` row of type Purchased Service / Other Direct.
   - **Inventory lines** (Part, Material): with no project stock, the receipt is **consumed at receipt**: Dr project account / Cr GRNI. The receipt also writes a Positive Adjmt. and Negative Adjmt. `itemLedger` pair, so quantity history and traceability survive. This is SAP account assignment P for stock material. With project stock (Phase 2) the receipt goes to project-striped stock.
   - PO lines created from a plan-line material with method *Purchase to Order* get the charge number.
   - **Commitments** = open PO line amount (ordered − received, at line price, in base currency) per charge number.
4. **Issue to project.**
   - The user starts from *Inventory → Issue to Project*, **Issue material** on the element page, or **Issue** on a plan-line material. The user picks item, quantity, storage unit / lot / serial, and charge number. Posts:
     - `itemLedger`: Negative Adjmt., document type **Project Issue**,
     - `costLedger`: Consumption at the FIFO cost from `shared/calculate-cogs.ts`,
     - GL: Dr project account / Cr the inventory account,
     - `projectLedger`: Material.
   - **Return from project** reverses an issue at its original cost.
5. **Purchase invoices, card charges, reimbursements.** These already carry `projectId`. They gain an optional `projectElementId` (null charges the project itself). For Expense, Capitalize and Contract projects, posting writes `projectLedger` rows and routes to the treatment account. Statistical projects behave exactly as today.

#### Cost treatment → posting account

`resolveProjectPostingAccount(project, projectElement, postingDate)` lives in a shared posting helper used by every posting function. It walks from the element up to the nearest treatment override effective on the posting date, else uses the project's treatment:

| Treatment | Debit account | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Statistical | the account the transaction would post to today | Dimensions only. No `projectLedger` row, so no cost report. This is what existing projects backfill to. |
| Expense | `project.costAccountId` → default `accountDefault.projectExpenseAccount` (new, "R&D / Project Expense") | ASC 730 expensing as incurred. IR&D and B&P are Expense projects with their own account. |
| Capitalize | the `assetAccountId` of the CIP class of the element's CIP asset (nearest `projectElement.fixedAssetId` up the tree, else `project.fixedAssetId`) | Uses the CIP work on main (`fixedAssetCipCost`, `Under Construction`, `capitalizeCip`). Every posting also writes a `fixedAssetCipCost` row against that asset, on the same journal line as its `projectLedger` row. Jobs go through `job.fixedAssetId`, not posting at source (Job, above). After the asset is placed in service, late costs post to the active asset (see *Capital projects*). |
| Contract | `project.costAccountId` → default `accountDefault.projectWorkInProgressAccount` (new, "Project WIP / Contract Costs") | Relieved to COGS by billing or revenue recognition (Phase 4). |
| By Coverage | the account of the charge's coverage (see *Service projects*) | Service projects only. |

Every project-posted journal line carries the **Project** and **Project Element** dimensions. Labor lines also carry Employee, and job-sourced lines carry WorkCenter / Process as today. That requires:
- `dimensionEntityType` gains `'ProjectElement'`, a group-level "Project Element" `dimension` row is seeded and backfilled the way Project was, and `DIMENSION_LABEL_SOURCES` resolves its labels,
- the shared journal-dimension builder (`buildJournalLineDimensionInserts`, from `@carbon/utils`) gains `projectId` and `projectElementId`.

#### Capital projects: Capitalize → construction in progress

A Capitalize project is SAP's investment-measure WBS. Its asset under construction is the CIP asset on main (`fixedAssetClass.isConstructionInProgress`, status `Under Construction`, `fixedAssetCipCost`, `capitalizeCip`; `.ai/specs/implemented/2026-09-22-revenue-recognition-rentals-and-contracts.md`). Projects is the front end the CIP asset lacks: budget, commitments, time, stock issues, more cost sources, several final assets, and expense mixed with capital.

- **One CIP asset per final asset.** Capitalize projects need `project.fixedAssetClassId`, a CIP class. Any element can be marked **Final asset** (`projectElement.isFinalAsset`). Each final-asset element gets its own CIP asset. A cost on an element lands on its nearest final-asset ancestor, or on itself. A cost with no final-asset ancestor, or with no element, lands on the project's own CIP asset (`project.fixedAssetId`). A production line built as machine + building improvements + software is three final-asset branches. Each branch capitalizes one-to-one with the existing `capitalizeCip`, so no splitting logic is needed.
- **The CIP asset is created on release** (SAP's investment profile creates the AuC on release). Releasing a Capitalize project creates its own `fixedAsset` in the project's CIP class and stamps `project.fixedAssetId`. Two actions create one asset per final-asset element. One marks an element as a final asset in a Released project. The other releases a project that has final-asset elements. The action names each asset `"<readable id> <element name>"` and stamps `projectElement.fixedAssetId`. Each asset has status `Under Construction` and the project's location. The action creates the assets in its own write transaction, with the change that triggered them.
- **Cost sources.** Everything that charges the project reaches CIP, each with a `fixedAssetCipCost` row (`sourceType`, `projectElementId`, `journalId`) alongside its `projectLedger` row:
  - Timesheets (`'Timesheet'`).
  - Issue to Project (`'Project Issue'`).
  - PO lines of any type charged to the element (`'Receipt'` / `'Purchase Invoice'`). The existing Fixed Asset line path still works, but a capex buyer no longer has to pick an asset.
  - Card charges and reimbursements (`'Charge'` / `'Reimbursement'`).
  - Jobs via `job.fixedAssetId` (`'Job'`).
  - Manual journals (`'Manual'`).

  The CIP ledger stays the asset's subledger (what gets capitalized); the project ledger keeps the cost type and the element.
- **Expense inside a capital project.** Training, removal of old equipment and repairs are not capitalizable. Put them on a branch with an Expense override; its costs never reach CIP.
- **Place in service.** A *Place in service* action is on each final-asset element, and on the project for all of them. It runs `post-asset-transfer` `capitalizeCip` for that element's CIP asset with these values:
  - target class (the depreciating class),
  - in-service date,
  - Dr target class asset account / Cr CIP for Σ `fixedAssetCipCost`.

  It then moves the element and its descendants to the project's default Completed state. Open PO lines and open jobs on the branch do not block it; their costs arrive as late costs.
- **Late costs after capitalization** (decided by Brad 2026-10-04): a cost can reach a final-asset branch whose CIP asset is already `Active`. That cost does three things:
  1. It posts **Dr the active asset's class asset account** (not CIP).
  2. It appends a `fixedAssetCipCost` row flagged `postCapitalization = true`.
  3. It increases `fixedAsset.acquisitionCost`.

  The addition is depreciated **over the asset's remaining life**. This is SAP's subsequent acquisition: capitalization stays one-way and nothing is reversed.
  - This requires the Straight Line branch of `calculateDepreciation` (`apps/erp/app/modules/accounting/accounting.utils.ts`, today `depreciableBase / usefulLifeMonths` capped at the remaining amount) to compute `(depreciableBase − accumulated) / remainingMonths`. That gives the identical figure for an asset with no additions. It spreads an addition over what is left, where today's formula would spill it past the useful life. The declining-balance branch already uses remaining months. Rounding equivalence is pinned by a test over existing assets.
  - The tax book (`calculateTaxDepreciation`) gets the same change.

#### Plan, budget, actuals: the Costs tab

Per element, rolled up through the tree:

- **Plan**: materials (quantity × the item's current unit cost), plus labor (hours × category cost rate), plus other.
- **Budget**: entered per element, defaults to plan on release. Phase 1 is a single amount; supplement / return documents and availability control come in Phase 5.
- **Committed**: open PO lines.
- **Actual**: Σ `projectLedger.amount`.
- **Remaining**: budget − committed − actual.
- **Estimate at completion**: actual + committed + remaining plan, or a manual override.

Every number drills to its rows: ledger lines with their source documents, the PO lines behind commitments. The project list shows the same five figures at project level.

#### Service projects (delivered with field service Phase A)

A service order (`.ai/specs/2026-10-08-field-service.md`) owns one project of type **Service**. The project's tree is the work breakdown of that one repair: for an engine rebuild, Engine → Cylinders → Boring, and Transmission. A technician logs time and issues parts to any node. So projects and service share one tree, one ledger and one set of charging actions.

**Modules follow job roles; the engine is shared** (Brad 2026-10-08). Projects is the workspace of program managers and engineers. Service is the workspace of service managers, dispatchers and technicians. A Service project is an implementation detail of a service order. No Service user sees the word "project". No Projects user sees a service order.

- **Creation.** Creating a service order creates its Service project in the same transaction:
  - `identifier` = the order's readable id (`SVC000123`), so its elements read `SVC000123-4`;
  - status Released, so charging can start at once;
  - treatment **By Coverage**;
  - the default states;
  - `parentProjectId` = the coverage contract's project, when the contract has one.

  The order builds its tree from its subject serial, the item's make method or a service template (see *Build the tree from a part*). A **service template** is a template project of type Service.
- **Status follows the order.** On Hold → On Hold; Completed → Technically Complete; Closed or Cancelled → Closed. A user never sets a Service project's status directly.
- **Visibility.** A Service project never appears in the Projects module:
  - the project list, the charge number picker, search and templates exclude `projectType = 'Service'` (service templates show only in Service);
  - its RLS rules check `service_*`, not `projects_*`;
  - the Project dimension picker and the Ramp project sync exclude it. Its journal lines still carry the Project and Project Element dimensions, so reports by order work.
- **My Timesheet is personal.** An employee's week shows every entry they own: project hours and service visit hours together. A service entry links to its service order, not to a project page.
- **Coverage on every charge.** Each charge on a Service project carries a coverage: Warranty, Contract, Billable or Capitalized. The field service spec decides it (`resolveCoverage`). The charge stores it on `timesheetEntry`, `projectExpenseEntry` and `projectLedger`, with `coverageReason` when a user overrides it.
- **By Coverage → account.**

  | Coverage | Debit account |
  |---|---|
  | Warranty | `accountDefault.warrantyExpenseAccount` |
  | Contract | the parent project's posting account, else `costOfGoodsSoldAccount` |
  | Billable | `costOfGoodsSoldAccount` |
  | Capitalized | `workInProgressAccount` (Refurbishment; the order's completion sweeps it into the serial) |
- **Expense entries.** `projectExpenseEntry` records an amount with no card charge or invoice, for example mileage. It posts Dr the treatment's account / Cr `goodsReceivedNotInvoicedAccount`, plus a `projectLedger` row of type Other Direct. Standard projects can use it too.
- **Billable items.** Posting a ledger row of a Service project also writes its `projectBillableItem`, in the same transaction:
  - Labor: quantity = hours; unit price = the sale price of `companySettings.serviceLaborItemId`.
  - Material: quantity; unit price = the item's `itemUnitSalePrice.unitSalePrice`.
  - Other Direct: quantity 1; unit price = the amount.
  - Warranty and Contract coverage: unit price 0, kept on the invoice with "Covered by …".
  - Capitalized coverage: no billable item.

  A user can change the unit price of an Unbilled item. Status: Unbilled → On Hold / Written Off → Billed. A reversal of a row whose item is Unbilled writes the item off with the reason "Reversed". A reversal of a Billed item creates a negative Unbilled item.
- **Fixed-price nodes.** `projectElement.fixedPrice` on a node of a Service project bills that node's whole subtree as one invoice line at that price. The billable items of the subtree stay at price 0 with "Included in fixed price". Other nodes bill time and materials.
- **Parent roll-up.** The Costs tab of a project shows its child Service projects (`parentProjectId`), one row per order. So a contract project sees its service cost.

### Phase 2: project stock, transfers, borrow/payback, and project-aware MRP

#### Project stock

- **`project.projectStock`** (boolean, settable only while the project is Draft, as in SAP) turns on striping.
- **Ledger columns.** `itemLedger.projectId`, `costLedger.projectId` and `itemStockQuantities.projectId` (sentinel `''` = common; see Data Model) carry the stripe.
- **What stripes.** Three movements stripe stock with the project:
  - receipts of PO lines charged to the project,
  - outputs of jobs charged to it with *Receive to project stock*,
  - Transfer / Borrow / Payback.
- **Valuation is per stripe:**
  - FIFO layers are drawn within the stripe, which makes the cost charged at issue the project's own cost (CAS 411 consistent).
  - Issue to project, and issue to a job that consumes to the project, charge `projectLedger` at issue. Receiving into project stock is **not** a project cost: it is the project's inventory (valuated), the way Oracle, IFS and Costpoint do it.
  - A cost-plus contract that must bill material at receipt marks the project **Charge at receipt** (Phase 4 billing reads this). In that case the receipt also writes a `projectLedger` row, and the later issue does not.
- **Issue rules.** These are enforced by the pickers, the pick-list generator and the posting functions:
  - project demand (a job charged to P, a plan-line material of P) may draw on P's stock or on common stock,
  - common demand may draw only on common stock,
  - another project's stock requires a Transfer or Borrow first.
- **Visibility.** Stock screens and `get_inventory_quantities` gain a project column and filter. The item's Inventory tab groups quantity by owner.

#### Transfer, Borrow, Payback

`projectStockTransfer` is a header plus lines.

- **Header**: type, from owner, to owner, reason, payback due date (Borrow only), status Draft → Approved → Posted.
- **Lines**: item, quantity, storage unit, lot/serial.
- **Approval**: a user with `update: projects` approves. The transfer records who approved it and when. The MMAS requires documented and consistent transfers.

| Type | Stock | Cost | Ledger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Transfer** | from → to | moves at the FIFO cost of the source layers | `itemLedger` Transfer pair with document type **Project Transfer**. `costLedger` layers re-striped at cost. No GL when both owners are inventory (same account). |
| **Borrow** | lender → borrower | **stays with the lender**. The borrower receives the units at the lender's layer cost as a *borrowed* layer (`costLedger.projectBorrowId`). If the borrower consumes them before payback, its `projectLedger` is charged at that cost, and payback then replaces the lender's stock. | Creates an open `projectBorrow` (quantity owed, due date) |
| **Payback** | borrower → lender, like-for-like item | The lender receives the borrower's current layer cost. The difference to the borrowed cost goes to the **borrower's** `projectLedger` as *Payback Variance* (Oracle EBS rule) and to GL `accountDefault.inventoryAdjustmentVarianceAccount`. | Closes the borrow FIFO-matched. A partial payback reduces the open quantity. |

**MRP sees an open Borrow** as firm demand on the borrower's segment and firm supply on the lender's. Both are dated on the payback date (Oracle EBS). MRP never cancels or reschedules either. Overdue borrows show an exception on the project and in the planning worklist.

**Government-furnished material.** `project.governmentFurnished` stock can never be transferred, borrowed or issued to a project whose customer differs or to common stock (MMAS standard 9). This is enforced on Approve.

#### MRP: a project-aware netting segment

This builds on the planning-action engine on main. The netting key gains a fourth component, **`planningSegmentId`**:
- `''`: common stock and common demand (all existing rows),
- a **planning group id**: for a project in a group (Phase 3),
- a **project id**: for a project with project stock that is not in a group.

Projects without project stock net in common, exactly as today.

- **Demand.**
  - Plan-line materials of elements in an Unstarted or Started state become a new demand source **Project Material**. These are Pull from Inventory lines, with open quantity = required − issued. `demandSourceType` and `demandForecastSourceType` gain `'Project Material'`, and a new view `openProjectMaterialLines` feeds it.
  - Job materials of jobs charged to a project-stock project carry the project's segment.
  - Sales order lines of a Contract project's sales order carry it too (Phase 4 links orders to projects).
- **Supply.** Open PO lines and jobs charged to the project, plus open Borrows/Paybacks, carry the segment.
- **On-hand.** `itemStockQuantities` grouped by segment.
- **BOM explosion.** Dependent demand inherits the parent's segment.
- **Netting sequence per bucket**, the Oracle model done in our engine:
  1. **Own segment.** The segment's on-hand plus its supply.
  2. **Common surplus.** Only for items with `itemPlanning.allowCommonSupplyForProjects` (default true): common on-hand left over after common demand within the planning horizon (IFS "true surplus"). When MRP uses it, it emits a **Transfer** planning action (common → project) rather than silently netting. The transfer is what makes the stock the project's.
  3. **Plan new supply** in the segment. Planned Order/Make actions are stamped with the segment, and **Apply** creates the PO line or job charged to that project or group.
- **Planning actions.** `planningAction` gains `planningSegmentId`, part of the natural key, plus `projectStockTransferId` for applied transfer actions. Two new action types: **Transfer** and **Borrow** (Phase 3). The responsible-employee ladder gains a top rung for the project manager on segmented rows.
- **Reorder policy and forecasts stay common-only.** Project segments are lot-for-lot with no reorder point or safety stock. `demandProjection` and forecast consumption apply only to segment `''`. Supersession redirects apply only to segment `''` in v1 (see Risks).

**This is where Carbon beats SAP.** SAP's MRP plans the grouping segment but never decides contract ownership. PP/DS drops grouping entirely, and loans are invisible to planning. Carbon's engine does four things:
- It nets each project's own stock.
- It proposes the transfers that make common stock the project's.
- It plans striped supply that Apply creates already charged.
- It plans borrows as firm supply and demand.

### Phase 3: planning groups, live pegging, distribution at the physical event

#### Grouping

- **Planning group.** `planningGroup` holds name, location, excess rule (*Common stock* | *Exception project*, plus `exceptionProjectId`) and priority rule (*Earliest need first* | *Project priority*).
- **Membership.** `project.planningGroupId` sets it, and every member must have project stock. All members' demand nets in segment = group id.
- **Grouped supply.** A PO line or job created for the group carries `planningGroupId` with no single `projectId`. Its cost is common inventory until distribution. Lockheed warns that proliferating groups lets programs hoard, so membership is visible on the group page.

#### Live pegging (in the MRP run)

After netting the group segment for an item, the engine allocates the segment's **supply** to its **member demands**:
- Supply: group stock, grouped open supply, and planned supply.
- Allocation is in the group's priority order: need date, then project priority, then demand id. Stock comes first, then supply by arrival date. This is the PMMO "MRP logic", computed in-process rather than as a separate job.
- Pegs propagate down the BOM: a grouped job's component demand inherits its pegs proportionally. So a grouped optics subassembly feeding three contracts pegs its lens demand to the same three contracts.

The run diffs the computed pegs against the current `projectPeg` rows and writes only the changes. A changed allocation **supersedes** the old row (`supersededAt`, `supersededByRunId`) and inserts the new one. So pegging history is complete, and any period's pegging can be reconstructed. That is the DCAA trail Lockheed had to custom-build.

| Peg state | Set when | Re-peg allowed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft** | supply is open (PO line not received, job not completed) | Yes, by MRP on any run. Moves only **commitments**, never cost. |
| **Hard** | the supply is received or completed, at which point it is distributed | No. Ownership is now physical stock. Moving it is a Transfer or Borrow. |
| **Manual** | a planner overrides an allocation, with a reason | MRP keeps it unless the demand disappears, which raises an exception and does not silently move it. Same rule as the "fixing rule". |

#### Distribution at the physical event

A grouped PO line can be **received**, or a grouped job's output can be **completed to stock**. Then the posting function reads the current pegs for that supply and **splits the receipt**:
- one `itemLedger` / `costLedger` row per pegged project, striped with that project, quantity = pegged quantity at the receipt's unit cost,
- the remainder handled by the group's excess rule: common stock, or the exception project's stock.

Those pegs become **Hard**. Later costs follow the same split:
- **Purchase price variance** at invoice is spread over the hard pegs in proportion (SAP distributes down payments and invoice differences the same way). On project stock still on hand it revalues the stripe's layer; on stock already issued it lands in each project's `projectLedger` as Material Variance.
- **Supplier down payments** on a grouped PO (Phase 4 billing reads these) are spread the same way.

Commitments: `projectCommitment` reads each soft peg's share of open grouped supply, so a project's committed cost includes its share of the group's open PO.

**Scrap and loss** on a grouped job before completion are spread proportionally over the job's pegs (PMMO history-proportional rule). Rework recursion and under-pegs go to the exception project, or to common stock if none is set.

In the worked example: one PO for 60 × ST-050. The run pegs 20 to P-101, 30 to P-102 and 10 to P-103 (soft). At receipt, three striped rows are written and the pegs turn hard. Each contract's lens cost is exactly its share, with no month-end run and no possibility of it shifting next week.

#### MRP-proposed Transfers and Borrows

Inside a group, a member's stock beyond its own demand within the horizon is **group excess**. When a member is short and a sibling has excess, MRP emits:
- a **Transfer** action if the sibling's excess is permanent (no own demand within the horizon), or
- a **Borrow** action, with a payback date at the sibling's next need date minus lead time, if the sibling needs it back.

Apply creates the Draft `projectStockTransfer` for approval. Nothing moves between contracts without a document and an approver.

### Phase 4: billing (T&M and Cost Plus) and revenue (detailed in `.ai/specs/2026-10-09-project-billing.md`)

> The detailed spec supersedes this outline where they differ:
> - the line gets `billingMethod` (Fixed, Time & Materials, Cost Plus), not a `kind`;
> - the revenue methods are `'Right to Invoice'` and `'Percent Complete'`;
> - WIP relief runs in the monthly recognition run.

- **Billing methods.** Contract lines (`customerContractLine.billingMethod`) gain **Time & Materials** and **Cost Plus**, each tied to a project's **billing element** (`customerContractLine.projectElementId`). It bills that element's subtree.
- **Billable items** (`projectBillableItem`, created with Service projects). Phase 4 also generates them from `projectLedger` rows of Contract projects under a billing element, on posting or by a backfill action:
  - **T&M labor**: hours × the bill rate of the entry's labor category on the contract line. Rate lookup is most-specific-wins: contract line × employee → contract line × labor category → company default (Costpoint rate sequence). Timesheets gain an optional *project labor category* when the contract's titles differ from the HR category.
  - **T&M materials and other direct**: cost × (1 + material handling %), set on the contract line.
  - **Cost Plus**: cost (plus burden at provisional rates once Phase 5 exists) × (1 + fee %). The fee is computed on allowable cost only; `projectLedger.unallowable` rows are never billable.
- **Review.** Items move Unbilled → On Hold / Written Off → Billed. *Projects → Billing* lists items by contract line, with bulk hold, release and write-off. This is SAP DP91's bill / postpone / reject.
- **Funding.** `customerContractLine.fundedAmount` and `ceilingAmount`. The invoicing source stops selecting items whose billing would exceed funded (Oracle hard limit). Notifications fire at 75% and 85% of funded (FAR 52.232-20/-22).
- **Invoicing.** Billable items are a third **source** of the shared recurring-invoicing layer. The `recurring-billing` job drafts one invoice per contract per billing period from Unbilled items up to the period end. It groups the invoice lines by labor category / cost type.
- **Revenue and WIP relief.** These ride the contracts spec's revenue methods. The recognition run generates each month's `customerContractRevenue` row for `'As Invoiced'` / `'Percent Complete'` lines. Invoice posting and the run post them unchanged.
  - **T&M**: revenue as billed (right-to-invoice expedient). On invoice posting, the billed items' cost is relieved Dr COGS / Cr Project WIP.
  - **Cost Plus** and fixed-price project lines: cost-to-cost percent of completion in the revenue recognition run, per `.ai/specs/2026-07-04-revenue-recognition.md`, using `projectLedger` actuals versus estimate at completion. It posts contract asset / liability and relieves Project WIP to COGS in proportion. A loss on the estimate at completion is provided in full.
- **Milestone billing**: billing plan lines released when a project milestone completes. This is a later layer of the same source.

### Phase 5: government contracting and control (detailed in `.ai/specs/2026-10-09-government-contracting.md`)

> The detailed spec supersedes this outline. It phases the work by SF 1408 need: 5a adequate system, 5b indirect rates, 5c control and reporting, 5d R&D tax.

- **Indirect rate pools** (fringe → overhead → material handling → G&A). Each pool is cost ÷ base, with provisional, target and actual rate sets per fiscal year. Burden is computed into `projectLedger` as Burden rows (memo, or GL against Applied accounts). Re-burden and true-up run when actual rates are set (Costpoint / FAR 52.216-7).
- **DCAA timesheet controls**:
  - daily entry window,
  - total time accounting reconciled to timecards (warn → block),
  - certification on submit,
  - correction reason and approver on every change,
  - charge restrictions per employee (Costpoint charge trees).
- **Cost accounts**:
  - `projectLedger.unallowable` and an `account.unallowable` default (FAR 31.201-6),
  - CLIN mapping on contract lines to elements, with cost and billing by CLIN,
  - pre-production flags.
- **Budget control.** Supplement / return / transfer documents. Availability control (warn / error at % of budget) runs on PO line save, timesheet submit and issue (SAP AVC).
- **Earned value.** Control accounts (WBS × organization) and work packages with an earned-value technique. BCWS / BCWP / ACWP / CPI / SPI per period from `projectLedger`, and an IPMDAR export. Required at ≥ $50M under DoD deviation 2026-O0011.
- **R&D tax tags**:
  - domestic / foreign location (§174A),
  - QRE category and business component (Form 6765 Section G, mandatory for tax years beginning after 2025),
  - a QRE report from `projectLedger` plus timesheets.

### Design Decisions

| # | Decision | Choice | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | One "project" concept | **Grow the existing `project` table** (keep `id('prj')`, the Project dimension and Ramp sync) into the header. Backfill existing rows as *Statistical* + Released, with an identifier derived from the name. | Avoids two project masters. The accounting tag and the managed project are one record in SAP, Oracle and NetSuite. |
| 2 | Tree | One `projectElement` table, self-referencing, with a **type**. Readable id = project identifier + per-project sequence (`SNS-12`). It never changes when the element moves. No root element: a top-level element has no parent, and a charge with no element charges the project. | Plane's work-item ids and Flow's typed items. A sequence id is stable without SAP's coding mask or a locked hand-typed code. |
| 3 | Where charging can happen | Any element flagged chargeable (default by type; Folder and Parameter are off), in an Unstarted or Started state. Parents roll up their own and descendants' rows. | SAP allows non-leaf account assignment. The state group replaces a separate element status. |
| 4 | Project ↔ job | A job **carries** a charge number. Production never runs "inside" the project. | Epicor practitioners' rule; SAP orders assigned to WBS. Keeps scheduling, MES and costing unchanged. |
| 5 | Settlement | **Cost treatment at source**: the posting account is resolved at transaction time from the project/branch treatment, effective-dated. No period-end settlement run. ## Acceptance Criteria

Phase 1

- [ ] **Backfill.** An existing flat project shows as *Statistical*, Released, with an identifier and the seeded states. A purchase invoice G/L line coded to it posts exactly as before (same account, Project dimension) and writes no `projectLedger` row.
- [ ] **Permission backfill.** A user with `production_view` and `production_update` but no other production rights gets `projects_view` and `projects_update` only. An employee type with Production permissions gets the same Projects permissions. A user without production permissions can no longer edit the flat project list.
- [ ] **Workspace.** A user creates project *P-900* with identifier `SNS` (Expense, account "R&D Expense") and releases it. They add these elements:
  - the System `SNS-1`,
  - the Requirement `SNS-4` under it (target `≤ 120 g`, method Inspection),
  - the Parameter `SNS-7` (Sum of two child Parameters of 50 g and 65 g),
  - the Task `SNS-12`,
  - the Test Case `SNS-15`.

  Moving `SNS-12` under another parent keeps its readable id.
- [ ] **Verification.** `SNS-4` with `actualParameterId` = `SNS-7` shows Pass at 115 g. Changing a child to 75 g makes it Fail. Linking `SNS-15` with *verifies* and setting its verdict to Fail keeps `SNS-4` at Fail. The link from `SNS-15` turns suspect when `SNS-4`'s target changes, until a user clears it.
- [ ] **Allocation.** A child Parameter with allocation 30 % under a 2,000 kg parent shows "≤ 600 kg (30.0 % of 2,000 kg)". It fails at 610 kg.
- [ ] **Constraint (Phase 1b).** Parameters `SNS-20 Power supply` = 50 W and `SNS-21 Power load` = 42 W exist. `SNS-22 Power margin` has the formula `= SNS-20 - SNS-21` and shows 8 W. Requirement `SNS-23` (category Design Constraint, target `≥ 0 W`, actual `SNS-22`) shows Pass. Changing `SNS-21` to 55 W makes `SNS-22` −5 W and `SNS-23` Fail at once. A formula that refers to itself shows an error.
- [ ] **Contingency (Phase 1b).** `SNS-7` at 115 g with contingency 10 % has a checked value of 126.5 g, so `SNS-4` (`≤ 120 g`) shows Fail.
- [ ] **Item parameter (Phase 1b).** `SNS-1` links item `ST-100`, which has the numeric custom field "Mass (g)" = 118. A child Parameter with source Item and that field shows 118 g.
- [ ] **Build from part (Phase 1b).**
  - Item `ST-100` has a make method with two Make to Order sub-assemblies and 30 bought parts. **Build from part** on it at depth 2 creates 2 System elements linked to the sub-assemblies. It creates no element for the bought parts.
  - On a serial Carbon built, it links each element to the serial of the component that the build consumed.
- [ ] **Direct time and materials.** From `SNS-12`'s page, a user logs 6 h and issues 4 lenses without leaving the page. With `timesheetApprovalRequired` off, the time posts on submit. The element moves from Todo to In Progress on the first charge. The Time & materials section and the Costs tab show both rows.
- [ ] **State gates.** A charge on an element in a Backlog or Cancelled state is refused. A late invoice for an open PO line on a Completed element posts.
- [ ] **Layouts.** The same elements show in six layouts:
  - List (grouped by state, with indented children),
  - Board,
  - Table (budget, actual and hours columns editable),
  - Tree (with trace links),
  - Timeline (with *precedes* arrows),
  - Reader.

  A saved view restores its layout, grouping and filters.
- [ ] **Baseline (Phase 1c).** A user takes a baseline before a target change and compares it with the current plan. The compare lists `SNS-4` as modified with the old and new target.
- [ ] **Timesheet posting.** `timesheetApprovalRequired` is on. For Tuesday, an engineer enters 6 h on `SNS-12` and 2 h on a Contract project element, then submits. After manager approval:
  - two journals post:
    - Dr R&D Expense / Cr Labor Absorption = 6 × the Engineer II cost rate,
    - Dr Project WIP / Cr Labor Absorption = 2 × the rate,
  - each journal carries Project, Project Element and Employee dimensions,
  - two `projectLedger` Labor rows exist.
- [ ] **Corrections.** Correcting a posted timesheet entry requires a reason. It creates a reversal and a replacement entry; the original row is unchanged.
- [ ] **Consume-to-project job.**
  - A job is charged to `SNS-12` with Consume to project on. Issuing 3 parts posts Dr R&D Expense / Cr Raw Materials at FIFO cost (no WIP line). Labor of 2 h posts Dr R&D Expense / Cr Labor Absorption.
  - Completing the job writes no `itemLedger` receipt.
  - Closing it posts no variance journal.
  - The Costs tab shows Material and Labor actuals on `1.2`.
- [ ] **Non-inventory PO line.** A PO line of type Service charged to `SNS-1` shows as committed on `SNS-1` until invoiced. The invoice debits R&D Expense, not the line's account, and the commitment drops by the invoiced amount.
- [ ] **Inventory PO line, no project stock.** A Part PO line charged to a project without project stock is consumed at receipt. The receipt posts Dr project account / Cr GRNI. It writes a Positive Adjmt. + Negative Adjmt. `itemLedger` pair, and on-hand stays unchanged.
- [ ] **Issue to project.** Issue 5 units to `SNS-12`, then return 2. The project's net Material actual = 3 × the original FIFO cost. On-hand and `costLedger` agree.
- [ ] **Status gates.**
  - On Hold: a new timesheet line is refused, and receiving an already-open charged PO line still posts.
  - Technically Complete: refuses new PO lines and still accepts the open line's invoice.
  - Closed: refused while an open PO line exists.
- [ ] **Plan versus actual roll-up.** A parent element's plan / committed / actual equal the sum of its own rows plus its descendants'. The project row matches the sum of every element plus the charges with no element.
- [ ] **Capital project.**
  - Project *P-500* (Capitalize, CIP class) with identifier `CAP`, final-asset elements `CAP-1 Machine` and `CAP-2 Software`, plus an Expense-override element `CAP-3 Training`. Releasing it creates the project's own CIP asset and two element assets, all `Under Construction`. The two element assets are stamped on `CAP-1` and `CAP-2`.
  - Costs land as follows:
    - 10 h of timesheet on `CAP-1` posts Dr CIP / Cr Labor Absorption. The same journal line has a `fixedAssetCipCost` row (`'Timesheet'`, `projectElementId` = `CAP-1`) and a `projectLedger` row.
    - A Service PO line charged to `CAP-2` lands in CIP on invoice.
    - 4 h on `CAP-3` posts to the expense account and never reaches CIP.
    - A job charged to `CAP-1` sets `job.fixedAssetId` to `CAP-1`'s asset and sweeps its WIP to CIP at completion. The project ledger shows its material and labor as they post.
  - *Place in service* on `CAP-1` capitalizes Σ its CIP rows into the target class, makes the asset `Active` and moves `CAP-1` to the Done state.
  - A supplier invoice for `CAP-1` arriving afterwards debits the active asset's account, adds a `postCapitalization` CIP row and raises `acquisitionCost`. The next depreciation run spreads it over the remaining months.
- [ ] **Depreciation basis.** Take every existing Straight Line asset with no additions. For each one, the remaining-life formula produces the same monthly amount as today's (within rounding at the currency's decimals).
- [ ] **Missing account default.** The company has no `projectExpenseAccount` default and the project has no account. Then posting to an Expense project fails with an error that names the missing default. Nothing posts.

Phase 2

- [ ] **Stock rules.** With project stock on, receiving a Part PO line charged to P-101 increases P-101's stock, not common stock. A job for P-102 cannot pick it. A job for P-101 can pick both P-101 and common stock.
- [ ] **Borrow and payback.**
  - An approved Borrow of 5 from P-101 to P-102 (payback due on a date) moves the stock and leaves the cost on P-101. MRP shows it as firm demand on P-102 and firm supply on P-101 on that date.
  - A Payback at a different unit cost charges the difference to P-102 as Payback Variance.
- [ ] **Segmented MRP.** With P-101 demand 20, P-101 stock 5, common surplus 8 and `allowCommonSupplyForProjects` on:
  - MRP emits Transfer 8 (common → P-101) and Order 7 in P-101's segment,
  - applying the Order creates a PO line charged to P-101,
  - existing common planning rows are unchanged.
- [ ] **Government-furnished material.** Transferring stock of a government-furnished project to a commercial project is refused on Approve.

Phase 3

- [ ] **Grouped demand.** P-101/102/103 are in group *Star Tracker*, with ST-050 demands 20 / 30 / 10 and no stock. MRP emits one Order of 60 in the group segment. Applying it creates one PO line with `planningGroupId`, and the run writes three soft pegs of 20 / 30 / 10.
- [ ] **Re-pegging before receipt.** P-103's demand drops to 5 before receipt. The next run supersedes its peg (history kept) with a 5 peg. Excess 5 follows the group rule. Commitments move; no cost moves.
- [ ] **Distribution at receipt.** Receiving the 60 writes striped `itemLedger` rows at the receipt cost. The rows follow the current pegs (20 / 30 / 5 plus 5 excess). The pegs become Hard, and later MRP runs never change them.
- [ ] **Price variance spread.** An invoice price variance on the grouped line is spread over the hard pegs in proportion. Each project's share either revalues its stock or lands as Material Variance in its ledger.
- [ ] **Sibling excess.** A member short by 4 while a sibling has 6 excess and no demand within the horizon gets a Transfer action. If the sibling needs it in 3 weeks, it gets a Borrow action with a payback date.
- [ ] **Pegging history.** The Pegging tab reconstructs any past date's allocations from the versioned rows.
