# How Manufacturing ERPs Model Warranty Tracking & Repair Processing

**Date:** 2026-08-20
**Question:** How do manufacturing ERPs define and register product warranties, run repair/depot-repair documents (in-house repair and send-to-OEM repair-and-return), decide warranty-free vs billable, and handle ownership/costing of customer units in the shop — to inform the design of a Carbon Repairs module on top of the new returns module?

Sources are official vendor documentation unless flagged. Epicor's Kinetic help is login-gated; Epicor claims come from Epicor-authored documents republished by licensees (the 10.0.700 *Application User Guide* PDFs mirrored by ssh.com.mx, a Service Management brochure mirrored on ziftsolutions) and Epicor's own Knowledge On Demand video pages, each flagged. One Infor page (Automatic Unit/Warranty Creation) redirects when fetched directly; its claims are reconstructed from search excerpts of the same docs.infor.com page and flagged. Anything that couldn't be verified against a primary page is marked **unverified**.

## Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

**The warranty carrier is the service item — equipment master data, not inventory.** "The term 'service item' refers to equipment or items that require service"; a service item card records the specific unit installed at a specific customer. Service items are created manually, from a service order via the **Create Service Item** action, or — the key automation — on shipment: "When you ship items by posting either sales orders or sales invoices, the shipped items are automatically registered as service items … The items must belong to a service item group with the **Create Service Item** check box selected. If the items have serial numbers registered in the Item Tracking Lines page, this information is copied automatically to the **Serial No.** field on the service item card" ([Create Service Items](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-how-to-create-service-items)). Exploded BOM components become **service item components** of the parent service item (same page). Service Management requires the **Premium** experience ([Setting up Service Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-setup-service)).

**Warranty terms = duration + a parts/labor split, defaulted from setup.** **Service Mgt. Setup** holds "default settings for response times and warranties. These include a default response time for starting service, warranty discount percentages for parts and labor, and how long warranties are valid for" ([Configure Service Processes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-setup-service-processes)). The per-unit registration carries the resolved dates and percentages: the **Service Item Line** table (5901) has **Warranty** ("Specifies that warranty on either parts or labor exists for this item"), **Warranty Starting/Ending Date (Parts)**, **Warranty Starting/Ending Date (Labor)**, **Warranty % (Parts)**, **Warranty % (Labor)**, and a `CheckWarranty(Date)` procedure ([Service Item Line table reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/application/base-application/table/microsoft.service.document.service-item-line)). Parts and labor coverage are *separate date ranges and separate discount percentages* on the same unit.

**Start trigger = posting date of the shipping document.** From the NAV-era field reference (still the live mechanic): "If the program creates a service item automatically when you post a sales order, the program inserts the default warranty for the service item, starting from the posting date of the sales order. If the program creates a service item automatically when you replace a service item on a service line, the program inserts the default warranty … starting from the posting date of the corresponding service order" ([Warranty Starting Date (Labor) field, NAV 2016 archive](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dynamicsnav-2016/hh178085(v=nav.90))). Note the second half: a **replacement performed during service starts a fresh warranty**. Out-of-warranty visibility is a report, not a block: **Service Items Out of Warranty** (report 5937) ([report reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-ca/dynamics365/business-central/reports/report-5937)); no hard warranty-expiry block on service order creation is documented.

**Repair document anatomy: service order → service item lines → service lines.** A service order has header + **service item lines** (one per unit being serviced) + **service lines** registered per service item on the **Service Item Worksheet**, with **Type** = `<Blank>` (standard text), **Item** (spare parts), **Resource** (labor hours), **Cost** (fees; an **Insert Starting Fee** action adds a Cost line). Fault code + resolution code are registered per service item from **Fault/Resol. Codes Relationships**. Replacing a component of a serviced unit prompts **Replace Component / New Component / Ignore** — Replace moves the old component to the service item's replaced-components list ([Work on Service Tasks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-how-to-work-on-service-tasks)).

**Lifecycle: repair statuses roll up to order status.** At least nine user-defined **Repair Status** options are required; each links to one of four fixed **Service Order Status** values — **Pending, In Process, Finished, On Hold** — with a priority (**High / Medium High / Medium Low / Low**): "When you change the repair status of a service item in a service order, the service order status is updated … If the service items are linked to two or more service order status options, the service order status with the highest priority is selected." Per repair status: **Posting Allowed** gates posting, and **Pending/In Process/Finished/On Hold Status Allowed** flags gate manual order-status changes ([Set Up Statuses for Service Orders and Repairs](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-order-repair-status)).

**Quoting and posting.** **Service Quotes** are "drafts of service orders" converted via Make Order; posting options on a service order are **Ship / Invoice / Ship and Invoice / Ship and Consume** (Consume = use parts without billing), restrictable per user via the **Service Invoice Posting Policy** (Allowed/Prohibited/Mandatory) ([Deliver Service](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-deliver-service), [Configure Service Processes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-setup-service-processes)).

**Warranty billing = automatic line discount, excludable per line.** On service lines of type Item or Resource the warranty discount from the service item line applies; the NAV-era **Service Line** table documents the override: **Exclude Warranty** "specifies that the warranty discount is excluded on the current service line" (only for Item/Resource types); "if the Fault Reason Code field for the service line excludes warranty discount … the program automatically inserts a check mark in the Exclude Warranty field"; checking Exclude Warranty clears the line's **Warranty** field and vice versa ([Service Line table, NAV 2016 archive](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dynamicsnav-2016/hh897175(v=nav.90)) — NAV-era reference; current BC does not enumerate these hooks on a dedicated page). So warranty ≠ document-level free/paid: it's a per-line discount % (typically 100) on covered cost classes within their own date windows.

**Loaners are first-class.** Loaner cards (**No., Description, Serial No., Unit of Measure**, own number series on Service Mgt. Setup) are lent and received back on service orders ([Set Up a Loaner Program](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/service-how-setup-loaner-program)).

**Ownership & costing.** Service items are master data — the serviced unit never enters inventory on a service order; only consumed parts move (item ledger via Ship/Consume). For depot flows through a **sales return order**, the return reason hooks apply: **Default Location Code** ("the returned items belong to the customer and should not affect the availability and value of your inventory") and **Inventory Value Zero** — "the item can then enter into inventory to be repaired, but its value will be set to zero" ([Return Reasons window, NAV archive](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/previous-versions/dynamicsnav-2015/hh173680(v=nav.80))). No warranty accrual/reserve mechanics are documented.

**Three-party chain: Create Return-Related Documents.** On a sales return order: "You can have replacement sales orders, purchase return orders, and replacement purchase orders created automatically during the sales return process. This is useful, for example, in situations where you want to handle items with warranties provided by vendors." The dialog takes a **Vendor No.** plus three checkboxes — **Create Purch. Ret. Order** ("if a returned item must be returned to the vendor"), **Create Purchase Order** ("if a returned item must be ordered from the vendor"), **Create Sales Order** (customer replacement) ([Process sales return orders](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/business-central/sales-how-process-sales-returns-orders)). Semantics worth noting: this models **replace-from-vendor**, not same-unit-back — the purchase return ships the defective unit out of inventory and the replacement PO receives *new* stock. BC documents no at-vendor status for the unit and no linked receive-the-same-repaired-unit-back flow.

## SAP Business One

**Warranty template → automatic equipment card + warranty contract on sale.** Contract templates are set up under *Administration → Setup → Service* and assigned to an item master's **Warranty Template** field. Prerequisites in *General Settings → Inventory*: serial numbers unique by company and automatic equipment card creation enabled. Then, on posting an **A/R Invoice or Delivery for a serialized item**, the system automatically creates a **Customer Equipment Card** and a **service contract of the Warranty type** for the customer+serial combination; the template must be of the **Serial Number** contract type ([SAP Learning: Implementing the Service Process in SAP Business One](https://learning.sap.com/courses/managing-logistics-in-sap-business-one/implementing-the-service-process-in-sap-business-one); [SAP Learning: Discovering the Service Process, Web Client](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-business-one-implementation/discovering-the-service-process-in-sap-business-one-web-client-1) — "with the appropriate settings when a phone is sold, the system automatically creates an equipment card and a service contract for the combination of the customer and the serialized item").

**Equipment card = per-serial install base.** It records serial, customer, item, address, "history for the item (including service calls, contracts, sales data, and transactions)", with status options **Active, Loaned, Returned, Terminated, In Lab** ([Implementing the Service Process](https://learning.sap.com/courses/managing-logistics-in-sap-business-one/implementing-the-service-process-in-sap-business-one)). **In Lab** is the closest any system here comes to a documented "unit is in the repair shop" state on the registration record.

**Contract anatomy.** Three contract types — **Serial Number** (equipment-specific), **Customer**, **Item Group**. The **Coverage** tab defines availability windows and which cost classes are covered: **Parts, Labor, Travel**; the General tab carries response/resolution time SLAs that compute "Response by"/"Resolution by" on service calls (same course).

**Repair document = service call.** Tabs: General (origin, problem type, call type, assignee, deadlines), Remarks, **Activities** ("record all steps concerning the analysis and the solution as activities"), Solutions (knowledge base), **Related Documents / Expenses**, Resolution, Scheduling. A call "cannot [be] set … to Closed if you have not attached a solution or entered a resolution" (same course). Parts/labor/travel actuals attach through the Expenses window, which creates real logistics/AR documents from the call — for customers the default document types are sales quotations, sales orders, **deliveries, returns**, A/R invoices, A/R credit memos, and stock **transfers to/from technicians** ([Implementing the Service Process](https://learning.sap.com/courses/managing-logistics-in-sap-business-one/implementing-the-service-process-in-sap-business-one); document-type list corroborated by a consultant mirror of the B1 help — flagged secondary). So the "repaired unit ships back" document is an ordinary Delivery created from the service call, and the customer's unit comes in as a Return row there.

**Warranty billing decision.** "The system automatically checks for warranty and contract coverage for the customer and the item… Any service costs not covered by contract or warranty are billed to the customer" (same course). Validity check on call creation is automatic ("the service contract is valid and … the call is within the coverage time" — Web Client course); no hard block is documented.

**Ownership & costing.** Equipment cards are master data, not stock; the unit in the shop is only represented by the card's status. No warranty GL/accrual mechanics documented. **Three-party chain:** nothing documented — no vendor-repair loop from a service call; the purchasing mirror is Goods Returns/A-P Credit Memo (see the RMA research), with no linked receive-back.

## Oracle NetSuite

**Warranty is a SuiteApp, not core.** The **Warranty and Repairs Management** SuiteApp (Bundle 51007) "lets you register item warranties and handle warranty claims"; it requires **Advanced Inventory** to install and **Assemblies/Work Orders** for repairs, and "only supports Inventory and Assembly Items" ([SuiteApp overview](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_3938292477.html), [Installing](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/bridgehead_4389357787.html), [FAQ](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_3933911525.html)). Core NetSuite has no native installed-base/warranty object; core's only warranty artifact is that RAs can be created "for warranty claims" via this SuiteApp ([Creating Return Authorizations for Warranty Claims](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4391869751.html)).

**Registration.** Warranty tracking is enabled per item; registrations are created from invoices (with an **Automatically Register Warranty on Invoice** preference; bulk-processed/billing-schedule invoices auto-register too), manually, or programmatically; "for serialized items, one registration per unit" and quantity-based for lot/regular items ([Warranty Registration](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3938315832.html)). Start-date basis is a preference: **Calculate Warranty Start Date From** = **Sales Order Date** (default) or **Invoice Date**; other preferences include **Auto Generate Registration No.**, **Close RMA Upon SO Creation**, and **Ship Replacement in Advance** ("create a sales order from a return authorization before the item receipt is created") ([Setting Warranty Preferences](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4389382197.html)). A warranty term can be overridden per registration line ([same page family](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3938292683.html)). The FAQ states CSV import of registrations is *not* supported in the current version, while the chapter overview mentions scheduled-script import — contradictory; treat CSV import as **unverified**.

**Claims validate against the registration — a hard block.** Claims are created from **active, unexpired** registrations only; expired warranty ⇒ the claim cannot be entered ("only valid claims can be entered and processed") ([Warranty Claims](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3938319263.html)). Claim action drives the downstream document: **Refund → Credit Memo** (cash refunds unsupported), **Replacement → Sales Order**, **Repair → Work Order**; the physical loop for all three is RA → item receipt ([Warranty Claims](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_3938319263.html), [FAQ](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/chapter_3933911525.html)).

**The repair is a work order that builds a "repaired item".** "The system can process repairs only for assembly items" (FAQ). Setup: "For assembly items, create a repaired item to use for claim processing. Also, add the assembly item as a component on the repaired item record" — a distinct item using the **Warranty Assembly Item Form** with **Special Work Order Item** checked; it then "appears in the **Repaired Item** field on the assembly item record" ([Creating Repaired Items](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/section_4389393717.html)). Flow: from the RA click **Create SO**; on the sales order check the **Create WO** column for the repaired item; after approval the work order link appears and the WO is tracked on the claim's Related Records; the sales order then ships the repaired item back to the customer ([Creating a Sales Order for a Repair Claim](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/netsuite/ns-online-help/bridgehead_3938327650.html)). Consequence: the returned unit is *consumed as a component* and a **new item/new identity** ships back — NetSuite fakes same-unit-return through a BOM. Partial repairs work "only in terms of quantity" on the same invoice line; loaners are explicitly unsupported; charging for a warranty at sale time is "a separate line item … on the sales order form" (FAQ).

**Vendor side.** The Vendor Return Authorization flow is enter → approve → **ship** → **credit** (RMA research); no receive-the-repaired-unit-back-from-vendor flow and no vendor-warranty claim object is documented — absence.

## Odoo (v18)

**No warranty model in the Repairs workflow.** The Repairs app has no warranty term, no registration, and no coverage object linked to repairs; its single warranty artifact is a checkbox on the repair order: **Under Warranty** — "if this box is checked, the sale price for all products from the repair order are set to zero" and the customer is not charged for parts ([Process repair orders](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/repairs/repair_orders.html), [Helpdesk After-Sales](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/services/helpdesk/advanced/after_sales.html)). Nothing computes whether the flag *should* be checked; that's the user's judgment. (The separate Maintenance app's equipment records do carry an informational **Warranty Expiration Date** field ([Add new equipment](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/maintenance/add_new_equipment.html)), but nothing connects it to repair orders or coverage decisions.) (A crowded field of third-party "warranty registration & claim" apps on the Odoo app store fills the gap — corroborating the core absence.)

**Repair order anatomy.** Fields: **Customer**, **Product to Repair**, quantity/UoM, **Return** ("select the return order from which the product to be repaired comes from"), **Under Warranty**, **Scheduled Date**, **Responsible**, **Tags**. **Parts** tab lines carry **Type = Add / Remove / Recycle** with Demand/Done quantities and a **Used** checkbox. States: **New → Confirmed** (via **Confirm Repair**, reserves components, adds a Forecasted availability column) **→ Under Repair** (via **Start Repair**) **→ Repaired** (via **End Repair**, with an "Uncomplete Move(s)" popup for unused parts); **Cancel Repair** aborts ([Process repair orders](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/repairs/repair_orders.html)).

**Billing.** For billable repairs, **Create Quotation** opens a sales order "pre-populated with the parts used in the RO, with the total cost of the repair calculated" — quote-after-the-fact, not before; there is no repair estimate stage in the document. Helpdesk tickets have a **Repair** button that pre-fills customer/product/lot on a new repair order ([After-Sales](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/services/helpdesk/advanced/after_sales.html)).

**Shipping back and costing.** The repaired product ships back by reversing the return receipt ("Return of WH/OUT/…" → **Return**) — an ordinary outbound transfer ([Process repair orders](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/inventory_and_mrp/repairs/repair_orders.html)). The returned unit sits in normal inventory during repair (no customer-owned segregation, no zero-value receipt documented); parts consumption is stock moves. No vendor-repair loop, no at-vendor tracking, no warranty GL treatment — absences.

## Epicor Kinetic

*(Primary Kinetic help is gated. Claims below cite the Epicor-authored 10.0.700 Application User Guide PDFs republished by licensee ssh.com.mx, an Epicor-authored brochure on a partner mirror, Epicor's Knowledge On Demand pages, and flagged community threads. Terminology in current Kinetic may differ from the 2014-vintage guide.)*

**Warranty definition & activation.** Epicor's Service Management brochure: "Automatically activate warranties, associated with a part or product group upon shipment of a part. When a repair is needed, quickly determine if the problem is still covered under warranty or if it requires a billable service call" ([Epicor Service Management brochure, ziftsolutions mirror](http://dynamic.ziftsolutions.com/epicor.ziftsolutions.com/file/8aef4d8453195d4001531c27f02914cc.pdf) — Epicor-authored, licensee-mirrored). Knowledge On Demand confirms shipment-time control: "Learn to add or change a warranty code at the moment of shipment in Customer Shipment Entry. You can also maintain or change the warranty activation effective dates" ([Accessing Warranty Information in Customer Shipment Entry](https://erpvideo.epicor.com/detail/video/5647799630001/access-warranty-information-in-customer-shipment-entry)). Community corroboration (flagged): warranties are "assigned at the top level part when the pack is created", product groups can carry default warranty codes, and serializing the top level lets you "apply any warranty at any level … even … warranties of different lengths for different parts"; adding a warranty after shipment requires workarounds ([epiusers: Implementing Warranties](https://www.epiusers.help/t/implementing-warranties-basic-questions/63819)). The Warranty Maintenance program's exact fields (material/labor/misc durations) are behind the gated help — **unverified**.

**Service call → service job (the repair execution model).** The Field Service module's **Service Call Center** "pulls information from existing service contracts and warranties"; "as a service job's operations are worked on by technicians, labor is recorded against these operations … Depending on the setup of the service call, those charges may be absorbed into the service contract or automatically invoiced to the customer." On a **service call line**: pick the part + reason + quantity, then *either* a **Contract** *or* a warranty reference — "If a part is still under warranty, click the **Packing Slip/Line** button to access a modified version of the Customer Shipment Search window; browse for the correct packing slip and line on which the part originally shipped … You cannot reference both a contract and a warranty on the same service call line." A **Coverage** section shows "if the contract or warranty covers material, labor, or miscellaneous charges". **Actions → Create Service Call Job** "launches Job Entry … with all pertinent information populated" (job type **Service**); Line Pricing shows **Estimate / Actual / Billable** for Service (labor), Material, Subcontract, Misc; calls auto-close when labor and material issue are complete, and invoiced calls can't reopen ([Epicor ERP Application User Guide Part 1, ch. 9 Field Service — ssh.com.mx mirror](https://www.ssh.com.mx/ftp_fiel/EPICOR%20Manuales/EpicorApplication_UserGuide_Part1of2_100700.pdf), flagged licensee mirror). The warranty reference *is the original shipment line* — validity derives from what actually shipped, not from a free-standing registration record.

**Service contract codes** cover **Material / Labor / Misc** (at least one required), with Duration + Modifier (Days/Months/Years), on-site flag, recurring invoicing, renewal codes (same guide).

**RMA → repair job.** "Once RMA materials are received into an inspection location, you must disposition them. The inspector reviews the RMA receipts and determines whether to disposition the material quantity back to inventory, **to a job (for repair)**, or reject it"; with the QA module, failed items flow to DMR ([Application User Guide Part 2, ch. 5 Customer Returns](https://www.ssh.com.mx/ftp_fiel/EPICOR%20Manuales/EpicorApplication_UserGuide_Part2of2_100700.pdf), flagged licensee mirror). The guide is explicit that RMA processing itself is only a communication vehicle: "The application does not automatically create credit or shipping transactions or rework jobs based on these returns … you enter or change jobs, create credit transactions … or reship part quantities as you need" (same chapter).

**Three-party chain: job subcontract shipping is the send-to-OEM leg.** "Use **Subcontractor Shipment Entry** to ship WIP parts directly from a job to a subcontract supplier"; the parts return through **Receipt Entry** ("you use Receipt Entry to enter the receipt of all inventory materials, non-inventory materials, and subcontract [items]"); **Miscellaneous Shipment Entry** handles "the return of discrepant parts to a supplier from a … DMR record" ([Part 2, ch. 4 Shipping and Receiving](https://www.ssh.com.mx/ftp_fiel/EPICOR%20Manuales/EpicorApplication_UserGuide_Part2of2_100700.pdf)). So a repair job holding a returned unit can carry a subcontract operation that ships the unit to the OEM and receives *the same unit* back into the job — the only documented same-unit-at-vendor round trip in this survey, though chaining RMA → job → subcontract op is composition, not a packaged flow (**synthesis, not a documented single feature**).

## Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine)

**The richest warranty data model surveyed.** "Warranties are tracked at both the unit and the component level. For example, a car has one warranty, but the tires on the car have a separate warranty. The system tracks whether a warranty is given by the manufacturer or through the vendor for purchased parts existing on a unit. You can set warranty durations by both expiration date and a meter amount such as miles, impressions, cycles, etc." ([Infor SyteLine Service User Guide 9.01.x PDF](https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/useradminlist/csbi_9.01.x_service_user_op_sl_en-us_50340.pdf), Units chapter). **Warranty Codes** form: code, description, **Warranty Type = User / Vendor / Manufacturer** ("establishes ownership of the warranty"), **Date Duration** (number + Day(s)/Month(s)/Year(s)/**Lifetime**), **Meter Amount** ([Maintaining Warranty Codes for Items](https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/csbiolh/lsm1454144763860.html)).

**Units (install base) + automatic creation.** The **Units** form holds unit ID, item, customer, ship-to, meter readings, and a **Warranty** indicator: "whether any active warranty records exist for this unit. Warranty records are added to a unit or sub-component on the Unit Configurations form. An active warranty record has a starting date prior to the current date, and either no end date or one in the future." **Unit Status Codes** are user-defined — the guide's own examples are "In Shop, Under Repair, Rented" — with an informational **Down/Unavailable** flag; unit configurations are tracked **As Built / As Is / As Was**, and registration history records each owner (Service User Guide, Units chapter). Units auto-create on shipment (**Create Unit On Shipment** on Service Parameters; a Unit Configuration Create Utility builds the configuration from the producing job's BOM) and optionally on **receipt from a vendor**; each warranty code carries a **Warranty Start Code Basis** so warranties activate on **ship date, receive date, or install date** — "if a unit is not automatically received or shipped, but the receive date or ship date is assigned on the unit, any item with a Receive Date or Ship Date basis warranty code is activated" ([Automatic Unit/Warranty Creation](https://docs.infor.com/ism/2022.x/en-us/ismolh/service_user_op_servicemanagement/lsm1454144765561.html) — page redirects when fetched directly; wording reconstructed from search excerpts of the same page, flagged).

**Repair document = SRO (Service Request Order), executed as header → lines → operations → transactions.** "When service work requires materials, labor, or other miscellaneous expenses, a Service Request Order (SRO) must be created"; transactions are typed **material / labor / miscellaneous** ([Using SROs](https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/csbiolh/lsm1454144753189.html)). Billing lives on the SRO: **Billing Type** = Calculated/Time & Material vs Project/Fixed, and the pivotal **Bill Code**, defaulted from the SRO Type: **Contract** (rate 0), **No Charge** (rate 0), **Warranty** ("The SRO is associated with a unit that is under warranty and the transaction should not be charged to the customer; the default rate is 0"), **Recover** (rate = cost), **List** (customer/item pricing for material, service pricing routine for labor); an **Accumulate WIP** flag stores issued costs in WIP accounts (Service User Guide, SRO chapter).

**Warranty GL treatment — the explicit answer to "where do absorbed costs go".** Product-code and misc-code setup includes a **Warranty tab**: "select the general ledger accounts to use for posting expenses to a SRO for a unit that is under warranty"; the posting matrix routes **Bill Code = WARRANTY → Revenue & COGS Accounts from Warranty** (vs Distribution/Contract accounts for other codes) (Service User Guide, accounting sections). This is warranty *expense routing* — no accrued-warranty-reserve mechanics are documented in any system surveyed.

**Warranty claims & partner reimbursement (three-party, servicer direction).** "Processing a Warranty Claim … performed by a partner employed by the manufacturer": create an SRO with **SRO Type = Warranty** (its Product Code + Billing Code apply), record the transactions, invoice through Order Invoicing ([Processing a Warranty Claim](https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/csbiolh/lsm1454144761411.html)). Partners carry **Reimburse Material / Reimburse Labor** flags and reimbursement accounts, so a dealer/service-partner doing warranty work for the manufacturer gets paid back per transaction (Service User Guide, partner sections) — the OEM side of the warranty chain, modeled as reimbursement rather than a purchase document.

**Rework order from an RMA (in-house repair execution).** "Open the Job Orders (or Estimate Job Orders) form and create a new order … In the Item field, select the returned item that you want to repair … Select the Rework field. Selecting this field will allow this job's bill of material to be recursive … Enter the item to be repaired as a component, along with any other materials necessary for the repair … On the Job Material Transactions form, issue the item to be repaired (from its non-nettable location where you originally received the RMA order) to the rework order … Enter job operation transactions to record setup, labor or machine time, and overhead, and to move the completed, reworked item to inventory" ([Creating a Rework Order](https://docs.infor.com/csi/9.01.x/en-us/csbiolh/lsm1454144440801.html)). Note the pattern: same item is both job output and job component (recursive BOM), received from a **non-nettable** (planning-invisible) location.

**Advance exchanges & loaners.** A dedicated **AdvExch** SRO type pairs a +1 line (item sent out, Bill Code List) with a −1 line (item coming back) carrying **Exchange Days**; on receipt "the system compares the transaction date with the Exchange Date … If the transaction date is after the Exchange Date, the Bill Code is set to No Charge and the customer is not credited for the return." Loaner SRO types, loaner warehouse/location, **Loaner Shipment / Loaner Return** transaction types, and Outstanding Exchanges/Loaners reports round it out; SRO parts ship via **Service Order Packing Slip Generation** (Service User Guide, SRO chapter).

## Acumatica (light pass — Field Service Edition, Equipment Management)

**Dual warranty durations — company-facing and vendor-facing — on the stock item.** In the Equipment Management training (Acumatica-authored): stock items flagged as equipment carry, on the **Service Management** tab of **Stock Items (IN202500)**, an **Equipment General Warranty** section with **Company Warranty** and **Vendor Warranty** durations — the exercises set e.g. Company Warranty 12 Months / Vendor Warranty 6 Months for a component, 2 Years / 18 Months for model equipment; item classes mark items as **Model Equipment** or **Component** ([Acumatica Open University: Equipment and Route Management PDF](https://openuni.acumatica.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/EU_FieldServices_Interm-1.pdf)). Selling model equipment auto-creates an equipment record whose **Components and Warranties** tab "ha[s] been filled in with the settings specified for the sold stock item", with **Installation Date** "filled with the date of the SO invoice", plus Sales Date and per-component serials (same PDF). This is the cleanest documented precedent for *storing the vendor's warranty alongside the customer's on the same registration*. Community threads (flagged) note the service-order side is thinner: "Acumatica doesn't provide warranty functionality beyond the 'Warranty' checkbox on service order detail", and vendor-warranty claim tracking gets customized ([community](https://community.acumatica.com/field-service-112/how-are-companies-supposed-to-track-warranty-costs-in-field-service-33754)).

## Katana / Fulcrum (light pass)

**Katana:** no warranty object. Serial tracking is marketed as supporting "warranty claims and recall cases" ([Katana traceability](https://katanamrp.com/solutions/track-inventory/)), and repairs are handled by selling a **Service** item ("repairs or maintenance — charges for product upkeep"; [How to create a service card](https://support.katanamrp.com/en/articles/10696839-how-to-create-a-service-service-card)) — no repair document, no coverage logic. **Fulcrum:** no public documentation of any warranty or repair-order feature — absence.

## Synthesis

### The common core

1. **Warranty = a reusable term definition + a per-unit registration stamped at a shipping/invoicing event.** Every system with warranty support separates the two: BC Service Mgt. Setup defaults → service item warranty dates; SAP B1 warranty template → equipment card + Warranty contract; NetSuite warranty term → warranty registration; SyteLine warranty codes → unit/component warranty records; Acumatica stock-item warranty settings → equipment Components and Warranties. Registration is automatic on the outbound event (BC posting a shipment; SAP B1 delivery/AR invoice; NetSuite invoice; SyteLine shipment). Epicor is the partial exception: warranty codes activate on the shipment pack, but there is no free-standing registration record — validity at service time derives from the original shipment line itself (see Divergences).
2. **The registration object is equipment/install-base master data, not inventory.** BC service items, SAP B1 equipment cards, SyteLine units, Acumatica equipment records all live outside stock, keyed by serial number, holding customer, dates, and service history. Serial number is the join key back to inventory identity.
3. **The start trigger is configurable, ship-date-flavored by default.** BC: posting date of the shipping order; NetSuite: SO date (default) or invoice date preference; SyteLine: per-warranty-code basis — ship date / receive date / install date; Acumatica: installation date from the SO invoice; Epicor: effective dates editable at shipment. Install-date basis exists only in the install-base-centric systems (SyteLine, Acumatica).
4. **Coverage splits by cost class — parts vs labor (vs travel/misc) — not by document.** BC has separate parts/labor date ranges *and* separate discount percentages per unit; SAP B1 contracts cover Parts/Labor/Travel; Epicor contracts/warranties cover Material/Labor/Misc; SyteLine prices material and labor transactions independently under one bill code. Partial coverage (parts covered, labor not) is native in those four; the NetSuite and Acumatica pages reached document warranty durations without a per-class split (**unverified** there), and Odoo has none.
5. **Warranty validity check at service initiation is automatic but advisory** — BC auto-fills the Warranty flag from dates, SAP B1 "automatically checks for warranty and contract coverage", Epicor shows a Coverage section — with one hard-block outlier: NetSuite's SuiteApp refuses claims on expired registrations.
6. **The free/billable decision is made per line, not per document.** BC: warranty discount % per Item/Resource line with an Exclude Warranty override (and fault-reason codes that force exclusion); SyteLine: bill codes (Warranty/Contract/No Charge → 0, Recover → cost, List → price) defaulted from the SRO type, overridable per transaction; Odoo: Under Warranty zeroes part prices; SAP B1: "any service costs not covered … are billed".
7. **The repair document is a service order: header + unit lines + typed charge lines** (parts = item lines, labor = resource/time, fees = cost/misc), with a user-definable status ladder (BC repair statuses → Pending/In Process/Finished/On Hold; Odoo New→Confirmed→Under Repair→Repaired; SyteLine SRO operation statuses) and quote-first support (BC service quotes; Odoo's Create Quotation is quote-after instead).
8. **Manufacturing-grade repairs execute on the production machinery, not on the service document.** Epicor: RMA disposition **to a job (for repair)** and service calls spawn **Service-type jobs**; SyteLine: **rework orders** (jobs with recursive BOMs — the repaired item is both component and output, issued from the non-nettable RMA location); NetSuite: a **work order** builds a "repaired item" whose component is the returned assembly. Odoo's standalone repair order is the lightweight exception.
9. **Customer-owned units either stay out of inventory entirely (service-item model) or enter at zero/segregated value (depot model).** BC service orders never receive the unit; the depot path uses return reasons with **Inventory Value Zero** ("enter into inventory to be repaired, but its value will be set to zero") or a customer-owned location; SyteLine receives RMA units into **non-nettable** locations; NetSuite receives via the RA item receipt. Parts consumed post as job/service COGS or WIP (SyteLine's Accumulate WIP).
10. **Where absorbed-cost routing is documented at all, it is dedicated warranty GL accounts — but only SyteLine documents it explicitly** (Bill Code WARRANTY → warranty Revenue & COGS accounts); BC reaches the same economic effect implicitly via 100% line discounts, and the other systems' cited pages are silent on warranty GL routing. Nobody documents an automatic warranty accrual/reserve.
11. **The three-party (vendor-warranty) chain is thin everywhere, and modeled three different ways:** as *documents* (BC Create Return-Related Documents: one vendor → purchase return + replacement PO + replacement SO), as *data* (SyteLine warranty Type = User/Vendor/Manufacturer; Acumatica Company vs Vendor warranty durations per component), or as *reimbursement* (SyteLine partner warranty claims: the manufacturer reimburses the servicing partner's material/labor per transaction). No system documents a serialized unit's at-vendor status during repair (SAP B1's equipment-card **In Lab** and SyteLine's free-form unit statuses like "In Shop" are informational nearest-misses), and only Epicor's job-subcontract loop (Subcontractor Shipment Entry → Receipt Entry) documents same-physical-unit-to-vendor-and-back mechanics — as a general job facility, not a packaged repair flow.

### Divergences worth noting

- **Registration granularity:** per-serial only (BC, SAP B1) vs per-serial + per-component with independent warranties (SyteLine, Acumatica) vs quantity-based for lot items (NetSuite).
- **What the warranty reference *is* at service time:** a free-standing registration record (NetSuite, SyteLine, SAP B1) vs the original shipment line itself (Epicor's Packing Slip/Line lookup — validity derived from shipping history, no separate object).
- **Repaired-unit identity:** every system keeps the same unit/serial except NetSuite, which consumes the returned assembly into a *different* "repaired item" — a warning about what happens when repair is bolted onto a BOM-only work-order model without genealogy.
- **Replacement-during-repair warranty reset:** only BC documents it (component replacement starts a fresh default warranty from the service order posting date).
- **Loaners:** first-class in BC (loaner cards) and SyteLine (loaner SRO types + shipment/return transactions + outstanding reports); explicitly unsupported in NetSuite's SuiteApp; absent elsewhere.
- **Advance exchange** (ship replacement before receiving the defective unit): packaged in SyteLine (AdvExch ±1 lines with Exchange Days and auto-No-Charge on late return) and half-packaged in NetSuite (Ship Replacement in Advance preference); manual everywhere else.
- **Meter/usage-based warranty** (miles, cycles) exists only in SyteLine.

### Implications for Carbon

Carbon already has: `salesReturnOrder` (authorize → receive via receipts → disposition via quality Issues → AR credit memo / replacement SO) with the same serialized `trackedEntity` re-entering **On Hold** with genealogy preserved; `purchaseReturnOrder` (ship via shipments → AP credit memo / replacement PO), with the quality-Issue "Return to Supplier" disposition drafting one; quality Issues with dispositions + MRB; jobs with material issuing and labor; `trackedEntity` statuses; AR/AP memos; item type Service — and **no warranty model at all**.

1. **The minimal prerequisite layer is two tables, and it comes before any repair document:** (a) a **warranty terms** table — duration + unit, coverage split (at minimum `coversParts`/`coversLabor` booleans; percentages later), start basis (default Ship Date; Invoice Date as the NetSuite-style option) — attached to items (and optionally overridable per customer, the SAP B1 template pattern); (b) a **warranty registration** per sold unit — for tracked items keyed on `trackedEntityId` (the BC/SyteLine per-serial model; Carbon's trackedEntity *is* the install base row, so no separate equipment-card table is needed initially), for untracked items keyed on the shipment line + quantity (NetSuite's lot-item pattern), stamped automatically when the shipment posts (every system's trigger), storing resolved start/end dates per coverage class. This is exactly BC's service-item auto-creation with Carbon's existing identity machinery substituting for the service item card.
2. **Register the supplier's warranty at the same time.** Acumatica's Company Warranty / Vendor Warranty pair and SyteLine's warranty Type (User/Vendor/Manufacturer) are the precedents: the same registration row (or a sibling row) should carry the *purchased* component's or unit's supplier-warranty end date, sourced from the PO receipt date. That single field is what later automates the "is the OEM repair leg free?" decision — no competitor computes this for you, but two of them store the data.
3. **Warranty check = advisory, not blocking.** On creating a `salesReturnOrder` line or repair for a registered trackedEntity, surface "in warranty until X / expired since X" per coverage class (BC/SAP B1/Epicor behavior). NetSuite's hard block on expired registrations is the outlier and forces workarounds for goodwill repairs. Reports (BC's Service Items Out of Warranty) come free once the dates exist.
4. **The repair document should be a thin authorization/tracking header whose execution is a job — Carbon is already an Epicor/SyteLine shop here.** Epicor's RMA-disposition-to-Job and SyteLine's rework order agree on the mechanics Carbon should copy: the received unit (trackedEntity On Hold, received through the existing returns receipt at zero value — see 5) is **issued as material to a repair job**; parts and labor accumulate on the job exactly as today; the job "output" is the *same* trackedEntity flipped back to Available/shippable — genealogy preserved, which is precisely what NetSuite's repaired-item BOM hack cannot do and Carbon's trackedEntity model does natively. The repair order document itself carries: link to the return (Odoo's `Return` field), the warranty registration + computed coverage verdict, repair states (Odoo's Confirmed → Under Repair → Repaired is the right minimal ladder; BC shows per-unit repair statuses are wanted eventually), the job reference, and the billing decision. The quality Issue's existing **Rework** disposition is the natural spawn point — mirroring how "Return to Supplier" already drafts a purchaseReturnOrder.
5. **Receive customer units at zero value into a non-nettable/quarantine state.** BC (Inventory Value Zero: "enter into inventory to be repaired, but its value will be set to zero") and SyteLine (RMA receipt into a non-nettable location) converge: the unit must exist physically (bins, genealogy, issue-to-job) without touching inventory valuation or MRP supply. Carbon's On Hold trackedEntity already covers planning invisibility; the receipt posting needs a zero-cost path.
6. **The send-to-OEM leg: model it as an outside-processing-style operation on the repair job, not as a purchaseReturnOrder.** BC's Create Return-Related Documents is the *replacement-from-vendor* flow (purchase return ships the unit away + a replacement PO receives new stock — identity lost) and Carbon already effectively has it (Return to Supplier disposition + replacement PO). For *same-unit-back* repair, the only documented precedent is Epicor's job subcontract loop: ship the unit from the job to the supplier (subcontract shipment against the existing shipments machinery), receive the same unit back into the job (existing receipts), with a repair PO (possibly zero/fee-priced) carrying the commercial terms — which is exactly the shape of Carbon's pending outside-processing work; the repairs module should ride that rail rather than invent a second one. Add the one thing no competitor has: a trackedEntity status for the at-vendor interval (e.g. **At Supplier**), since SAP B1's "In Lab" card status and SyteLine's free-text "In Shop" show the demand while remaining merely informational.
7. **Billing: quote first, decide per line, default from coverage.** Support a repair quotation before work (BC service quotes; Odoo only manages quote-after via Create Quotation — a documented weakness). On the billable path, generate a sales order/invoice carrying the job's parts + labor (Odoo's Create Quotation pre-population; Epicor's Estimate/Actual/Billable split is the mature target). Default each charge line to no-charge when the registration covers its class and the date holds (SyteLine bill-code semantics: Warranty → rate 0), with a per-line override both directions (BC's Exclude Warranty; SyteLine's Recover = bill at cost is a useful middle setting). Coverage classes map cleanly: parts = job material, labor = job operations.
8. **GL: one warranty-expense routing decision, no reserves.** Follow SyteLine: when the repair is warranty-flagged, the job's absorbed costs post to designated warranty expense/COGS accounts instead of normal COGS, via the existing posting-group machinery. No surveyed ERP documents automatic warranty accrual — do not build reserves into v1.
9. **Loaners and advance exchange are documented, deferable patterns.** BC loaner cards and SyteLine's loaner/AdvExch SRO types show the shape (loaner ships and returns as non-billable transactions tied to the repair; advance exchange = replacement out first with a deadline that flips the credit to No Charge). NetSuite ships without either. Defer both, but keep the repair header able to reference an outbound "loaner" shipment later.
10. **Warranty on the repair itself:** BC's component-replacement reset (fresh default warranty from the service posting date) is the only documented answer to "what warranty does the repaired unit carry"; a simple optional "repair warranty" term stamped on completion (SyteLine also names a repair-warranty concept in Infor LN's sibling docs) covers the 90% case.
