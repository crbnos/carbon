# Cut Lists in ERP/MES: Cross-Industry Research

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Purpose:** Research input for a PRD on adding a Cut List capability to Carbon (job-shop / make-to-order manufacturing: metal fab, woodworking, general discrete).
**Method:** Primary sources only — official vendor docs/help portals, source code and GitHub issues, first-party forums, and the original operations-research literature. Every claim is linked to the source that owns it.

---

## 1. What a Cut List Is — Across Industries

A **cut list** (also: cutting list, saw list, cut plan, cut sheet, cutting ticket) is the document — increasingly a live data object — that tells a saw/cutting operator *which pieces to cut, at what dimensions, in what quantity, from which stock*. Every cutting industry has one; the vocabulary and the constraints differ.

### 1.1 Industry variants

- **Metal fabrication (bar/tube/plate saw cutting):** parts lists nested onto bar/tube/beam stock. SigmaNEST's 1D product SigmaCTL "calculates optimal cut plans for complex work orders with varying stock lengths and thicknesses," supports miter matching ("miter support flips and rotates parts to match up miter geometry for a better nest"), bundle cutting, and "customizable cutting plan reports" ([SigmaCTL product page](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/sigmactl)).
- **Structural steel:** the cut list is a first-class Production Control object. Tekla PowerFab has a *Review > Cut Lists* screen where users create a cut list, filter it, save it, and "Share Cut List so that SP-PLM can import it" for nesting ([Trimble/Tekla support article](https://support.tekla.com/article/4-saving-a-cut-list-in-tekla-powerfab-and-importing-the-cut-list-in-to-sp-plm)). Structural steel uses the verb **"mult"** for combining linear items (beams, angles) onto stock lengths, vs. **"nest"** for area materials (plate, grating); PowerFab offers "Mult," "Nest," or "Mult & Nest" as explicit options ([Tekla PowerFab 2026 docs — Steel Projects PLM integration](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-powerfab/2026/int_steel_projects_plm)).
- **Woodworking / cabinetry:** the "cutting list" plus a **cutting diagram** (sheet layout). CutList Plus fx "generates optimized sheet layouts" for "plywood, lumber and other sheet materials … including glass, metal, granite, fabric and plastic," snapping parts to **saw kerf** lines on the diagram ([cutlistplus.com](https://cutlistplus.com/)). The open-source reference implementation is **OpenCutList**, a SketchUp extension "automating the generation of cut lists … computing cutting diagrams, printing labels and generating cost and weight reports" ([lairdubois/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension](https://github.com/lairdubois/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension)).
- **Glass:** cutting plans on jumbo sheets with **guillotine-cut** constraints (see §4), plus "residual plate" management. A+W DynOpt does "fully automated optimization, cutting, and sorting … automatic & flexible input of residual plates and stock sheets," and its residual plate management "ensures that, in addition to stock plates, the optimization also considers valuable residual plates" ([A+W DynOpt](https://www.a-w.com/software/dynopt/)).
- **Wire/cable and other cut-to-length (CTL):** SAP treats these as "one-dimensional materials like cables and tubes" under Long Products Planning, where length is a batch characteristic and cutting is a planning decision ([SAP Long Products Planning process flow](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/c1/8977ea79bf144f908c654543f07561/content.htm?no_cache=true)).
- **Steel service centers (cut-to-size as a business):** the entire order line *is* a cut instruction (customer buys "40 pieces of 1" bar at 5.7 inches"). Systems like Invera INVEX/STRATIX and Enmark Eniteo are built around processing orders, remnants, and heat traceability (§2.11).

### 1.2 Terminology glossary (with owning sources)

| Term | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Kerf** | Material destroyed by the blade width; optimizers must add it to every cut. TigerStop exposes kerf compensation settings; MaxCut "automatically respect[s] grain direction, kerf widths, and material-specific trims" | [TigerStop functionality blog](https://www.tigerstop.com/blog/what-to-do-with-a-tigerstop/), [MaxCut cutting optimization](https://maxcutsoftware.com/features/cutting-optimization/) |
| **Drop / offcut / remnant** | Reusable leftover after cutting. Global Shop calls the pool of drops the "**bone yard**"; Eniteo also tracks "**dog legs**" (L-shaped plate remnants) | [Global Shop nesting interface](https://www.globalshopsolutions.com/nesting-interface-software-for-manufacturing), [Eniteo overview (SoftwareConnect review of vendor material)](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/enmark-eniteo-erp/) |
| **Skeleton** | The web left after parts are cut from a plate; ProNest can "save remnants or skeletons and use them later" | [ProNest optional modules](https://www.hypertherm.com/products/software/pronest-optional-modules/) |
| **Stock length / mill length / random length** | Purchased bar lengths. "Most mills supply bars in 12 ft, 20 ft, and random lengths"; "12 ft random would be 10 to 12 or 11 to 13 ft" — random lengths let the vendor "utilize their end cuts" | [Associated Steel — What is a random length bar?](https://www.associatedsteel.com/what-is-the-length-of-a-random-steel-bar/), [Associated Steel — Cut pieces vs random bar](https://www.associatedsteel.com/cut-to-length-steel-and-random-bar-lengths/) |
| **Nest / nesting** | Arranging parts on stock to maximize yield (1D = linear nesting/multing; 2D = sheet nesting) | [Tekla PowerFab docs](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-powerfab/2026/int_steel_projects_plm) |
| **Mult** | Structural-steel term for 1D nesting of linear members | [Tekla PowerFab docs](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-powerfab/2026/int_steel_projects_plm) |
| **Yield** | Used material ÷ consumed stock. Epicor computes co-part quantities as "Co-Part Quantity = Primary Part Quantity × Yield Per" | [Teccweb — Co-Parts in Epicor Kinetic](https://teccweb.com/co-parts-epicor-kinetic/) |
| **Trim loss** | SAP's term for waste in trim optimization: "the difference between the sum of the lengths of all reels and the sum of the length of the jumbo reels" | [SAP — Trim sheet and trim sheet order](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/79/64a93ef8508913e10000000a114084/content.htm?no_cache=true) |
| **Heat number** | Mill-assigned melt identifier; "must be maintained throughout the entire manufacturing chain" and must match the certificate for EN 10204 3.1 | [Projectmaterials — MTC EN 10204](https://blog.projectmaterials.com/epc-projects/testing-inspection/mill-test-certificates-3-1-2/) |
| **Tag** | Service-center term for the physical inventory unit (a specific plate/bar/coil with its heat); INVEX controls plate "by Tag, Heat or just a location" | [Invera INVEX](https://invera.com/invex/) |
| **Guillotine cut** | Edge-to-edge straight cuts only (glass, beam saws) | [Wikipedia — Cutting stock problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_stock_problem) |
| **Grain direction** | Woodworking constraint: parts may not rotate on grained material; MaxCut "group[s] panels so the optimizer places them on the same board with continuous grain flow" | [MaxCut](https://maxcutsoftware.com/features/cutting-optimization/), [CutList Plus fx](https://cutlistplus.com/) |

## 2. How Major ERPs Handle Cut Lists

### 2.1 SAP S/4HANA / SAP Mill Products — the deepest native model

SAP is the reference implementation of native cut-list logic in a general ERP, via the Mill Products industry solution (DIMP) and APO/SCM:

- **Trim optimization → trim sheet → trim sheet order.** An optimization run produces a *trim sheet* containing operations ("trim sequences — cutting patterns between knife changes"), trim patterns, output reels, and trim loss. In planning, it becomes a *trim sheet order*, a standard planning order with status "Input fixed and Output fixed" that must be released, executed, and confirmed ([SAP — Trim Sheet and Trim Sheet Order](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/79/64a93ef8508913e10000000a114084/content.htm?no_cache=true)). SAP deliberately externalizes the math: the "Interface for Industry-Specific Optimizers" hands the problem to third-party trim optimizers and takes back the pattern solution ([SAP — Interface for Industry-Specific Optimizers](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_snc70/helpdata/EN/0c/08d4e8c8a542459e4f15181c3d8b9b/content.htm?no_cache=true)).
- **Long Products Planning (1D).** For "one-dimensional materials like cables and tubes," SAP reads "both the required and available product quantities and the specified and available lengths in ATP and planning," then covers demand in a fixed hierarchy: (1) exact-length batches, (2) "available batches with excess lengths but otherwise matching characteristics" via cutting operations, (3) expected receipts, (4) new procurement. Length is a batch/variant characteristic (`SAP_MILLCA_LENGTH`), assignments are protected by "automatically fixed pegging," and open requirements are rounded to full lengths ([SAP — Long Products Planning process flow](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/c1/8977ea79bf144f908c654543f07561/content.htm?no_cache=true)).
- **Order combination.** Transaction `MILL_OC` combines multiple production orders for similar materials into one combined order for a single machine run; "when you settle the combined order the system distribute[s] the quantities to the individual orders" ([tcodesearch — MILL_OC](https://www.tcodesearch.com/sap-tcodes/detail?id=MILL_OC), [SAP Help — Changing Combined Orders](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_ERP/39d6d7a325d04826b95328b25fad91ab/01cfc353b677b44ce10000000a174cb4.html)). A companion "Interface Harmonization" business function unifies the cutting (`MILL_CUT`) and order-combination transactions ([SAP Help — Mill Products, Interface Harmonization](https://help.sap.com/docs/PRODUCT_ID/15f9651d9b6043b0b829feec66ff5963/616357dc3c5c497faed54fbc472498e4.html)).
- **Length-based ATP** checks "not only for total length, but also consider individual length as defined in the sales order" — the canonical cable/mill requirement that 100 m of stock ≠ one 100 m piece ([SAP Community — Mill Products discussions](https://community.sap.com/t5/sap-for-mill-products-discussions/sap-mill-product/td-p/10780744)).

**Takeaways from SAP's model:** cut plans are orders (schedulable, confirmable, settleable); the optimizer is pluggable; length/dimension is an inventory characteristic, not just a quantity; combining demand across orders is a first-class transaction with cost settlement back to the originals.

### 2.2 Odoo — no native capability; visible unmet demand

- Odoo's native `mrp` has no cut list or cutting optimization concept. A furniture manufacturer asked for "a module or third party app" for cutting waste optimization, referencing cutlistoptimizer.com; the thread's only reply is another user asking "Did you find anything for this?" — unresolved ([Odoo forum — MRP - CUTLIST OPTIMIZER](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/mrp-cutlist-optimizer-193424)).
- A second thread (Odoo 17): "A cut list is pretty standard in all production companies, so I can not imagine this is not somehow covered in Odoo." The accepted workaround was modeling each cut piece as a product with dimensions in the BOM; the user ultimately *extended the shopfloor module himself* to display width/length/thickness/quantity from BOM lines ([Odoo forum — Creating cut lists for CNC / sawing](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/creating-cut-lists-for-cnc-sawing-245070)).
- **OCA:** no cutting/cut-list module exists in the OCA ecosystem — the [OCA/manufacture](https://github.com/OCA/manufacture) repository and OCA organization listings contain nothing for cutting stock or nesting (verified by search across [github.com/OCA](https://github.com/OCA)).
- The gap is served by paid custom work, e.g. Aktiv Software's bespoke "Cut List Optimizer" module that "takes your panel list and available stock sheets and calculates the cutting layout … allowing for saw blade kerf, and enforcing grain direction" ([Aktiv Software case study](https://www.aktivsoftware.com/cut-list-optimizer-in-odoo/)).

### 2.3 ERPNext — no native capability; users improvise with variants/batches

- No cutting/nesting feature exists in [frappe/erpnext](https://github.com/frappe/erpnext); a `gh` search of the issue tracker for *cutting*, *offcut*, *remnant*, *nesting* returns no feature work (closest is scrap-item handling at work-order level, [issue #35917](https://github.com/frappe/erpnext/issues/35917)).
- The demand shows up on the first-party forum instead. An aluminum fabricator (buys 4×10/4×12 sheets, cuts to order, keeps offcuts) tried modeling every remnant as an item variant, hit performance walls ("took about 30 seconds to load the popup"), and was steered to batch-based tracking: "you can make item = Red Aluminum and add the dimensions as batch on it" with custom dimension fields ([Frappe forum — Handling Large Number of Variants](https://discuss.frappe.io/t/handling-large-number-of-variants/53875)).
- ERPNext markets to metal fabricators via item variants for length/finish permutations, not via any cutting logic ([frappe.io — metal fabrication ERP page](https://frappe.io/erpnext/manufacturing/open-source-metal-fabrication-erp)).

### 2.4 Epicor Kinetic — integration product + community workarounds

- **NESTLink** (official add-on) is "automation middleware" between Kinetic and nesting software: Kinetic sends work orders and part/sheet inventory; nesting returns "material consumption data, scrap rates and quantities, labor costs, updated work order information"; it "automates the creation of job records, work orders, and material transactions" over a "secure API-based integration" ([Epicor NESTLink](https://www.epicor.com/en-us/products/enterprise-resource-planning-erp/kinetic/nestlink/)).
- **Co-parts + Yield Per** is the native way to model multiple parts from one stock: "Co-Part Quantity = Primary Part Quantity × Yield Per" ([Teccweb — Co-Parts in Epicor Kinetic](https://teccweb.com/co-parts-epicor-kinetic/)).
- **The pain is real and documented on the first-party user forum.** Nesting combines parts from many jobs onto shared sheets, but Epicor makes one job per part: "a single operator, who's time should be spent operating the laser, would now have to sign in and out of every single one of the 200 jobs." Job Batching exists but "costs of the batched job" don't split back accurately, "leaving orphaned costs"; nests are created *after* jobs release; batching requires identical operation codes ([epiusers.help — Job Batching / Nesting Sheet Parts for CNC](https://www.epiusers.help/t/job-batching-nesting-sheet-parts-for-cnc/124778)).
- **Remnant practice:** users keep partial sheets "as the total remaining square inches" and document usable dimensions in lot memos: "open Lot Entry … add a memo to the lot documenting … the remaining square inches"; the acknowledged flaw is that "an operation requires the sheet of MDF to be a certain dimension, which isn't communicated by a SQFT quantity" ([epiusers.help — sheet metal/plate raw material inventory](https://www.epiusers.help/t/how-do-you-deal-with-sheet-metal-plate-raw-material-inventory/95171)).

### 2.5 Infor CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine)

No public first-party documentation of native cut-list/remnant functionality was found; Infor's materials describe generic APS, inventory, and Factory Track shop-floor tracking ([Infor SyteLine product page](https://www.infor.com/solutions/erp/syteline)). Cutting is handled the same way as Epicor: via third-party nesting integrations (ProNest lists Infor-class ERPs among its integration targets, §3.1). Treat SyteLine as *no differentiated capability*.

### 2.6 Global Shop Solutions — "Nesting Interface" as a named ERP feature

Global Shop productizes the ERP side of the interface: the ERP "exports job requirements to the machine nesting software, which then feeds back what is needed per job and the drop parts that need to be created for remnant material"; outbound data includes "workorders, inventory information, workcenter details"; inbound includes "cutlist details, material drops, scrap, and estimates for routing and workorders." Supported nesting systems: "SigmaNEST®, Ncell, TRUMPF, ProNest, Bystronic and more." It "makes it easy to track and use 'bone yard' remnants" and reduces setup "by running multiple jobs at once" ([Global Shop — Nesting Interface Software](https://www.globalshopsolutions.com/nesting-interface-software-for-manufacturing), [blog: 5 Reasons Your ERP Should Provide a Nesting Interface](https://www.globalshopsolutions.com/blog/the-5-reasons-your-erp-software-should-provide-a-nesting-interface)).

### 2.7 JobBOSS² (ECI)

Official nesting integration synchronizes "plate/sheet stock between systems"; laser/plasma/waterjet jobs become "available for nesting application based on user-definable parameters such as scheduled date, due date, work center, material thickness" ([JobBOSS customer portal — Nesting Integration](https://customers.jobboss.com/nesting-integration)). QBuild's CADLink for JobBOSS² can "create and modify nesting work orders and automatically sync the data with your ERP" ([QBuild CADLink for JobBOSS²](https://www.qbuildsoftware.com/cadlink/cadlink-for-eci-jobboss2/)).

### 2.8 ProShop ERP — traceability-first, no optimizer

ProShop's differentiation is the traceability chain, not cutting math: at receiving "the team generates a barcode label and links it to the material record in the ERP, capturing the heat number, lot number, material specification and supplier. When stock is issued to a work order, the operator scans the label, and the ERP records the material consumption against the job" ([ProShop blog — Achieving Full Traceability](https://proshoperp.com/blog/achieving-full-traceability-shop-floor/)); AS9100/ITAR/NADCAP support is the selling point ([ProShop aerospace page](https://proshoperp.com/industries/aerospace/)).

### 2.9 Fulcrum — the modern job-shop reference implementation

Fulcrum (cloud MES/ERP for job shops) has the most complete *native* workflow among Carbon's direct competitor set:

- **Quoting:** attach a DXF to a part; "Fulcrum … will generate a soft nest when you click Update Nests," combining material weight/cost with the nested layout to compute per-part material cost for the quote ([Fulcrum — sheet metal fabrication workflows](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/fulcrum-for-fabricators-workflows-for-custom-sheet-metal-shops)).
- **Nest planning:** requires three things on a job — "raw material on the BOM, a cutting operation, and a DXF file." Fulcrum then "pulls DXF files from all the jobs" and *suggests groups to nest* "based on material, machine type, and the current production schedule" ([Fulcrum — nesting workflows showcase](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)).
- **Round-trip without an API:** the user nests in external software and drops the **nest PDF** back in; Fulcrum "will read the file to import material usage per part, machine time per part, number of individual setups," and auto-creates a work order grouping the nested parts ([same source](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)).
- **Execution & remnants:** operators see nest sheets on the shop-floor UI and "can enter remnant sizes, with Fulcrum auto-populating based on the nest PDF but allowing manual adjustments"; "Fulcrum captures actual drops automatically from the shop floor" ([nesting showcase](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum), [sheet metal fabrication software page](https://fulcrumpro.com/sheet-metal-fabrication-software)).
- **Costing:** "Fulcrum automatically allocates setup labor, machine time, and material costs to individual parts" of the nest ([nesting showcase](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)).

### 2.10 StartProto / Cetec ERP

Thin public capability. Cetec markets machine-shop material handling that "pull[s] in material only when needed, whether it's barstock, a single cut length, or customer-supplied inventory" plus lot/serial traceability ([Cetec — machine & job shops](https://cetecerp.com/industries/machine-job-shops/)); StartProto markets general job-shop MRP with no published cut-list feature ([startproto.com](https://www.startproto.com/customer-types)). Neither documents an optimizer or remnant model.

### 2.11 Steel service-center systems — the remnant/heat gold standard

- **Invera STRATIX/INVEX:** "Accurate plate remnant diagrams for irregular-shaped plates that remain after processing can be recorded and viewed online in the stock inquiry"; plate inventory "can be inventory controlled by Tag, Heat or just a location"; received plates' "chemical and mechanical results … are compared to the industry specifications (ASTM, ASME, MIL)"; irregular sketches/DXF files attach to order items; "price books help calculate the cutting charge" and capacity planning verifies promised dates ([Invera INVEX](https://invera.com/invex/), [INVEX for plate service centers](https://invera.com/plate/)). STRATIX had real-time bidirectional integration with SigmaNEST as far back as 2012, with "SigmaNEST diagrams viewable directly from STRATIX, including planned remnants before a job starts as well as the final remnant" ([Invera/PRWeb — Brunswick Steel STRATIX+SigmaNEST](https://www.prweb.com/releases/2012/12/prweb10227561.htm)).
- **Enmark Eniteo (Steel-Plus successor):** "remnant tracking, heat traceability, and support for multiple branches" are core; it tracks "inventory remnants and dog legs," covers "Sales, Purchasing, Receiving, Shipping, Shop Floor, Production Order Scheduling, and Accounting," and supports toll processing "from simple fabrication orders to complex process orders" ([Enmark Eniteo product pages](https://eniteo.enmark.com/products/eniteo/), [solutions](https://eniteo.enmark.com/solutions/), [SoftwareConnect review of Eniteo](https://softwareconnect.com/reviews/enmark-eniteo-erp/)).
- **STRUMIS (structural steel ERP):** "wizard-driven linear and plate nesting, with support for remnants"; "offcuts and remnants can be placed back in inventory with the actual true shape available … reducing material wastage to as little as 3-5%"; cutting-plan module "records details about returned remnants"; barcode scanning identifies and consumes remnants; it "generates cutting sheets and inventory feed sheets for workshop machine operators, ensuring 100% traceability" ([STRUMIS nesting/MRP module](https://strumis.com/steel-fabrication-nesting-software-strumis), [STRUMIS inventory](https://www.strumis.com/strumis_inventory.php), [STRUMIS Mobility](https://strumis.com/steel-fabrication-management-software-strumis-mobility)).
- **Tekla PowerFab + Steel Projects PLM:** XML-based export of parts to mult/nest, optionally "include existing inventory materials" and "materials that are on order"; results import back automatically; "if the required material is not available in the Tekla PowerFab inventory, Steel Projects PLM will ask you to create a requisition" — nesting shortfall drives purchasing ([Tekla PowerFab 2026 — Steel Projects PLM integration](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-powerfab/2026/int_steel_projects_plm)).

## 3. Dedicated Cutting/Nesting Software and ERP Integration Patterns

### 3.1 ProNest (Hypertherm) — the canonical 2D integration contract

ProNest's ERP/MRP integration is the clearest published statement of what crosses the ERP↔nesting boundary ([Hypertherm — ProNest ERP/MRP integration](https://www.hypertherm.com/products/software/pronest-erp-mrp-integration/)):

- **Data Sync** (required for all integrations): real-time exchange of "job information (parts and plates)"; returns "updated part and plate information." Marketed as 15-minute plug-and-play setup.
- **Work Order Processing:** imports work orders in real time; "exchange of all work order related information (in addition to parts and plates)"; sorting/grouping/combining and change-order tracking inside ProNest.
- **Plate Inventory:** internal plate database; "after job completion, remnants are stored for future use and all plate usage data is reported back to ERP/MRP/MES."
- Named integrations: "Abas, ECI JobBOSS, Epicor, Global Shop Solutions, Oracle, Sage, SAP, Tekla PowerFab, STRUMIS," QBuild; dedicated API connectors for PowerFab/STRUMIS outperform generic Data Sync. Hypertherm also publishes a connector spec: [ERP/MRP/MES Integration — Connecting ProNest to third party software](https://xnet.hypertherm.com/Xnet/library/download/?file=HYP262276).

### 3.2 SigmaNEST ecosystem — nesting vendor growing an MRP

SigmaNEST attacks from the opposite direction: **SigmaMRP** "manages transactions, customer, supplier and inventory information" and can "push and pull integral business information from your ERP/MRP or accounting system" ([SigmaMRP](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/sigmamrp)); **SimTrans** is the configurable data-transaction bus ([SigmaNEST 24 suite announcement](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/news/sigmanest-24-suite-connects-cad-cam-shop-floor-and-business-systems)). Work-order nests close the loop: "As labor and material are applied to the WO Nests, the completed status is sent back to SigmaNEST … which in turn updates the order status and triggers inventory consumption and remnant creation" ([Shop Manager](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/shop-manager)). **SigmaCTL** covers 1D bar/tube/beam with batch processing "grouped … by bar type and thickness," nesting "from existing inventory or generate required order quantities," and "remnant and inventory tracking" ([SigmaCTL](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/sigmactl)).

### 3.3 TigerStop — saw automation defines shop-floor execution UX

TigerStop (automated stop/pusher systems retrofitted to saws) documents the operator-level cut-list workflow: "manually enter or download a cut list"; **TigerLink 6** downloads cut lists over the network to the machines; **Dynamic Optimization** "determin[es] the optimal cutting order from your parts list" for best yield at the saw itself; operators can "cut and print labels on materials from a downloaded list," "scan a barcode to build a cut list," configure kerf compensation, and "specify whether you want the waste from a piece of material cut at the beginning of the material or at the end" ([TigerStop functionality blog](https://www.tigerstop.com/blog/what-to-do-with-a-tigerstop/), [TigerSaw 2000/products pages](https://www.tigerstop.com/products/)). Design signal: *the optimizer can live at the machine*; the ERP's job is to deliver a clean part list and absorb the results (labels, quantities, drops).

### 3.4 Woodworking optimizers

- **CutList Plus fx:** desktop sheet-layout optimizer; kerf-aware diagrams; per-material rotation control for grain; "calculate exactly how much material to buy for your project, and what it will cost"; higher editions add material costing, labor rates, and printed quotations ([cutlistplus.com](https://cutlistplus.com/), [feature definitions](https://cutlistplus.com/Purchase/Definitions)).
- **OptiCut (Boole & Partners):** "panel and bar cutting optimization" (both 1D and 2D) handling "grain direction, perfect grain continuity between several parts, trim cuts, edging, pre-cuts, stocks and recoverable offcuts, panel turnaround, and strip grouping"; prints "configurable labels including QR-Codes and Barcodes" and has "a configurable export function to ERP software" ([boole.eu — OptiCut](https://www.boole.eu/opticoupe.php)).
- **CutRite (HOMAG):** the saw-vendor optimizer; modular; "compatible with ERP/PPC systems and provides a seamless flow of information from quotation through to delivery"; can feed "both cutting systems (saw and nesting) with data concurrently"; speed-vs-waste optimization strategies ([HOMAG CutRite features](https://indiashop.homag.com/pages/cutrite-features)).
- **MaxCut:** "generates the best layouts, automatically respecting grain direction, kerf widths, and material-specific trims"; outputs "cutting diagrams, panel labels, order sheets, and costing reports"; grain matching groups panels for "continuous grain flow" ([MaxCut cutting optimization](https://maxcutsoftware.com/features/cutting-optimization/)).
- **OpenCutList (open source, GPL):** parts list + cutting diagrams + labels + cost/weight reports from SketchUp models; supports fractional-inch lumber conventions regardless of model units; 13 languages ([GitHub — lairdubois/opencutlist](https://github.com/lairdubois/lairdubois-opencutlist-sketchup-extension)).

### 3.5 Glass — A+W

A+W Clarity/Enterprise is "an integrated, database-based overall ERP solution for the flat glass industry … from order entry via dynamic cutting optimization and production management, up to optimized direct packing"; DynOpt adds buffer-based dynamic optimization, "recutting of broken slices, automatic linking and mixing of cutting jobs," and automated residual-plate input ([A+W DynOpt](https://www.a-w.com/software/dynopt/), [A+W Clarity](https://www.a-w.com/aw-clarity/)). Signal: in glass, the cut plan is *dynamic* — re-optimized mid-run as breakage occurs.

### 3.6 The integration contract, distilled

Across ProNest, SigmaNEST, Global Shop, JobBOSS², Epicor NESTLink, and Tekla/Steel Projects, the interface is remarkably consistent:

**ERP → optimizer:** part demands (part ID, qty, material grade/thickness, due date, job/work-order reference, DXF for 2D or length for 1D), available stock (sheets/bars *and remnants*, with dimensions, heat/lot, location), machine/work-center parameters, grouping filters (material, thickness, machine, date window).
**Optimizer → ERP:** nest/cut-plan documents (patterns, sequence), per-part material usage and cut time, setup counts, actual consumption (which sheet/bar/tag), scrap, and **new remnants with dimensions** (true shape for plate), plus labels/programs to the machines.
**Formats in the wild:** XML (Tekla/Steel Projects), CSV, DXF (part geometry in; Invera accepts DXF on order items), PDF (Fulcrum parses the nest PDF), and vendor APIs (NESTLink, PowerFab/STRUMIS connectors).

## 4. The Optimization Math

### 4.1 1D cutting stock (bar/linear nesting)

- Formalized as integer programming over cutting *patterns*; NP-hard; "the number of possible patterns grows exponentially as a function of m, the number of orders" ([Wikipedia — Cutting stock problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_stock_problem)).
- **Gilmore & Gomory (1961/1963)** solved practical instances via *delayed column generation*: solve a restricted LP master over few patterns, then price new patterns by solving a knapsack subproblem with the LP duals; iterate to LP optimality, then round ([A Linear Programming Approach to the Cutting-Stock Problem, Operations Research 9(6):849-859](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.9.6.849); [Part II, OR 11(6):863-888](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/opre.11.6.863)).
- LP relaxation yields fractional pattern counts ("produce this pattern 3.67 times"); practical systems round up or use branch-and-price ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_stock_problem)).
- **Heuristics:** first-fit-decreasing / best-fit-decreasing (sort pieces longest-first, place greedily) are the standard fast fallback; FFD is within 11/9·OPT + 6/9 bins for bin packing, the 1D CSP's sibling ([Wikipedia — First-fit-decreasing bin packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-fit-decreasing_bin_packing)). TigerStop's on-saw "Dynamic Optimization" is exactly this class of greedy/lookahead ordering ([TigerStop](https://www.tigerstop.com/blog/what-to-do-with-a-tigerstop/)).
- **Physical allowances every real 1D solver needs** (all exposed as settings in shipped products): **kerf** per cut (TigerStop, MaxCut, OptiCut above); **end trim** ("trim cuts" in OptiCut; TigerStop's waste-at-start-or-end setting); **clamp/grip margin** — the unusable zone the pusher or vise holds (TigerStop pusher systems make this explicit in machine parameters); minimum usable remnant threshold (Acumatica thread's "minimum viable scrap thresholds," §6).
- **Variants that matter:** the *assortment problem* (which stock lengths to purchase — maps to the buy-12ft-vs-20ft-vs-cut-to-size decision), *skiving* (welding short ends together — rebar), and *minimum pattern count* (fewer setups at equal waste) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_stock_problem)).
- **Open-source solvers exist and are mature enough to embed or port:** [bozokopic/opcut](https://github.com/bozokopic/opcut) (1D/2D panel, 183★), [emadehsan/csp](https://github.com/emadehsan/csp) (Google OR-Tools column generation, 155★), plus numerous OR-Tools/Gurobi reference implementations found via GitHub search.

### 4.2 2D (sheet/panel/plate)

- Rectangular 2D CSP with the **guillotine constraint** (edge-to-edge cuts, required by beam saws and glass cutters) is well studied and tractable with staged-pattern heuristics; **free-form (true-shape) nesting** of irregular DXF profiles is the hard "nesting problem" ([Wikipedia — Cutting stock problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutting_stock_problem), [Guillotine cutting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillotine_cutting)).
- Woodworking adds **grain direction / rotation locks and grain continuity across parts** (OptiCut, MaxCut, CutList Plus, §3.4); metals add **heat/lot homogeneity** — a nest cut from one plate carries that plate's heat to every part, which is exactly why service-center systems track by Tag/Heat (Invera, §2.11) and why traceability-driven shops resist mixing jobs with different cert requirements on one sheet.
- **Realistic build-vs-integrate line, as evidenced by shipped products:** every ERP examined — including SAP, whose optimizer interface is explicitly third-party ([SAP optimizer interface](https://help.sap.com/saphelp_snc70/helpdata/EN/0c/08d4e8c8a542459e4f15181c3d8b9b/content.htm?no_cache=true)) — *integrates* true-shape 2D nesting (SigmaNEST/ProNest/TruTops class) rather than building it. Conversely, 1D linear optimization and rectangular guillotine panel layout are implemented natively by dozens of small vendors (SigmaCTL, OptiCut, MaxCut, CutRite, TigerStop firmware, OpenCutList) and by open-source projects, i.e., in-house is realistic for 1D + rectangular-2D; DXF true-shape nesting is not.

## 5. Business Flow: Where Cut Lists Sit in Order-to-Ship

Synthesized strictly from the sources above; each stage names its evidence.

1. **Estimating/quoting — yield drives cost.** Fulcrum computes per-part material cost from a soft nest at quote time ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/fulcrum-for-fabricators-workflows-for-custom-sheet-metal-shops)); CutList Plus "calculate[s] exactly how much material to buy … and what it will cost" and prints quotations ([cutlistplus.com](https://cutlistplus.com/)); Invera prices the *cutting charge* itself via price books ([Invera plate](https://invera.com/plate/)).
2. **Engineering/BOM — the cut piece is a BOM line with dimensions.** Odoo's answer: model "Plank, 5cm x 2m" as a product on the BOM ([Odoo forum](https://www.odoo.com/forum/help-1/creating-cut-lists-for-cnc-sawing-245070)); Epicor expresses it as co-parts with Yield Per ([Teccweb](https://teccweb.com/co-parts-epicor-kinetic/)); Fulcrum requires "raw material on the BOM, a cutting operation, and a DXF" ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)).
3. **Planning — aggregate demand across jobs into one cutting run.** SAP `MILL_OC` combines orders and settles costs back ([tcodesearch](https://www.tcodesearch.com/sap-tcodes/detail?id=MILL_OC)); PowerFab builds a cut list across a job and shares it for multing ([Tekla](https://support.tekla.com/article/4-saving-a-cut-list-in-tekla-powerfab-and-importing-the-cut-list-in-to-sp-plm)); Fulcrum suggests nest groups "based on material, machine type, and the current production schedule" ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)); JobBOSS² filters nesting candidates by "scheduled date, due date, work center, material thickness" ([JobBOSS](https://customers.jobboss.com/nesting-integration)). The Epicor forum documents what happens when the ERP *can't* do this: 200 job sign-ins for one laser run ([epiusers.help](https://www.epiusers.help/t/job-batching-nesting-sheet-parts-for-cnc/124778)).
4. **Purchasing — stock lengths vs cut-to-size, and optimization-driven requisitions.** Suppliers sell mill lengths (12/20 ft) and random lengths precisely to move their own end cuts; buying "cut pieces" shifts the drop cost to the supplier ([Associated Steel](https://www.associatedsteel.com/cut-to-length-steel-and-random-bar-lengths/)). Steel Projects PLM "will ask you to create a requisition" when nesting finds insufficient inventory ([Tekla](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-powerfab/2026/int_steel_projects_plm)); SAP's assortment/LPP logic rounds open demand to full purchased lengths ([SAP LPP](https://help.sap.com/doc/saphelp_snc70/7.0/en-US/c1/8977ea79bf144f908c654543f07561/content.htm?no_cache=true)).
5. **Shop-floor execution — operator sees the list, marks cuts, labels pieces.** TigerStop downloads lists to the saw, prints part labels, scans barcodes ([TigerStop](https://www.tigerstop.com/blog/what-to-do-with-a-tigerstop/)); STRUMIS "generates cutting sheets and inventory feed sheets for workshop machine operators" ([STRUMIS](https://strumis.com/steel-fabrication-management-software-strumis-mobility)); OptiCut/MaxCut print per-piece labels with barcodes/QR ([boole.eu](https://www.boole.eu/opticoupe.php), [MaxCut](https://maxcutsoftware.com/features/cutting-optimization/)); Fulcrum's floor UI shows nest sheets and takes remnant entry ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)).
6. **Inventory — consume the stock unit, return the remnant as a dimensioned lot.** STRUMIS returns remnants "with the actual true shape available" and consumes them by barcode scan ([STRUMIS](https://www.strumis.com/strumis_inventory.php)); Invera records "plate remnant diagrams … viewed online in the stock inquiry" ([Invera](https://invera.com/invex/)); ProNest "remnants are stored for future use and all plate usage data is reported back" ([Hypertherm](https://www.hypertherm.com/products/software/pronest-erp-mrp-integration/)); SigmaNEST WO-nest completion "triggers inventory consumption and remnant creation" ([SigmaNEST Shop Manager](https://www.sigmanest.com/en/shop-manager)). The failure mode without this model is documented at length: fractional-sheet quantities that MRP can't use (0.5 + 0.25 = 0.75 sheets) ([Acumatica community](https://community.acumatica.com/manufacturing-89/metal-sheets-inventory-tracking-after-cutting-operation-20732)) and square-inch balances that don't say whether a part fits ([epiusers.help](https://www.epiusers.help/t/how-do-you-deal-with-sheet-metal-plate-raw-material-inventory/95171)).
7. **Costing — allocate the shared stick/sheet, capture yield variance.** Epicor's batch jobs strand costs when allocation is naive ([epiusers.help](https://www.epiusers.help/t/job-batching-nesting-sheet-parts-for-cnc/124778)); Fulcrum allocates setup/machine/material to parts automatically ([Fulcrum](https://fulcrumpro.com/article/product-showcase-video-nesting-workflows-in-fulcrum)); NESTLink returns "material consumption, scrap rates, and labor costs" for "cost accuracy" ([Epicor](https://www.epicor.com/en-us/products/enterprise-resource-planning-erp/kinetic/nestlink/)); SAP settles combined orders back to originating orders ([SAP Help](https://help.sap.com/docs/SAP_ERP/39d6d7a325d04826b95328b25fad91ab/01cfc353b677b44ce10000000a174cb4.html)).
8. **Traceability — heat number from mill cert to cut piece to finished part.** EN 10204 3.1 certificates are "traceable to the specific heat number of the material in the delivery," and "the heat number on the certificate must match the heat number marked on the physical material" ([Projectmaterials — MTC guide](https://blog.projectmaterials.com/epc-projects/testing-inspection/mill-test-certificates-3-1-2/)); ProShop captures heat/lot/spec/supplier at receiving and ties consumption to jobs by scan ([ProShop](https://proshoperp.com/blog/achieving-full-traceability-shop-floor/)); Invera validates imported chem/mech results against ASTM/ASME/MIL specs ([Invera](https://invera.com/invex/)); a remnant that loses its heat linkage breaks the chain — service-center systems keep "full heat number genealogy" on the remnant ([top10erp on metals ERPs / Invera positioning](https://www.top10erp.org/blog/why-these-are-the-top-erp-systems-for-metals-and-steel-fabricators)).

## 7. Implications for Carbon — Design-Relevant Takeaways

1. **Model the cut list as a first-class, schedulable document** — like SAP's trim sheet order and PowerFab's Production Control cut list: it has lines (piece, length/size, qty, source job/line), a state machine (draft → released → in progress → complete), and posts inventory/cost on confirmation, not a report generated on the fly.
2. **A cut list line must carry its demand pedigree.** Every piece points back to job/work-order + BOM line (Fulcrum, JobBOSS², SAP order combination all preserve this) so completion, costing, and traceability flow back per originating job — the exact thing Epicor's job batching fails at.
3. **Cross-job aggregation is the core value, and it needs a grouping key.** Every system groups candidate demand by *material + form/thickness + machine + date window* (Fulcrum, JobBOSS², SigmaCTL). Carbon should aggregate cut demand across jobs into one cutting run with automatic candidate suggestion.
4. **Dimensioned inventory, not fractional quantities.** The industry-proven model is piece-level (tag/lot) tracking where each stock unit and each remnant is a discrete record with dimensions (and shape metadata for plate). Fractional-sheet and square-inch balances are documented failure modes (Acumatica, Epicor threads). Carbon already has lot/serial tracking — remnants should become new lots with dimension attributes and parent-lot genealogy.
5. **Remnant return is part of cut-list confirmation, not a separate adjustment.** ProNest/SigmaNEST/Fulcrum/STRUMIS all create remnants automatically at job completion, pre-populated from the plan and operator-adjustable. Include a minimum-remnant threshold below which the piece is scrap.
6. **Heat/lot genealogy must survive the cut.** Consuming bar lot X for pieces A, B, C must link A/B/C (and the new remnant) to X's heat/MTR. This is table stakes for AS9100/ISO shops (EN 10204 3.1 heat matching; ProShop receiving-scan flow; Invera tag/heat control) and is a differentiator Carbon's QMS story can amplify.
7. **1D optimization in-house; 2D true-shape via integration.** Ship a native 1D optimizer (FFD/BFD heuristic first, optionally column generation later — OR-Tools reference implementations exist) with kerf, end-trim, grip margin, min-remnant parameters. For sheet/plate, do rectangular guillotine at most; for DXF nesting, follow the universal pattern: export candidates / import results (even Fulcrum only parses the nest PDF; SAP outsources the math entirely).
8. **The integration contract is known — build to it.** ERP→optimizer: parts (ID, qty, material, due, job ref, geometry/length), stock incl. remnants, machine params. Optimizer→ERP: patterns, per-part usage and time, consumption by tag, scrap, new remnants. CSV/XML import-export covering this contract makes Carbon compatible with ProNest Data Sync-style flows and TigerLink-style saw downloads without bespoke connectors.
9. **Operator UX: one run, one screen.** The saw/laser operator works one cutting run: sees pattern/sequence, marks cuts done (per pattern, not per job), prints piece labels (barcode/QR — TigerStop, OptiCut, MaxCut, STRUMIS all print labels), and records the actual drop. MES is where this lives in Carbon.
10. **Labels are load-bearing.** Every execution-grade system prints per-piece labels for downstream identification and remnant re-identification (scan-to-consume in STRUMIS). Carbon's printing/labels package should treat cut pieces and remnants as label targets from day one.
11. **Yield belongs in quoting.** Material cost per part = f(nest yield), and Fulcrum demonstrates soft-nesting at quote time; at minimum, Carbon quoting should support pieces-per-stock-length math (with kerf/trim) instead of naive per-unit-length costing, and a scrap/yield factor that flows to standard cost.
12. **Purchasing tie-in: cut-list shortfall → requisition; stock-length choice is an optimization output.** Steel Projects raises requisitions from nesting shortfall; the 12ft/20ft/random/cut-to-size decision (assortment problem) should at least be visible: show yield per purchasable stock length when the buyer chooses.
13. **Costing: allocate actual stock consumption across served jobs proportionally to nested material (with policy options), and post scrap/yield variance.** The Epicor thread proves naive batch costing strands costs; Fulcrum's automatic allocation is the benchmark; SAP settles combined orders back to originals.
14. **Length/dimension-aware availability.** SAP's length-based ATP exists because total length ≠ usable length. Carbon's cut-list planner must match required piece length against *individual* stock/remnant records, never against aggregate quantity-on-hand.
15. **Positioning: none of Carbon's open-source competitors (Odoo, ERPNext) has this**, both have documented unmet demand, and mid-market proprietary competitors either charge for connectors (Epicor NESTLink, Global Shop Nesting Interface) or built it natively as their headline fab feature (Fulcrum). A native cut list with 1D optimization + remnant/heat genealogy + open CSV/XML nesting contract is a defensible wedge for metal fab and woodworking job shops.
